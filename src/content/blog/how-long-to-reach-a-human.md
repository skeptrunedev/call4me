**The short answer:** across the 93 calls where we reached a live person and the post says when, the **median time to a human was 2:06**, measured from the start of each published recording. Half of them got there between **1:26 and 3:46**. The fastest was **SimonMed Imaging's cardiology scheduling line at 0:13**, and all four calls to it took under 22 seconds. The slowest were **AT&T at 44:30**, **Priceline at 25:09**, **United at 19:09** and **Delta at 16:15**, and on all four most of that was hold time after the menu had already put us in the queue.

Those 93 calls come from 138 calls we made to 77 companies' customer service lines between September 28 and October 26, 2026, all described on this blog and almost all with the recording published. Some more numbers from the same set:

- **42 of the 138 calls never reached a person.** At 5 of the 77 companies, Coinbase, Experian, FedEx, Lyft and Uber, no call we dialed did. Coinbase and Lyft answered with a recording and hung up, and Uber's lines sent us to the app. FedEx's agent only came on when FedEx called us back.
- **An AI or virtual assistant answered first on 46 of 137 calls**, at 25 of the 77 companies. (On the 138th call, the post doesn't say.) When one did, the median time to a person was **3:07** (27 calls). When a plain phone menu answered, it was **2:00** (65 calls). Three of the four slowest companies, Priceline, United and Delta, are in the assistant group. The slowest, AT&T at 44:30, answered with an automated voice system. On all four most of the wait was hold time in the queue, so don't read too much into a sample this small.
- **40 of the 93 calls reached a person in under 2 minutes.** Fifteen took more than 5 minutes.

Four things got us through, again and again:

1. **Say you don't have the number they're asking for, or don't enter one.** [Fifth Third](/blog/fifth-third-bank-customer-service) transferred us "due to invalid entries" at 1:44. [Fabletics](/blog/fabletics-customer-service) sent us to a consultant at 1:08 once we said we didn't have the account phone number. [Capital One](/blog/capital-one-customer-service) answered at 1:22 after we said we didn't have a card. [DIRECTV](/blog/directv-customer-service) put a person on at 2:47 after we said we had no account number.
2. **Pick the option for new customers.** The sales line at [Windstream (Kinetic)](/blog/windstream-customer-service) answered in 0:55, after two calls on the customer lines stopped at an account prompt. "Insurance for you or your family" at [UnitedHealthcare](/blog/unitedhealthcare-phone-number) took 1:04, the quote option at [AAA](/blog/aaa-insurance-customer-service) 1:24, a quote on a "new policy" at [Progressive](/blog/progressive-customer-service) 1:30, "open a new checking account" at [Bank of America](/blog/bank-of-america-customer-service) 2:05, "get a new policy", then "auto", at [GEICO](/blog/geico-customer-service) 2:35, and "not a member" at [USAA](/blog/usaa-phone-number) 3:28. "Make a purchase" on [Dell](/blog/dell-customer-service-number)'s sales line got a person at 4:38, who transferred us to customer care, after two calls to Dell's customer service line ended at an AI assistant that hung up.
3. **Give the menu a short, specific reason.** "Customer service" at [Pottery Barn](/blog/pottery-barn-customer-service) (0:49). "Booking a flight" at [JetBlue](/blog/jetblue-customer-service) (1:27). "Missing item", then "refund", at [DoorDash](/blog/doordash-customer-service-number) (2:08). "Flight change fee" at [United](/blog/united-change-flight), where a long sentence got "Sorry, I didn't understand." "Book a held reservation" at [American Airlines](/blog/american-airlines-flight-credit). "Cancel my account", then "disconnect", at [Spectrum](/blog/spectrum-retention-department).
4. **Ask for a person again, and keep answering.** [Hertz](/blog/hertz-customer-service-number)'s virtual assistant connected us after our sixth request (3:07). [Fubo](/blog/cancel-fubo)'s took two requests (1:59), and [Cash App](/blog/cash-app-customer-service-number)'s virtual agent three (1:35). At [UPS](/blog/ups-contact-number), "representative" said twice at the tracking number prompt got us into the queue (5:57). [Alaska Airlines](/blog/alaska-airlines-customer-service)' virtual assistant didn't transfer us when we asked for a representative, but once it had answered our questions it offered to connect us to reservations, and we said yes (12:44, most of it hold).

Saying "representative" on its own was the most common thing that **didn't** work. More on that below.

## How to reach a human at customer service: what worked

**Don't have the number? Say so, or enter nothing.** Most menus ask for an account, phone, card or confirmation number first. When we didn't have one, saying so out loud or just not entering one often moved us along:

- [Fifth Third](/blog/fifth-third-bank-customer-service) asked three times for a "full account or card number". We didn't enter one, and it said "Your call is being transferred due to invalid entries." A person answered at 1:44.
- [Fabletics](/blog/fabletics-customer-service)' voice assistant asked twice for the phone number on the account. When we said we didn't have it, it said "I will transfer you to a consultant for more help."
- [DIRECTV](/blog/directv-customer-service)'s AI agent asked for the phone number three times and then the account number. Saying we didn't have either, then choosing satellite, got a live representative.
- [Capital One](/blog/capital-one-customer-service)'s credit card menu wouldn't take "representative" at the card number prompt. Saying we didn't have an account got "Let's get you to someone who can help", and a person at 1:22.
- [Wells Fargo](/blog/wells-fargo-customer-service): "I don't have it" twice, then "agent", then "checking account question". A representative at 2:14. On the [credit card line](/blog/wells-fargo-dispute-charge), "I don't have it", a short reason, "my credit card" and "personal account" got a person at 2:52.
- [Capital One](/blog/capital-one-dispute-charge)'s 1-800-CAPITAL card line: "I don't have a Capital One card" got "Let's get you to someone who can help", and a representative at 0:47, our fastest bank call.
- [Discover](/blog/discover-dispute-charge) asked for the last four digits of a Social Security number. "I don't remember it", then "I don't have it", got us to billing disputes at 2:03, though the representative wouldn't say anything without an account.
- [JetBlue](/blog/jetblue-customer-service) asks for your birthday "to verify your identity". We entered nothing, and it asked what we were calling about instead.
- [Delta](/blog/delta-customer-service)'s AI-powered voice assistant asked for a six character confirmation number. "I don't have that. I just have a general question and I need a representative" got us transferred.

This doesn't always work. Experian, Expedia, FedEx, Uber, Verizon and Windstream stopped us at exactly this step (see below).

**Choose the new customer option.** Sales, quote and joining lines were consistently quick, and none of these calls needed an account number:

- [Windstream (Kinetic)](/blog/windstream-customer-service): the sales line, (866) 703-8175, press 1. A live sales rep at 0:55.
- [UnitedHealthcare](/blog/unitedhealthcare-phone-number): press 1, "insurance for you or your family". A licensed agent at 1:04.
- [AAA Mountain West Group](/blog/aaa-insurance-customer-service): press 4, "to become a AAA member or obtain an insurance quote", then a ZIP code. A person at 1:24.
- [Allstate](/blog/allstate-customer-service-number): "Let's get you to an agent. Just a few quick questions first to tailor your quote." A licensed insurance agent answered at 2:08.
- [Progressive](/blog/progressive-customer-service): an auto insurance quote, "new policy", then the state the car is parked in. A live agent at 1:30.
- [Bank of America](/blog/bank-of-america-customer-service): "open a new checking account", then saying we had no account number. A representative at 2:05, on our second call. Our first call said "general question" instead, and the menu ended it.
- [GEICO](/blog/geico-customer-service): "get a new policy", then "auto", and saying we wanted a new quote at the date of birth prompt. A licensed agent at 2:35, on our second call.
- [USAA](/blog/usaa-phone-number): press 1 for "not a member", then 2 for joining. The eligibility department at 3:28.
- [Dell](/blog/dell-customer-service-number): the sales line, 1-877-275-3355, "make a purchase", then "something else" at the product question. A person at the "Dell Sales Reception Desk" at 4:38. Our two calls to Dell's customer service number, 1-800-624-9897, never reached one.

Both [Wayfair](/blog/wayfair-customer-service) calls also ended up with Wayfair Sales (0:56 and 1:12). The catch: you may get a salesperson, not the team that can fix your problem. Ask them to transfer you. At Dell, that worked: when we said we had general questions, not a purchase, the sales agent transferred us, and a customer care representative answered at 7:10.

**Give a short reason in the menu's own words.** Speech menus want a few words, not a sentence:

- [Pottery Barn](/blog/pottery-barn-customer-service): "customer service", a person at 0:49.
- [JetBlue](/blog/jetblue-customer-service): "booking a flight", a crew member at 1:27.
- [DoorDash](/blog/doordash-customer-service-number): asking for a representative got "you can say things like, Dasher never arrived, missing item, or refund." Naming the problem, "missing item", then "refund", got a person at 2:08.
- [United](/blog/united-change-flight): "flight change fee". A long sentence about fees got "Sorry, I didn't understand."
- [American Airlines](/blog/american-airlines-flight-credit): "Apply a flight credit to a held reservation and ticket it" wasn't understood. "Book a held reservation" was.
- [Spectrum](/blog/spectrum-retention-department): "cancel my account", then "disconnect", to reach the people who make retention offers.
- [DIRECTV](/blog/directv-customer-service): "cancel service", a tip from DIRECTV's own community forum.
- [AT&T](/blog/how-to-lower-att-bill): "A billing question. My bill is too high." A representative at 1:59.
- [Xfinity](/blog/how-to-lower-xfinity-bill): "billing", once the voice assistant asked whether the call was about billing, a payment or an internet issue. Asking for an agent before naming a reason only got more questions. A billing representative answered at 6:29.

**Ask again, and keep answering.** Some assistants only connect you after a few tries:

- [Hertz](/blog/hertz-customer-service-number): "Let me connect you now" came after our sixth request, 2 minutes 44 seconds in. A person answered at 3:07.
- [Fubo](/blog/cancel-fubo): the AI assistant offered to answer instead and warned of "a wait of five minutes or more". We asked again for a human, then chose customer service. A person answered at 1:59.
- [Cash App](/blog/cash-app-customer-service-number): the virtual agent asked us to describe the issue, then offered to answer general questions itself. The third request for a live representative got the transfer, and a person at 1:35.
- [UPS](/blog/ups-contact-number): "representative", said twice at the tracking number prompt.
- [SiriusXM](/blog/how-to-cancel-siriusxm): we said it was a general policy question and asked for a person twice.
- [Verizon](/blog/verizon-customer-service): "Could I just speak with a representative, please?" didn't work. "An agent, please" did.
- [Alaska Airlines](/blog/alaska-airlines-customer-service): the virtual assistant answered our request for a reservations representative with "Can you tell me what you're calling about so I can get you to the right person?" We told it our questions and let it answer them. Then it asked, "Did you want me to connect you to reservations now to confirm for today?" We said yes, gave a first and last name, and a live agent answered at 12:44, after about 9 minutes 20 seconds on hold.

Some menus want a key press after a recording. [Airbnb](/blog/airbnb-customer-service-number) answered "agent" with a help message, then offered "press 0 to be transferred to a specialist". At [Expedia](/blog/expedia-customer-service-number), pressing 0 at the itinerary number prompt got us into the queue. At [Chime](/blog/chime-customer-service-number), press 9 ("all other questions"), then 0 at the message that points you to help.chime.com. A representative answered at 1:21, and nothing on the way asked for a card, account or Social Security number.

Some keypad menus are just quick. At [Instacart](/blog/instacart-customer-service-number), press 1 for English, then 1 for "your customer account". "Please hold while we transfer you," and a live agent answered at 0:24, with no voice assistant and no account number prompt.

And sometimes the answer is to do nothing. At [Hulu](/blog/hulu-customer-service-number) we pressed nothing after the menu, as it invites you to, and a person answered at 1:28.

## What didn't work

**"Representative" on its own.** It's the first thing everyone tries, and on these calls it was ignored more often than not:

- [Fifth Third](/blog/fifth-third-bank-customer-service): "Let's try again."
- [FPL](/blog/fpl-phone-number): "Sorry. That option is not available."
- [Experian](/blog/experian-phone-number): "I don't recognize that entry", then the Social Security number prompt again.
- [PayPal](/blog/paypal-customer-service-number): we said "Representative, I have a general question" three times and it carried on with its own questions.
- [USAA](/blog/usaa-phone-number): "Sorry, for what product?"
- [Capital One](/blog/capital-one-customer-service): it asked for the card number again, saying agents can help "much faster" if it verifies the card first.
- [Bank of America](/blog/bank-of-america-customer-service): "agent" got "For security, you must provide required information to speak with an associate" twice before it moved on.
- [Airbnb](/blog/airbnb-customer-service-number): "We didn't quite get that. Please try again."
- [Alaska Airlines](/blog/alaska-airlines-customer-service): "Can you tell me what you're calling about so I can get you to the right person?"
- [Dell](/blog/dell-customer-service-number): "You said you'd like to speak to a representative, is that right?", then a five option menu instead of a person.
- [Xfinity](/blog/how-to-lower-xfinity-bill): "I understand you'd like to speak to an agent, but I need a little more information from you", to "representative", "agent" and "speak to a person" alike.
- [AT&T](/blog/how-to-lower-att-bill): "Without a reason for your call, I'll need to end this call now," and it hung up.
- [FedEx](/blog/fedex-customer-service-number): "Sorry, I cannot connect you to a representative. For assistance, please call us back later with your door tag or tracking number."

**Account number walls.** Some lines wouldn't go anywhere without one:

- [FedEx](/blog/fedex-customer-service-number): none of our three calls without a tracking number reached a person, and none got a callback offer.
- [Verizon](/blog/verizon-customer-service): with no account, the AI assistant said "Please call back once you have that information ready" and ended the call.
- [Experian](/blog/experian-phone-number): the credit freeze line wants your full Social Security number before it does anything.
- [Windstream](/blog/windstream-customer-service): both calls that hit the account number prompt ended there.
- [Expedia](/blog/expedia-customer-service-number): saying we had no itinerary number got "I don't recognize that as a valid itinerary number" three times, then a hang up.
- [Bank of America](/blog/bank-of-america-customer-service): on our first call, "speak to a person" and no account number got "Please try your call again later when you have this information available."
- [FPL](/blog/fpl-phone-number): it asks for an account number or Social Security number. Our first four calls never reached a person.

**AI assistants that end the call.** Four assistants hung up on us, and four more calls ended because of our caller:

- [Hertz](/blog/hertz-customer-service-number): Haley said "Let me connect you with a live agent", then nothing happened. When our caller stayed silent through two "haven't heard from you" prompts, Haley ended the call. If it comes back and talks, answer it.
- [Fubo](/blog/cancel-fubo): we let the AI assistant answer a question instead of insisting on a person. It said "I'm sorry, but I can't help you with that" and the call ended.
- [Verizon](/blog/verizon-customer-service): the no-account call above, ended by the assistant.
- [Dell](/blog/dell-customer-service-number): both calls to the customer service number, 1-800-624-9897, ended at Dell's "AI-powered voice assistant", which sent us to www.dell.com/orders or "my account" and then hung up. On the second call we asked it twice for an agent, and both times it said "please visit www dot dell dot com forward slash orders or log into my account."
- [DIRECTV](/blog/directv-customer-service): on the first call, our caller turned down the AI agent's second offer to connect it and hung up. That one was our mistake.
- [Cash App](/blog/cash-app-customer-service-number): on the first call, our caller let the virtual agent answer its questions and said yes to "Have I resolved all of your issues today?" The call went to a survey without a person.
- [DIRECTV](/blog/how-to-lower-directv-bill), again on October 24: the AI agent asked "Would you like me to connect you with a live agent now?" Our caller said "Not yet" and ended the call.
- [Verizon](/blog/how-to-lower-verizon-bill): our caller said only "An agent, please," with no reason. "If you're not able to share that, I won't be able to continue the call," and the AI assistant hung up.

**Lines with no person at all.** Three companies gave us no way to a person on any call:

- [Coinbase](/blog/coinbase-customer-service-number): both US numbers Coinbase lists, 1-888-908-7930 and 1-844-613-1499, answered with the same recording, "phone support is currently unavailable", and hung up about 11 seconds in. No menu, nothing to press.
- [Lyft](/blog/lyft-customer-service-number): Lyft doesn't publish a general customer service number. Both numbers on Lyft's own site, 855-865-9553 and (707) 663-4025, played a recording sending us to the Help tab in the Lyft app and help.lyft.com, then hung up about 29 seconds in.
- [Uber](/blog/uber-customer-service-number): Uber's rider help page says "we don't offer a customer service number for Uber riders." The premium support line, 1-800-314-2308, only asks for the 10-digit phone number on your Uber account. After three entries it didn't recognize, it said "For security reasons, we'll unfortunately need to end this call." The other rider number on Uber's help center, +1 833-315-8991, is "for riders in the state of Colorado". Both lines end with "Please contact our support team through the help section of your app."

**Menu loops.**

- [FPL](/blog/fpl-phone-number) sent every question back to its new account signup. It took us five calls to find the route that reached an agent.
- [UPS](/blog/ups-contact-number) answered "We don't have a tracking number" with "I'm sorry, I didn't understand that".
- [USAA](/blog/usaa-phone-number) offers "membership questions" as an option but re-asked the question when we said it. Plain "membership" worked on the second try.

## When you can't reach a person

- **Ask for a callback.** [FedEx](/blog/fedex-customer-service-number) only offered one once we gave it a tracking number. It called back about 4 minutes after we first dialed, and an agent was on the line 23 seconds into the callback. [Delta](/blog/delta-customer-service) offered a callback "within 14 minutes" or messaging with "the wait time is zero minutes". We stayed on hold instead, so we can't say how those went.
- **Try a different number.** At [Windstream](/blog/windstream-customer-service), the support line and the existing customer option both stopped at an account prompt. The sales line's new customer option reached a person. At [SimonMed Imaging](/blog/simonmed-imaging-phone-number), the cardiology scheduling line skipped the menu and reached a person in 0:13 to 0:21 on four calls; the main line took 1:44 and 1:49 through a menu, two submenus and a hold. At [Dell](/blog/dell-customer-service-number), the customer service number ended at an AI assistant twice; the sales line got a person, who transferred us to customer care.
- **Call again with a different approach.** Our second calls to Bank of America, Cash App, DIRECTV, Expedia, Fubo, GEICO, UPS, Venmo and Verizon reached a person after the first didn't, and so did our third call to Hertz and our fifth to FPL. On October 13, our fourth call to AT&T did after three didn't, and on October 24 so did our second calls to AT&T, Costco and DIRECTV. Each time we changed something.
- **Some lines are built so you can't.** Across 15 calls one afternoon, no route on [Experian](/blog/experian-phone-number)'s credit freeze line reached a consumer support agent, and on October 25 its membership line's AI assistant said it "can only transfer calls under specific circumstances" and hung up. Pressing 2 on its headquarters line, 714-830-7000, got a real operator, but only to transfer us back into the same system. It kept steering us to freeze online or by mail. On our calls, the phone lines at [Coinbase](/blog/coinbase-customer-service-number), [Lyft](/blog/lyft-customer-service-number) and [Uber](/blog/uber-customer-service-number) never put anyone on. Each one pointed us to a help center or the app instead (see above).

## Methodology

- **Who called:** call4me, our AI phone caller, on behalf of an AI agent. Twenty-seven of the 138 company line calls were made by call4me users' agents for their own errands: AAA Northeast, Afternic, American Airlines (2), Aquasana, Blue Shield of California, Etihad, Global Blue, Justworks, MetLife Pet Insurance, Peloton, Piedmont Natural Gas, SimonMed Imaging (6), Southwest, Spectrum, T-Mobile (3, to the Home Internet line), Vanguard, Vitalant, Vivint and Walmart. So were the store, clinic, tow company, government and court calls. The rest were our own calls, mostly general questions with no account, plus a few on our own accounts (Amazon Pharmacy, Experian, a FedEx package, Verizon).
- **When:** September 28 to October 26, 2026. Each company was called on a single day, except SimonMed Imaging (three days) and AT&T, Capital One, Costco, DIRECTV, Experian, Southwest, T-Mobile, Verizon, Walmart, Wells Fargo and Xfinity (two days each), at whatever time we were writing about it, so these numbers say nothing about how hold times change by time of day or day of week.
- **Which calls count:** the calls in the published customer service, cancellation, retention, bill, return policy, dispute and hospital bill posts for these 77 companies, one row per published call. Most companies got one call. The Experian post describes 15 calls to the freeze line but publishes one recording, so the freeze line counts as one call, plus one call to its membership line. The hospital bill guide covers three hospital systems, so each one is its own company. The Bank of America, Expedia and Wells Fargo posts each describe a first call whose recording isn't published; those count too, from the post's description. So do the first calls in the AT&T, Costco and DIRECTV bill and return posts, the second call in the Verizon bill post, Amazon's second call (placed by Goose) and the first AT&T customer service call, which our own time limit ended on hold. AT&T, Capital One, Costco, DIRECTV, Verizon, Wells Fargo and Xfinity each have two posts; both posts' calls count under one company, and the company's time on [the hold times page](/hold-times) is from its first post. Best Buy's time there is its first call, 4:19. The SimonMed Imaging post mentions three calls it doesn't publish; we count only the six it publishes. Uber's premium support line and its Colorado rider line count as one company, Uber. Dell's time is to the sales agent who answered at 4:38, not the customer care representative they transferred us to at 7:10. The Walmart post describes three calls that a recording refused, all from one of our phone numbers, and publishes one; we count that one. Its AI agent call, where we didn't ask for a person, counts as a call that didn't reach one. Where a post has several, each one is a separate row in the data, and the table above says which call reached a person. We left out two calls that don't measure a company's line: a follow-up to the LA Fitness club that nobody answered, and Verizon's callback after our call hit call4me's time limit (the representative was already reached on the call before). The single location calls (gym clubs, stores, clinics and a tow company), the government and court calls, and FedEx's callback are in the CSV, marked by line type, and left out of the stats.
- **How times were measured:** from the start of each published recording to the first moment a live person speaks, read from the timestamps in each post. Several recordings have long silences shortened, so on those the real wait was a little longer. Where the post gives the real elapsed time, the CSV notes it. For example, United's person picked up 19 minutes 27 seconds after dialing, and Verizon's 6 minutes 34 seconds into the call. If a post doesn't say when a person answered (SiriusXM), or the call that reached one isn't published (Wells Fargo's first call, about 3 minutes 45 seconds in by the post's account), we left the time blank rather than estimate it.
- **"AI assistant first"** means the post itself calls what answered an AI, virtual, voice or automated assistant, or an AI agent. A speech or keypad menu that the post calls a menu or phone system counts as no.
- **Our caller made mistakes.** In 14 of the 42 calls that didn't reach a person, the post puts at least part of the blame on our caller: treating the virtual agent's answers as the goal (Cash App), never asking for a person (Hertz), staying silent when an assistant came back (Hertz), turning down a transfer (DIRECTV, twice), letting the AI assistant answer instead (Fubo), a bug on our side (UPS), a call that dropped on our side (AT&T), picking "pay my bill" just to give a reason (AT&T), stalling (Windstream), picking the wrong option (Windstream), asking for a person without giving a reason (AT&T and Verizon) and pressing into the wrong menu (Costco). They're still in the data.
- **What this isn't:** a ranking of customer service quality. It's a small sample, mostly one call per company, and how fast someone picks up says nothing about whether they can help. Several of the fast answers were sales teams.

## Have your agent make these calls

call4me is a phone for your AI agent: your agent (Claude Code, Codex, ChatGPT) tells it what to get done and it makes the call, waits through the menu and the hold, and reports back with the outcome and the transcript. It can also ring you when a person picks up, so you skip the hold: see [an AI that waits on hold for you](/hold-for-me) and [an AI that calls customer service for you](/call-customer-service). If you dread these calls, the numbers above say you're not wrong to, and our [phone anxiety guide](/blog/phone-anxiety) has scripts for the ones you make yourself. If you use call4me, paste this into your agent:

> Call [company] customer service at [number] and reach a live person. Answer the menu's questions in a few words. If it asks for an account or confirmation number, give [my number], or say I don't have it and ask for a representative. If it doesn't take "representative", try "agent", say what I'm calling about in a few words, or pick the new customer option and ask to be transferred. If an assistant goes quiet, answer it when it comes back. Ask [your question], and don't agree to any change or charge without checking with me.
