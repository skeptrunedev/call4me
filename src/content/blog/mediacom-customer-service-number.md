**The Mediacom customer service number is 1 855 633 4226.** Mediacom lists it in its [official broadband support information](https://mediacomcable.com/legal/internet-broadband-disclosures/), which says customer service agents are available 24 hours a day, seven days a week for broadband issues.

We called on **November 2, 2026**, using [call4me](/), and reached a person about **1 minute 44 seconds** into the recording. The voice menu asked which service our question concerned. Saying **“Internet”** led to a transfer.

Our question was general: where should an existing customer check for an outage, and what should they have ready when calling support? We did not have a Mediacom account or an outage to investigate. This call measures one route on one day, not an average wait or a promise that your call will follow the same menu.

## How we reached a person at Mediacom

The menu did not immediately understand our general question. Here is what happened:

1. **The greeting asked about an account.** It offered the star key for people who were not Mediacom customers. Otherwise, it asked for the phone number associated with the account. Use your own account details when calling about your service.
2. **The voice menu asked why we were calling.** We described it as a general question and asked for the available choices. The menu said it had not understood, so the caller repeated the request.
3. **It asked which service we needed.** The choices spoken were Internet, cable TV and telephone, with an option to say more than one. Our caller answered “Internet.”
4. **The system announced a transfer.** It offered text messaging with a representative by pressing 1, or staying on the line to speak with someone. We stayed on the line.
5. **A representative answered.** We asked the general question and received an answer without supplying an account number or claiming to have a service problem.

This is evidence for the internet support route we encountered. We did not test billing disputes, a cancellation request, mobile service or technician scheduling.

## Where should you check for a Mediacom outage?

The representative said existing customers could use the **Mediacom mobile care app** to check for outages or maintenance in their area. She said signing in required a **Mediacom ID**.

Mediacom's website also links to its [official outage map](https://support.mediacomcable.com/OutageMap) and [customer support portal](https://support.mediacomcable.com/). Those are useful starting points for your own service. We did not sign into a customer account, check an address or verify an outage during this test.

At the end of the call, the representative offered to check for an outage. Our caller declined because there was no customer problem to investigate. The result was an answer about the support process, not a repaired connection or a confirmed outage.

## What should you have ready before calling?

The representative said phone support generally needs the **phone number on the account, account name and service address**. She also said additional identity verification might be needed, depending on what was set up for the account.

Have those account details available, along with a clear description of the problem. For an internet issue, describe when it began, whether every device is affected, and what you have already checked. Ask whether the next step is an area outage update, troubleshooting or a technician visit.

We did not test account verification. If an assistant calls for you and the representative needs to verify your identity, join the call and provide the required information directly.
