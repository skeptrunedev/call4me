**The short answer:** to lower an AT&T bill, start with the discounts AT&T publishes, then call **800-288-2020** and tell the voice system **"a billing question. My bill is too high."** That got us to a live representative **1 minute 59 seconds into the call**. Asking for a "representative" without a reason got us hung up on.

The concrete ways to lower an AT&T bill:

- **AutoPay with a bank account and paperless billing.** AT&T's [AutoPay discount page](https://www.att.com/deals/autopay-discount/) says a bank account gets "a monthly discount of $10 per phone line," and $10 a month on internet. A debit card gets $5. "There's no longer an AutoPay discount for other credit cards." You also "have to sign up for paperless billing."
- **Bundle internet and wireless.** The representative called it the "converged discount": "$20 off for a certain amount of time, or the converged discount stays on ongoing for internet."
- **Multi-line pricing.** "If it's four lines, they're offering $5 off per line."
- **Bring your own phone.** "Bring your own devices, which gives you $5.56 for next 36 months."
- **Discounts by who you are.** "First responders, military, 55, or depending what they do for work, they also get a signature discount."
- **Waived fees.** "Some term discounts give you activation fees waived."

The bundle, line, phone and group discounts are the representative's words on October 24, 2026, not terms we checked. They depend on your plan and area. The representative mostly answered as if we were thinking of switching to AT&T, asking how many lines and whether we wanted fiber or Internet Air, so ask which of these apply to an existing account.

**What else the representative said:**

- **By phone, with the account holder.** Asked if these can be handled by phone with the account holder on the call: "That's correct, yes."
- **Who to ask for.** "If you talk to the AI bot or like our automated system, you could just say start new services, or you could ask for anybody from mobility sales or like sales or anybody in billing, because some of those people could handle that."
- **Hours.** "Mondays to Fridays, we're open 8 a.m. to 8 p.m. in central time. And then on Saturdays, we're open 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. And then Sundays, we're closed."

We called on Saturday, October 24, 2026, at about 5:28 pm Central, with [call4me](/) asking general questions. We're not AT&T customers and gave no account details. This page is what we heard.

## The AT&T number to call

AT&T's [customer service page](https://www.att.com/cfd/customer-service/) lists **800-288-2020** for existing customers, "Available 24/7." Its [Internet support page](https://www.att.com/support/internet/) lists the same number for "Bill account & tech support," open "M - F, 8 a.m. - 8 p.m. CT" and "Sa, 9 a.m. - 7 p.m. CT," closed Sunday. The representative's hours (above) were different again. For billing, call on a weekday between 8 am and 8 pm Central to be safe.

For a wireless bill, AT&T also lists **1-800-331-0500**. Our [AT&T customer service guide](/blog/att-customer-service-number) has that line's phone tree and a 44 minute hold.

## The phone tree, step by step

1. **"Welcome to AT&T."** A recording notice, "Para español, oprima el 8," and an ad for the AT&T app.
2. **"What's the issue you're calling about?"** Say **"A billing question. My bill is too high."**
3. **"What's the account number or phone number on the account?"** Give yours. We said "I don't have it," and it said "That's fine."
4. **"Which product are you calling about?"** We said "Internet," then "high-speed internet" (the other choices were DSL, Internet Air and fixed wireless internet).
5. **"A personal or business account?"** "Personal."
6. **It offers to text you a link** about the charges on your bill. Say **"No thanks, I'd rather speak to a representative."**
7. **"Let me find someone to help you."** A person answered about 25 seconds later. We didn't hear a hold time quoted.

**What doesn't work:** on our first call that evening, our caller said "Representative" at the first prompt, then gave no reason. The system said "I do need a reason for your call to keep going," then: "Without a reason for your call, I'll need to end this call now," and hung up. That was our caller's mistake, and the same thing happened on our earlier [AT&T wireless calls](/blog/att-customer-service-number).

## What to say: a script

> Hi, I'm the account holder. My bill has gone up to [amount] a month. I'd like to see what you can do: am I getting the AutoPay discount, is there a bundle or converged discount for my internet and wireless, and do I qualify for any discounts for [first responders / military / 55+ / my employer]? I'm also looking at [competitor and price].

Have your account number or the phone number on the account, and your account passcode. On our [earlier AT&T wireless call](/blog/att-customer-service-number), the representative said to have "their account number available, as well as their four to six digit PIN number."
