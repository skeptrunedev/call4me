**Blood work has no single price because the test list matters.** On October 7, 2026, Labcorp OnDemand listed a complete blood count (CBC) for **$29**, while Quest Health listed its CBC for **$29 plus a $6 physician service fee**, or **$35 for that test and stated fee**. A metabolic panel or a longer list of tests is a different purchase. These are published online prices, not estimates of what your insurance or a hospital will charge.

Your AI assistant can help turn a clinician's test list into a comparison: find the matching tests, check fees, call about missing details and bring back a quote. [call4me](/) supplies the phone calls so the assistant can continue the errand when a lab needs to answer directly. Our guide to [an AI agent that makes phone calls for you](/blog/ai-agent-that-makes-phone-calls) explains how to set the task and check the result. Here, the useful output is the price for the exact requested work at an available location.

## Published blood test prices to use as a starting point

We checked the providers' own product pages on October 7, 2026. All figures below are US dollars. No test was purchased, and these prices were not confirmed by a new phone call for this article.

| Provider and test | Published price | Fee or qualification |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| [Labcorp OnDemand CBC](https://www.ondemand.labcorp.com/lab-tests/complete-blood-count) | $29 | Online product price; check availability and the final order total |
| [Quest Health CBC](https://www.questhealth.com/product/complete-blood-count-cbc/6399M.html) | $29 plus $6 physician service fee | $35 for the test and stated fee; optional services can add cost |
| [Labcorp OnDemand comprehensive metabolic panel](https://www.ondemand.labcorp.com/lab-tests/comprehensive-metabolic-panel) | $36.75 promotional price; $49 regular price shown | The page displayed 25% off when checked; confirm the current offer |
| [Quest Health hemoglobin A1c](https://www.questhealth.com/product/hemoglobin-a1c-test/496-min.html) | $35.10 promotional price plus $6 physician service fee | $41.10 with the stated fee during the offer; $45 at the listed $39 regular test price |

The CBC rows offer a starting point for a comparison. The metabolic panel and A1c rows illustrate why “blood work” needs a specific test name. They are not substitutes for a CBC or a recommendation to buy more tests. Ask your clinician which tests are appropriate and whether a proposed product matches the order.

For several tests, ask for the total for the whole order. Do not multiply a service fee by the number of tests without checking how it is charged. If you need collection at home, price that separately: [Quest Mobile](https://www.questhealth.com/product/quest-mobile-in-home-sample-collection/12043M.html) lists a $79 collection service in eligible areas, with testing purchased separately.

## Have your AI assistant compare blood work costs

Connect [call4me](/mcp) to your assistant using the [Claude Code guide](/blog/claude-code-phone-calls) or [Codex guide](/blog/codex-phone-calls). Give it the test names from your clinician's order, your location and the information you authorize sharing.

> Compare cash prices for the tests on my clinician's order: [exact test names and any specified methods or codes]. Look near [city or ZIP code]. Check official provider pages first, then use call4me to call up to [number] labs for missing information. Confirm that each quote covers the same requested tests and ask about collection, ordering, processing and any outside lab fees. Ask whether they accept my existing order, which location can collect the samples, available dates, how to receive a written itemized estimate and how long it is valid. If they need time to price it, they may call back on my call4me number. Return confirmed answers, dated prices and unanswered questions in a table. Do not select different tests, purchase anything, book an appointment or share additional medical history without asking me.

If you already have prices online, narrow the call to what is missing. For example: “Does this location accept my order, and does this quote include collection?” Asking a short, specific question is more useful than asking a receptionist to price an undefined full panel.

## Blood work cost without insurance: compare the complete order

Start by deciding which billing route you are asking about. A consumer product bought online and a clinician's order billed through a lab can have different terms. Quest Health's [CBC product page](https://www.questhealth.com/product/complete-blood-count-cbc/6399M.html) says its direct purchases are not billed to insurance and cannot be submitted for insurance reimbursement. That statement applies to the Quest Health purchase route, not every service performed by Quest Diagnostics.

For an existing order, ask the lab for a cash estimate covering all listed tests. Have your assistant record which fees are included and which require a separate quote. If comparing an insurance route too, request an estimate for your particular plan through the lab and insurer; a posted cash price does not establish your insured cost.

| Detail to confirm | Question for the lab |
| :--- | :--- |
| Exact tests | Does the quote match every test and method on this order? |
| Collection | Is the blood draw included, and at which location? |
| Order requirements | Can you accept this order, or is separate provider review required? |
| Outside processing | Will anything be sent elsewhere and billed separately? |
| Complete price | What is the total, including ordering and collection fees? |
| Quote status | Can you send an itemized estimate and its expiration date? |

Keep those questions attached to each provider's answer. An inexpensive test with an unanswered collection fee remains an incomplete comparison.

## What if the lab needs to call back with a price?

This is a useful task for an assistant to continue. In a call4me workflow we reviewed, a lab could not finish pricing during the first call. It later called back with a quote and collection logistics. We are using that workflow here without publishing the customer's private test list or presenting their custom price as a typical bill.

When a lab needs more time, ask who is preparing the estimate, what information is missing and when a reply is expected. If you authorize a callback, the assistant can give your call4me number and collect the response. Have it compare the returned estimate with the original request before marking the task complete.

Sometimes the lab needs a written order first. Ask for its approved way to receive it and review the destination before sending sensitive information. A call that obtains submission instructions is progress, but it is not yet a price quote.

## Do you need a doctor's order?

Ask about the route you intend to use. Labcorp OnDemand's [CBC page](https://www.ondemand.labcorp.com/lab-tests/complete-blood-count) explains that an authorized provider reviews and approves its test requests. That does not mean every independent lab accepts every outside order, or that a cheaper online product matches the test your clinician requested.

Your assistant should gather ordering requirements and bring questions back to the lab or clinician. It should not choose diagnostic tests or change preparation instructions. Confirm preparation for the exact appointment with the healthcare team.

## Turn the quote into a confirmed appointment

Once you choose a provider, give the assistant a separate booking instruction with your available dates. Ask it to confirm the location, arrival time, accepted order, quoted price and where preparation instructions will arrive. Keep any unanswered fee or order question visible in the final recap.
