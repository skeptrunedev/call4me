## What our caller got wrong

The recording shows a few things to fix in your own brief:

- **It said goodbye too early.** Asked who referred the patient, it said it would follow up and call back, and said "Bye", then carried on when the receptionist kept going. Give the referral answer up front (the dentist's name, or "no referral") so this question has an answer.
- **It said it would check, despite being told not to pause.** "Let me check on that real quick" and "Give me one second" are hold phrases. The receptionist answered anyway.
- **It read the group number two different ways.** The first reading and the second reading did not match digit for digit. If the office is going to verify insurance, the number has to be read the same way each time.
- **The reminder number.** The assistant gave a number for text reminders that was not the personal phone in the patient's saved details. Name the exact number you want reminders sent to.

## Implant consultations and bone grafting: what to ask a San Francisco oral surgeon

This call shows what an oral and maxillofacial surgeon's front desk asks a new patient before a consult: the kind of visit, insurance carrier and member numbers, the patient's name and date of birth, a phone number for reminders, the referring dentist, and which days work.

It also shows what the front desk will and won't answer by phone. A slot and the exam fee were easy to get. A plan-specific insurance check was not available on the spot, and treatment prices for an extraction, socket bone graft and implant depend on the surgeon's exam. If you are comparing offices for dental implants or bone grafting in San Francisco, ask for the exam fee and the earliest opening on the first call, and ask the office to verify your plan before the visit rather than expecting itemized estimates up front.
