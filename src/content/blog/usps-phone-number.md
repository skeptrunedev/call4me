**The short answer:** the USPS customer service phone number is **1-800-ASK-USPS (1-800-275-8777)**, open **Monday to Friday 8 AM to 8:30 PM Eastern and Saturday 8 AM to 6 PM Eastern**. **An automated voice menu answers, not a person.** It understands spoken answers, but it kept steering us into sub-menus and offering to text a link. What finally worked: **say "no" to the text link, give a ZIP code when it asks, and when it asks for your street address, say "representative."** It asked "Do you want to speak with an agent?", quoted a 5 minute wait, and **a live representative answered 8 minutes 14 seconds into the recording**, about 22 seconds after the wait estimate.

The number and hours are from USPS's [Contact Us page](https://www.usps.com/help/contact-us.htm), which lists the Customer Care Center as "1-800-ASK-USPS® (1-800-275-8777)" with hours "Monday – Friday 8 AM – 8:30 PM ET" and "Saturday 8 AM – 6 PM ET."

Here's the path that got us to a person:

1. Call 1-800-275-8777. Press 1 for Spanish, 2 for the privacy policy.
2. "To better serve you, please enter your five digit zip code." We didn't enter one here, and it moved on after a service alert.
3. "In a few words, please tell me how I can help you." We said we had a general question for a representative. It read the main menu: Package, Mail, Tools, Stamps, Alerts, or Other.
4. We said **"Other,"** and from there the menu wandered (technical support, then Informed Delivery). Saying "representative" inside a sub-menu got "Sorry, I didn't get that."
5. It offered twice to text a link "where you can see and enter your information on the screen." **We said no both times.**
6. "I need to get the zip code first so I can look up the city and state." **We said a ZIP code** (muted in the recording) and confirmed it.
7. "Please say the street address." We said we didn't have one, it asked again, and **we said "Representative."**
8. "Do you want to speak with an agent? Is that correct?" **"Yeah, that's right."** A high call volume message, then "Your estimated wait time is five minutes."
9. A recorded Click-N-Ship ad, then a person, at 8:14.

**What the representative told us** (his answers, not official USPS policy, checked against usps.com [below](#the-representative-versus-the-website)):

- **Delivery time:** Ground Advantage "typically has a 2 to 5 business day delivery window" and Priority Mail "2 to 3." "They both contain $100 in insurance."
- **Missing mail:** a search "can be initiated as early as 7 days from the date of mailing and no later than the 365th day," online at USPS.com.
- **Hold Mail:** online, "by speaking to an agent like myself," or at your local post office. Online requests can be made "up to 30 days in advance," and "if requests are submitted before 2 a.m. Central Time," the hold can start the same business day.
- **Texts:** USPS sends package texts only if you sign up. A text asking for personal information, a card number or a redelivery fee is "typically scam messages." Forward it to **7726** and email **spam@uspis.gov**.

## The USPS phone menu, step by step

It's a voice menu that takes full sentences, with keypad numbers on some levels. Our caller answered by voice and pressed no keys.

1. **"Thank you for calling the United States Postal Service Customer Care Center. Para continuar en español, oprima uno. To hear our privacy policy, press two."**
2. **"To better serve you, please enter your five digit zip code."** We didn't, and after a pause it played a service alert ("Continued impacts from Hurricane Isaias in the Gulf region of the U.S. may delay final delivery of your mail and packages") and a survey notice.
3. **"In a few words, please tell me how I can help you."** Our first answer included a ZIP code and got "Sorry, I didn't get that." Our second, "I have a general question for a representative," got the main menu.
4. **The main menu**, spoken only:
   - Track a package, report a delivery issue, check a package service request, or schedule a redelivery: say **Package**
   - Daily mail services, including delivery, pickup, change of address and Hold Mail: say **Mail**
   - Prices, post office hours and locations, ZIP codes: say **Tools**
   - Order stamps or check a stamp order: say **Stamps**
   - Service alerts: say **Alerts**
   - Anything else: say **Other**
5. **"Other"** reads a numbered list: technical support (1), passports (2), reporting fraud or vandalism (3), GoPost or small business (4), free shipping supplies (5), Every Door Direct Mail (6).
6. **From here our call wandered.** Our caller said "Hmm," and the menu heard "technical support." It asked "how-to information or troubleshooting help," then read a how-to list (Click-N-Ship, claims, PO Box, Informed Delivery, domestic and international refunds). "Representative" here got "Sorry, I didn't get that." It offered to text an Informed Delivery link, then read an Informed Delivery help list.
7. **"To continue, we need to collect some information to get you the right help. I can send you a text link where you can see and enter your information on the screen. Should I do that? Yes or no."** We said no twice.
8. **"I need to get the zip code first so I can look up the city and state."** It read back the ZIP with the city and asked us to confirm.
9. **"Please say the street address."** We had none to give. The second time it asked, we said "Representative," and it asked whether we wanted an agent.
10. **The transfer.** A high call volume message, a survey notice, "Your estimated wait time is five minutes," a Click-N-Ship ad, and then the representative, about 22 seconds after the estimate.

If your call is about a real package or address, the text link or the address step may be the faster route for you. We had no address to give, so we can't say where those lead.

## The representative versus the website

We checked each answer on usps.com and uspis.gov on October 13, 2026. Most of it matches. Here's where it doesn't, or where the website adds something.

| Topic | The representative | The website |
|---|---|---|
| Delivery time | Ground Advantage 2 to 5 business days, Priority Mail 2 to 3 | The [Mail & Shipping Services page](https://www.usps.com/ship/mail-shipping-services.htm) lists Priority Mail at "2-3 days" and Ground Advantage at "2-5 days" |
| Insurance | Both "contain $100 in insurance" | The same page says "Up to $100 insurance included" for both, and that you can buy more |
| Missing mail search | 7 to 365 days after mailing, online | The [Missing Mail FAQ](https://faq.usps.com/articles/FAQ/Missing-Mail-The-Basics) says the earliest is "seven days after the original date of mailing" and the latest is "365 days after the original date of mailing" |
| Hold Mail, how to ask | Online, by phone, or at the local post office | The [Hold Mail FAQ](https://faq.usps.com/articles/FAQ/USPS-Hold-Mail-The-Basics) lists all three: online, "calling 1-800-ASK-USPS (1-800-275-8777)," or in person with "PS Form 8076, Authorization to Hold Mail" |
| Hold Mail, how far ahead | Online up to 30 days ahead; same business day if before 2 AM Central | The FAQ: "up to 30 days in advance of the start date," and "Online USPS Hold Mail requests submitted before 2:00 A.M. Central Time can begin on the same Postal business day" |
| Hold Mail, length | "As early as three days from the date of mailing and no later than 30 days from the date of mailing" | The FAQ says holds "must be for a minimum of 3 days and a maximum of 30 days." We think he meant the hold length; "date of mailing" doesn't fit Hold Mail |
| Picking up held mail | You have 10 days after the hold ends | The FAQ: "Mail must be picked up within 10 days or it will be returned to the senders" |
| USPS texts | Only if you opt in with a tracking number or Informed Delivery | The Postal Inspection Service's [smishing page](https://www.uspis.gov/news/scam-article/smishing): "USPS will not send customers text messages or e-mails without a customer first requesting the service with a tracking number, and it will NOT contain a link" |
| Redelivery fees | "We do not charge fees for re-deliveries" | The [Redelivery FAQ](https://faq.usps.com/articles/FAQ/Redelivery-The-Basics): "Scheduling a Redelivery is free" |
| Reporting scam texts | Forward to 7726, email spam@uspis.gov | The smishing page: "Forward the smishing/text message to 7726" and "send an email to spam@uspis.gov" |

Two things the website says that the representative didn't: a real USPS tracking text **won't contain a link** at all, and phone or in-person Hold Mail requests "must be accepted by close of business of your local Post Office location or the Customer Care Center for the request to be completed by the next delivery day," so the 2 AM same-day rule is for online requests.

## How to spot a fake USPS text

The representative's list of warning signs: a text that asks for personal identifying information, asks you to click a link because a package has an insufficient address and will be returned, or asks for a credit card or a redelivery fee. "The Postal Service, we do not send unsolicited text message emails or phone calls to customers regarding packages." Real fees, he said, are postage due or collect on delivery, collected by the carrier at your door or by a notice to pay at your post office. "We'll never ask for credit card information over the phone or through email or text."

To report one, the [USPIS smishing page](https://www.uspis.gov/news/scam-article/smishing) says: "send an email to spam@uspis.gov. Without clicking on the web link, copy the body of the suspicious text message and paste into a new email. Provide your name in the email, and also attach a screenshot of the text message showing the phone number of the sender and the date sent." It also says to "Forward the smishing/text message to 7726 (this will assist with reporting the scam phone number)."

## What wasn't answered

- **How to skip the menu.** We didn't ask the representative, and the menu never offered a direct "agent" option. The steps above are just what worked on our call.
- **Whether USPS offers a callback.** Nobody offered one, and we didn't ask.
- **What to have ready.** For a real package question, the menu clearly wanted a ZIP code and street address, and the tracking line exists for a reason, so having your tracking number, ZIP and address ready is likely to help. That's our inference from the menu, not something USPS told us.

## Things we didn't test

- **Saying "agent" or "representative" at the very first prompt,** before giving any reason.
- **Pressing 0,** or any key.
- **Saying yes to the text link,** or giving a street address.
- **The Package or Mail branches,** which are probably where a real tracking or Hold Mail question belongs.
- **Calling at a busier time.** Our quoted wait was 5 minutes and the real one was about 22 seconds, late on a Tuesday afternoon Eastern.
