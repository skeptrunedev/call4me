**The short answer:** if you're a member, the UnitedHealthcare phone number to call is **the one on your member ID card**. That's what uhc.com tells members of Medicare Advantage, Part D and Medicaid plans, and the "Already a member?" line on its Medicare page says the same. UnitedHealthcare customer service is split by plan type, so the card is the quickest way to the right team.

If you don't have your card, these are the numbers UnitedHealthcare lists on [uhc.com](https://www.uhc.com/contact-us), checked on October 1, 2026:

| Your plan | Call |
|---|---|
| Medicare Advantage, Part D or Medicaid (members) | The number on your member ID card |
| A plan through work | **1-866-801-4409** |
| Medicare, if you don't have a member ID | **1-877-596-3258**, TTY 711, 8 a.m. to 8 p.m., 7 days a week ([uhc.com/medicare](https://www.uhc.com/medicare)) |
| Medicare supplement | **1-800-523-5800** |
| Individual and family ACA marketplace plans (members) | A different number for each state, for example Texas **1-866-811-2704** and Florida **1-888-200-0405**. "8 a.m. to 8 p.m. local time, Monday through Friday, in most states" |
| Individual and family plans, shopping | **1-888-585-0631**, TTY 711 ([uhc.com/individuals-families](https://www.uhc.com/individuals-families)). For ACA plans uhc.com also lists **1-877-853-1760** |
| Short term plans | **1-844-635-2495** on the individual and family page, and **1-800-657-8205** on the contact page |
| Dental and vision | **1-866-877-6187** |
| Oxford | **1-800-444-6222** |
| Business sales | **1-800-650-5826** |

**If you're not a member,** this got us to a person on the first try:

1. **Call 1-888-585-0631.**
2. **Press 1** ("If you're looking for insurance for you or your family").

"You will now be connected with a licensed advisor", and a licensed insurance agent answered 1 minute 4 seconds into the call, about 25 seconds after we pressed 1. He told us:

- **In California, individual coverage goes through the marketplace.** UnitedHealthcare also has fixed indemnity plans, but California requires minimum essential coverage, "which would be a policy through the marketplace anyways."
- **Open enrollment begins November 1.**
- **Before then, you need a qualifying life event,** like losing coverage through an employer or a denial from Medicaid. Without one, "we wouldn't be able to look at a policy now."

We called as a non-member, on Thursday, October 1, 2026, at about 4:09 pm Eastern, with [call4me](/) asking the questions. We didn't test the member lines. This page is what we heard.

## What the agent told us

We asked how someone without UnitedHealthcare coverage would get an individual plan in California, and whether UnitedHealthcare sells one.

- **Where the plans are:** "So it would be through the marketplace for UnitedHealthcare. We also have, like, fixed indemnity plans, but in the state of California, you have to have minimum essential coverage, which would be a policy through the marketplace anyways."
- **When you can sign up:** "with the marketplace, open enrollment begins November 1st."
- **Signing up before then:** "So if there's not, like, a qualifying life event, so if you haven't got, lost any coverage, like to an employer, or got any denial through Medicaid, anything in that format, we wouldn't be able to look at a policy now. We would have to wait until then."
- **Getting more help:** he offered, "I can get you over to a team to see, you know, about that." We didn't take him up on it.

Then we asked what number someone should call to reach a real person, and whether it's just the number on the member ID card. He said "Let me see if I can pull up a different number", looked for about half a minute, and read out a number he said "will get you to a live agent that would be certified in California". **We couldn't verify that number.** It doesn't match any number UnitedHealthcare lists, and it isn't a valid US phone number. So we've left it out of this page and muted it in the recording. For a person, use the numbers in the table at the top of this page, or the one on your ID card.

## How to talk to a real person at UnitedHealthcare if you're a member

We called as someone without UnitedHealthcare coverage, so we haven't tested a member call yet. This is what we expect, based on the menu we heard and what uhc.com says. We'll replace it with a recorded member call.

1. **Call the number on your member ID card.** uhc.com tells Medicare Advantage, Part D and Medicaid members to do exactly that, and plans through work, Medicare supplement, Oxford, short term and dental and vision each have their own line (the table at the top of this page).
2. **If you call 1-888-585-0631 instead, press 3.** The menu we heard sends anyone who "currently [has] insurance with us, [has] a claims question or [has] applied for coverage" to option 3. We pressed 1, so we don't know where 3 leads.
3. **Expect to be identified before anything else.** We expect a member line to ask for the member ID on your card and your date of birth before it talks about your plan, since that's what it needs to find you. Have the card in front of you.
4. **If a voice menu won't put you through, ask for a person in a few words.** On other companies' lines today, "representative" said twice reached a person at [UPS](/blog/ups-contact-number), and "An agent, please" did at [Verizon](/blog/verizon-customer-service) after "representative" didn't. We haven't tried either at UnitedHealthcare.
5. **Hours:** uhc.com lists the Individual and Family ACA Marketplace member lines as "8 a.m. to 8 p.m. local time, Monday through Friday, in most states". Other plans' hours are on your card or plan documents.

For a claims question, also have the date of service, the provider's name and any claim number from your explanation of benefits.

## What to have ready

- **If you're a member: your member ID card.** It has the number for your plan's team, and it's the number uhc.com points members to.
- **If you're shopping: your state.** Our first question was about California, and the agent's answer depended on it.
- **Whether you've had a qualifying life event,** such as losing coverage from an employer or being denied by Medicaid, if you're calling before open enrollment starts on November 1.
- **If you're 65 or older,** press 2 instead of 1. That's the menu's Medicare option.
