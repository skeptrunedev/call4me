**The short answer:** Spectrum doesn't publish a separate retention phone number. Call **(833) 267-6094**, the number Spectrum lists for TV, Internet and Home Phone, and when the automated system asks what you need, say **"cancel my account."** It then offers a list of options. Say **"disconnect."** After a few questions about why you're leaving, it puts you in line for a **"customer solutions specialist,"** which is Spectrum's retention team. The rep on our call later called it "the retention department or customer solutions department, either way."

On Thursday, October 1, 2026, a call4me user's AI phone assistant did exactly that. The user's Spectrum internet, the 1 Gig plan, had gone from about $62 a month to $85. After a phone menu, about 5 minutes on hold and an identity check, the user joined the call, and the rep offered two deals:

| Offer | Internet price | How long | Also included |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **$55 a month** | 1 year | A free year of one unlimited Spectrum Mobile line, then $40 a month if you keep it |
| **2** | **$60 a month** | 2 years | The same free mobile year |

The user took the 1-year $55 deal. The rep said both offers come with "no contracts included at all," and confirmed the next bill would be $55 instead of $85. That's $30 a month, or $360 over the year.

The AI handled the menu, the hold, and the start of verification. Spectrum then wouldn't change the account for an assistant: it wanted the account holder. So our caller patched in the user, who finished the call. We publish the AI's part of the recording (12 minutes 45 seconds). The rest, with the user on the line, is summarized in text. ## The Spectrum retention phone number

There isn't a separate public one. Spectrum's [contact page on spectrum.net](https://www.spectrum.net/contact-us) lists **(833) 267-6094** for TV, Internet and Home Phone questions. That's the number our caller dialed, and saying "cancel" there is what reaches retention. (Spectrum's newer contact page at spectrum.com lists 833-949-0036 for support. We didn't call that one.)

## The Spectrum phone tree, step by step

All times are in the recording below.

1. **"Please tell me the 10-digit phone number associated with your account."** You can say it, or key in the phone number or your full account number. Our caller keyed in the account number.
2. **"In a few words, please describe what you need help with today."** Say **"cancel my account."**
3. The system offers four options: "reduce service" (fewer channels or a slower speed), "I'm moving," "seasonal service hold," or "disconnect" to completely disconnect TV, internet or phone. Say **"disconnect."**
4. **"Which of your services are you calling about today?"** We said "Internet."
5. **"What is the reason you want to disconnect?"** We said "The bill's gotten too high, and the service keeps cutting out."
6. **"Which provider are you considering switching to?"** We said "Verizon Fios or T-Mobile."
7. **Verification.** It offers to text a one-time passcode to a mobile number on the account, asking for the last four digits. If that number isn't on file, it asks for your **four-digit Spectrum security code**. If you have neither, stay on the line: we were sent through to a person anyway.
8. **"Please hold while we connect you with a customer solution specialist."** The queue offered a callback in "between five and seven minutes," with press 1 for a callback, 2 to schedule one, 3 to stay on hold. Our wait was about 5½ minutes of ads and music.

One oddity: the hold queue greeted us with "Thank you for calling Time Warner Cable." Spectrum's parent company, Charter, bought Time Warner Cable in 2016.

## What happened after the user joined

This part isn't published. Here is what was said:

- **Verification, again.** The rep texted a fresh code to the number on file, the user read it back, and confirmed the service address.
- **The bill history.** The rep confirmed it "got started about 62 and went to 65," and was now $85.
- **Two questions first.** Any problems with the service? (Yes, it cuts out for a few minutes "a couple times a day.") What do you use the internet for? (Working from home, Zoom calls, streaming.) Then: "I'm going to go ahead and take a look and see what I can do, so I can get that bill lowered for you," and a hold of about 2 minutes.
- **The offers.** "I found two promotions. One, the first one is good for a year, so that would drop your price to 55. And then I also have a two-year deal for 60." Both include a free year of unlimited mobile service, "no contracts included at all," and $40 a month for the line after the first year if you keep it.
- **The confirmation.** The user took the 1-year deal and asked whether the next bill, on October 10, would be $55 instead of $85. "That is correct."

## How to get a free Spectrum technician visit

The user asked about the outages. The rep offered new equipment or a technician visit, which he said normally costs "about $50, $60." Then he gave this tip: once the new pricing registers, wait 24 hours, call back, and ask for "the retention department or customer solutions department, either way, or you can just tell them that you want to cancel." Tell them the internet keeps cutting out, "and we'll send a technician out there to you free of charge."

## Who can lower a Spectrum bill?

Only the **account holder** or an **authorized user**. Spectrum's [account roles page](https://www.spectrum.net/support/account-and-billing/spectrum-account-roles-and-privileges) says authorized users can add, remove, upgrade or downgrade service, and that "Authorized Users can only be added or removed by calling (833) 267-6094." On our call, the rep stopped as soon as he learned he was talking to an assistant. If someone else in your household usually calls, add them as an authorized user first.

## How to lower your Spectrum bill: what worked

1. **Call (833) 267-6094 and say "cancel my account," then "disconnect."** That's the route to retention. You don't have to actually cancel.
2. **Have your account number, and ideally your security code.** The security code used to be printed on bills, the rep said, "but they did take that off." Without it, expect a one-time text to the phone number on the account.
3. **Be the account holder, or an authorized user.** The rep wouldn't make changes for anyone else.
4. **Give a reason and a competitor.** The menu asked for both. Ours was "the bill's gotten too high, and the service keeps cutting out," and "Verizon Fios or T-Mobile."
5. **Ask about the price history.** The rep pulled it up himself: $62, then $65, then $85.
6. **Compare the 1-year and 2-year offers.** $55 for 1 year or $60 for 2 years. If the price goes back to $85 after the first year, the 2-year deal costs less over two years: $1,440 against $1,680.
7. **Confirm the date it starts.** "My next bill... will be 55 instead of 85, right?" "That is correct."
8. **Bring up service problems after the new price is set**, through retention, for a free technician visit.

## Where our caller fell short

- **It went silent while waiting on the user.** Twice, our caller had to wait for the user to answer: once for the text code (72 seconds) and once for whether they could join (76 seconds). Both times it said nothing to the rep, and he asked, "Muse, are you still there?" We fixed this the same night: when the line goes quiet while it's waiting on you, our caller now says something like "sorry, still checking on that, one sec."
- **It said its own reasoning out loud:** "Hmm. No security code on hand. I'll say I don't have it and ask to verify another way." Also "I'll just keep listening" and "Alright, pausing for them." Fixed in the same change.
- **It answered a security question with the wrong fact.** Asked what street the account holder lived on in third grade, it gave the service address. It should have said it didn't know.
- **It gave the last four digits of the user's own phone**, which wasn't on the account, so the text code went nowhere.
- **It wasn't the account holder.** Nothing would have changed that. If you use an assistant for calls like this, add it to your plan to join the call, or add the person calling as an authorized user.

## What to say: a script that worked
