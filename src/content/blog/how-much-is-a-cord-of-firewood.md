**The short answer:** a full cord of firewood (128 cubic feet, stacked) cost **$265 to $899 delivered** in the three markets we priced on November 27, 2026. Near Truckee, California, local pine and lodgepole ran **$265 to $340** delivered, and oak or almond trucked in from elsewhere in California ran **$550 to $650** (one online marketplace lists $740 before its delivery fee). Around Wellesley, Massachusetts, a full cord ran **$655 to $874** delivered where the seller posts its delivery fee, and kiln-dried, the only kind two sellers had left when we called, cost **$825 to $874**. In Wayzata and Orono, Minnesota, the one seller that posts a full cord price charges **$849 for oak and $899 for birch** delivered, and face cord prices there work out to about **$897 a cord**.

These are three markets, not a national average. We found every firewood seller that delivers to each one ourselves, from many sources, and 48 businesses qualified. Only 21 of them post a price online at all. We converted every price we could to the same unit, a delivered full cord, and re-checked each one on the seller's own page on November 27, 2026. Our AI caller also called a few sellers on Black Friday, and two in the Wellesley area gave us their delivered price on the phone.

## What a full cord is, in one paragraph

A **full cord** is a stack 4 feet high, 4 feet deep and 8 feet long: 128 cubic feet of wood and air. A **face cord** is one row 4 feet high and 8 feet long, as deep as the pieces are long; with 16-inch pieces that's about 42.7 cubic feet, a third of a cord. A **loose-thrown** cord is measured in the truck, not in a stack, so it takes more cubic feet to hold the same wood. We converted face cords only when the seller gave the piece length, and pallets only when the seller gave the pallet's size. For more on the units real sellers use, see [face cord vs full cord](/blog/face-cord-vs-full-cord).

## How to buy a cord without overpaying

Everything here comes from the prices above and what the sellers said.

1. **Compare per 128 cubic feet, stacked.** A "face cord," a pallet, a bin and a loose-thrown load are all different amounts. Divide the price by the fraction of a cord you're getting. Spikes & Houles' $399.99 "face cord" pallet is about $1,463 a cord; Espeseth's $299 face cord is $897.
2. **Ask how many cubic feet, stacked.** Beech Hill's page doesn't say, and BostonFirewood.com's owner said "a hundred and twenty" stacked, then "more than that." If a seller can't give you a number, measure the stack when it lands: 4 x 4 x 8 feet.
3. **Buy the full cord if you can store it.** Two half cords cost a median of 18% more, and often two delivery fees.
4. **Add the delivery and stacking before you compare.** Minuteman's $775 cord is $874 delivered to Wellesley; Beech Hill's $825 cord is $825. Stacking runs from free to $250 a cord.
5. **In late November, expect to pay kiln-dried prices.** Two Wellesley-area sellers had only kiln-dried on Black Friday, and Gary's and Kosich had sold-out lines. Next year, order seasoned wood early.
6. **In Truckee, decide between local softwood and hauled-in hardwood.** Local pine and lodgepole cost about half as much as oak or almond. Cross Check doesn't deliver during storm cycles, and deliveries from December through April depend on snow, so order before the big storms.
7. **Call the sellers who don't post a price.** 27 of the 48 businesses that deliver to these towns post none.

- **We found the sellers ourselves.** For each market we used web search, sellers' own sites, Yelp, Nextdoor, BBB, Facebook, Yellow Pages, Angi, firewood directories such as Firewood Scout and Firewood Atlas, Minnesota's list of certified firewood producers, Reddit and local magazines, not one directory. The rest mostly didn't deliver there as far as we could find, had closed or showed no sign of selling this season, or sold something else (bags, compressed logs, logs you cut yourself). Each town post has the breakdown. Private sellers on Craigslist or Marketplace aren't counted or named.
- **Every price is the seller's own.** We used only the price on the seller's own site, store page or listing, and re-checked each one on November 27, 2026. Where a page wouldn't load for our re-check (Firewood 'N More's full cord page, and the Mulch Mound, Wilson Farm and OneLogFire shops), we say so next to the price. Prices from earlier years are shown and labeled, not counted.
- **The phone only filled gaps.** Our caller rang two firewood sellers per market whose answers weren't online. Only BostonFirewood.com's price came from the phone alone; Beech Hill's phone price matched its site.
- **Conversions.** Price per full cord = price divided by the fraction of a cord, using the seller's own fraction (half cord, 1/3 cord, 1/6 cord bin) or measurements (16-inch face cord, pallet size). We didn't convert loose-thrown loads or face cords with no piece length, because a stacked amount can't be worked out from them.
- **What's not included.** Sales tax, and any delivery fee a seller doesn't post.
