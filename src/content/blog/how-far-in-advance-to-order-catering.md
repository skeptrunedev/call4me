**The short answer:** for a small pickup order, one to three days of notice was enough at every bakery and restaurant that answered, and a ready-made ice cream cake can be a same-day errand. On Tuesday, November 24, 2026, two days before Thanksgiving, a **Hy-Vee bakery in Olathe, Kansas took an order for 5 dozen fall-themed cupcakes for pickup the next day, at $14.99 a dozen, paid at pickup**. That same morning, two restaurants in Tustin, California said a catering tray order placed that day or the next could be ready for pickup that Friday. A Little Rock restaurant said yes to a group pickup order two days out, and a Baskin-Robbins in Brooklyn had cookies and cream ice cream cakes in stock the afternoon it was asked. The thing that fills up is a table: 16 days before Thanksgiving, a Melbourne, Florida restaurant said its Thanksgiving brunch reservations were already "pretty full."

These calls cover small orders: a few dozen cupcakes, trays for a group pickup, one cake, a holiday table. A wedding or a full-service party for a hundred people is a different kind of order, and none of these calls tested one.

What each errand took:

1. **Hy-Vee bakery, Olathe:** one call to place the cupcake order for the next day, and a second call right after to cut it to 4 dozen. Pay when you pick up, no deposit and no card.
2. **Baskin-Robbins, Brooklyn:** one shop never picked up, the other hung up on the first try, and the second try got sizes, prices and "Yeah, no problem" to holding a cake for later that day.
3. **Catering trays in Tustin:** a late-night voicemail, a catering line that went to call screening, and then two restaurants that answered: India Kitchen ($60 trays that serve about 8) and EverSpring Modern Chinese (one tray size, serving about 4 to 5).
4. **Group pickup in Little Rock:** Bheemas Indian Kitchen & Bar said "Yes, we can do it" to a group order two days out, and asked whether we wanted a half tray or a full tray.
5. **Thanksgiving in Florida:** three restaurants gave their Thanksgiving hours, one listed its holiday menu (no regular menu that day), and the same one booked a Thanksgiving table with no card in a two-minute call.

All 16 calls were made by five call4me users' AI assistants, to businesses in Brooklyn, New York; Olathe, Kansas; Tustin, California; Little Rock, Arkansas; and Melbourne, Palm Bay and Indian Harbour Beach on Florida's Space Coast. Our caller didn't pay for anything or give a card on any of them. Names and phone numbers are muted in the audio and shown in brackets in the transcripts. Prices, menus and availability are what each business said on the day of the call, and they can change any day.

## Hy-Vee bakery cupcakes: $14.99 a dozen, ordered the day before

The store is a Hy-Vee in Olathe, Kansas. The phone menu offers "For bakery, press five," and the bakery picked up 23 seconds into the call.

Our caller asked for 50 fall-themed cupcakes, half chocolate and half vanilla, for pickup the next day. The bakery's first answer covered the two things to know before a Hy-Vee bakery order:

- **Lead time:** one day was enough. The bakery said yes for the next day.
- **Quantity:** "we do them by the dozen or half dozen." 50 isn't an option, so our caller asked for 5 dozen, 30 chocolate and 30 vanilla, and got "Yeah, whichever" for 4 or 5 dozen.

Then the associate asked whether the frosting should be buttercream or whipped icing (our caller said buttercream, "with fall colors, like leaves, pumpkins, orange and brown frosting"), a name for the order, and a phone number. "All right, we'll have them ready for you."

The price came only when our caller asked: "They're fourteen ninety nine a dozen," which our caller worked out to $74.95 before tax for 5 dozen. Asked whether the user could pay at pickup: "Yeah, you pay when you pick up." No deposit, no card, and no order number. The order sat under the user's name and a callback number.

Right after that call, a second call changed the order. A different associate in the bakery took it: "Okay. So two dozen white, two dozen chocolate." "All right, I got it." The associate updated the callback number and gave the new total: "it's still fourteen ninety nine a dozen, so fifteen times four, which is what, sixty." Exactly: 4 dozen at $14.99 is $59.96 before tax. The two calls took 4 minutes 5 seconds and cost $1.25 at call4me's 25 cents a minute.

Hy-Vee's own page for this store lists it as "Thanksgiving, closed" and "Christmas, closed," with bakery hours of 6 a.m. to 7 p.m. At this store, the Wednesday before Thanksgiving was the last day to pick up, and December 24 is the last day before Christmas.

There's no recording of the bakery calls here: the audio includes the pickup day and time, and we don't publish when a user will be somewhere.

## Baskin-Robbins ice cream cake the same day: sizes, prices and a hold

If the holiday dessert or the party cake is the thing you forgot, an ice cream cake from the freezer can be a same-day errand. On Tuesday, October 20, a call4me user's assistant called two Baskin-Robbins shops in Brooklyn a little before 2:30 pm, looking for a cookies and cream ice cream cake to pick up that day.

- **One Baskin-Robbins didn't pick up, twice.** Both calls got an automated message, first that the voicemail wasn't taking messages, then that the voicemail box was full.
- **A second Baskin-Robbins picked up and hung up.** On the first try, someone said "Hello" and the call ended 19 seconds in.
- **The second try, a minute later, got everything.** In stock today? "Yeah, of course," then "We do." The sizes: "we have nine round, half sheet, one third sheet." The 9-inch round: "Fifty four ninety nine." The half sheet: "Half-sheet one is forty four something." Could they hold one for pickup later that day? "Yeah, no problem." Until what time? "we are open 24 hours."

Our caller only asked whether the shop could hold a cake; it didn't actually put one on hold. A half sheet quoted below a 9-inch round sounds backwards. Ask how many people each size serves before you pick one, and get the price again at the counter. Our caller didn't ask the price of the one-third sheet.

What [Baskin-Robbins' FAQ](https://www.baskinrobbins.com/en/faqs) says: "Our fully customized cakes take at least 24 hours to create," and "Need one sooner? Call or visit your favorite shop and choose from a variety of cakes available for immediate pick-up." That matches the call. A custom cake needs at least a day, but a shop can have ready-made cakes the same day, and calling is how you find out which flavors and sizes it has. For more on setting something aside by phone, see [asking a store to hold an item](/blog/ask-a-store-to-hold-an-item).

## Thanksgiving dinner at a restaurant: hours, menus and booking

On Tuesday, November 10, 16 days before Thanksgiving, a call4me user's assistant asked three restaurants on Florida's Space Coast whether they'd be open on Thanksgiving and what their lunch hours would be.

- **The Shack Riverfront, Palm Bay:** "Yes, we're open from eleven to eight on Thanksgiving."
- **Charlie & Jake's Barbecue, Indian Harbour Beach:** "We are open on Thanksgiving Day and we open at 7 a.m." After checking: "we close at 11 p.m."
- **Frigate's Waterfront Bar & Grill, Melbourne:** "We're doing a Thanksgiving brunch," and "it's from 9 AM, and our last seating's at 3. However, reservations are pretty full. So your best bet to get a reservation would probably be around 9 AM to 10."

A few minutes later, our caller called The Shack back to ask what it would serve. The person who answered said, "Could you give me just a moment there? I'm in the middle of dinner," and came back about 3 minutes later with the list: "we have turkey, we have ham, we have shrimp alfredo, parm crusted snapper, lobster mac and cheese, filet, ribeye, prime rib, grouper and mahi. All served with our unlimited salad bar." Would the regular menu also be available? "No." It's the Thanksgiving menu only.

That evening, a third call to The Shack booked a Thanksgiving table. After about a minute of waiting, the restaurant asked for the time, how many people, a phone number, a first name and a last name, then: "Alright, reservation is made." No card, no deposit and no confirmation number.

What The Shack's own site says, as of November 25: its [Thanksgiving events listing](https://www.theshackpb.com/events/) gives Thanksgiving reservations from 11 AM to 8 PM, with the restaurant open 11 AM to 11 PM, and prices for the same entrees: turkey, ham, shrimp Alfredo and snapper from $39.95, lobster mac and cheese and prime rib at $49.95, and filet, ribeye, grouper or mahi at $59.95, with the salad bar and an ice cream sundae bar included. The same listing says "Reservations for parties of four or more." Nobody mentioned a minimum party size on the phone. If a holiday listing has a party-size rule that doesn't fit you, call and ask anyway.

## Where our caller fell short

- **It asked a two-part allergy question and got a one-word answer.** At India Kitchen it asked, "is the Gobi Manchurian dairy-free? No butter or cream in it?" and the answer was "No," which fits either reading. A quick "no" on the phone isn't an allergen guarantee anyway. Ask one yes or no question at a time ("Is there any butter or cream in the Gobi Manchurian?") and repeat the allergy when you place the order.
- **At EverSpring, it started reading a list of dishes to check for peanuts.** The answer was "if you have any allergy, just let us know," then "Are you AI?" Our caller said yes, and the call ended a few seconds later without an answer on peanuts.
- **At the second Baskin-Robbins, it asked before anyone was clearly on the line.** Its question went out in the first seconds of the call, the shop said "Hello" about 14 seconds later, and then hung up. The call right after, which waited for a person, got every answer.
- **It called The Shack in the middle of dinner service.** Calling at 6:18 pm on a weeknight meant about 3 minutes on hold. Mid-afternoon is a better time to ask a restaurant about its holiday menu.
- **Its first Shack call said "Thanksgiving Day next year."** It meant this year, and said "Thursday, November twenty-sixth" in the same sentence, so the answer was still right. Give the full date.

## Tips for ordering holiday food by phone before Christmas

Built from what these calls showed:

1. **Small pickup orders don't need weeks.** The Hy-Vee bakery took 5 dozen cupcakes for the next day, and three restaurants said yes to tray orders 2 to 3 days out. If you're past the date you meant to order by, call anyway.
2. **A ready-made cake can be same day.** A Baskin-Robbins in Brooklyn had cookies and cream ice cream cakes in stock that afternoon and said it could hold one. A custom cake is different: Baskin-Robbins says those take at least 24 hours.
3. **Tables go first.** 16 days before Thanksgiving, one brunch was "pretty full" with the best chance at 9 to 10 am. The Shack's own listing says "don't wait until Thanksgiving week to reserve your table!" Book a sit-down holiday meal two weeks out or more.
4. **Check holiday closures.** The Olathe Hy-Vee lists itself as closed on Thanksgiving and on Christmas, so the order has to be for the day before or earlier.
5. **Order in the units they sell.** Hy-Vee does cupcakes "by the dozen or half dozen." Bheemas asked half tray or full tray. EverSpring has one tray size. Baskin-Robbins had a 9-inch round, a half sheet and a one-third sheet.
6. **Ask how many people one tray or one cake serves.** Trays served about 8 at India Kitchen and about 4 to 5 at EverSpring, and a half sheet cake was quoted below a 9-inch round.
7. **Know your frosting.** Hy-Vee asked buttercream or whipped icing before it took the order.
8. **Ask for the price.** Nobody volunteered it. "What's the total?" got "fourteen ninety nine a dozen," and you can do the math before tax.
9. **Ask to pay at pickup.** At Hy-Vee it was "Yeah, you pay when you pick up." Neither the bakery order nor the Thanksgiving booking asked for a card or a deposit.
10. **Ask whether the regular menu is available.** On Thanksgiving, The Shack serves its holiday menu only.
11. **Changing an order is a short call.** The Hy-Vee change took 99 seconds, right after the first call.
12. **Use the main line during open hours, and call more than one place.** EverSpring's late-night voicemail and its separate catering number both missed, and its main line answered at 11:30 am. One Baskin-Robbins never picked up, so the second shop was the one that mattered.

## What to say: a script that worked

For a bakery order:

> Hi, could I order [number] [item], [flavors], for pickup [day and date]? What's the earliest pickup time, what's the price, and can I pay at pickup?

For a same-day cake:

> Hi, do you have a [flavor] ice cream cake in stock today? What sizes do you have, and how much is each? Could you hold one for pickup later today?

For catering trays:

> Hi, if I placed a catering order today or tomorrow, could you fill it for [time] pickup on [day and date]? How many people does one tray serve, and is there a larger size? What's the price for a tray of [dish]?

For a holiday table: "Are you open on [holiday], [full date]? Is it a special menu, or is the regular menu available too? Could I book a table for [number] at [time]?"
