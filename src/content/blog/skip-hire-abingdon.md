## Cheapest skip hire near Oxford for rubble: what the calls showed

**For a 4 yard skip this week, Collard was the strongest answer.** At £263 plus VAT it was within a few pounds of Oxford Skip Hire's £260 plus VAT, came with two weeks' hire, and was the only 4 yard quote with a possible next-day delivery. Collard didn't give a tonnage. The answer was that both sizes can be filled with hardcore "but the skip does need to be level with the sides", with "no wet concrete".

**For a 6 yard skip, Mick's Skips was the cheapest stated VAT-inclusive price** at £330, with 14 days and tomorrow or Wednesday delivery. Collard's £295 plus VAT works out at £354, so Mick's is about £24 cheaper if both are accurate. Mick's would not give a weight: "that doesn't matter, you can only level fill the skip."

**If you can wait a week, Cotswold Skips' 4 yard at £260 was the lowest all-in figure.** On the follow-up call, the assistant asked whether £260 was "the final price, including VAT, delivery, collection, and hire". The answer was "Yes". It comes with seven days' hire and "roughly around about the four ton mark". Cotswold only does mini skips, 2 and 4 yard.

**M&M Waste Solutions was the most expensive, but the most specific.** It gave VAT-inclusive totals unprompted, tonnage for both sizes, and the longest hire periods: three weeks for a 4 yard and six weeks for a 6 yard.

**Grundon didn't quote.** It said its smallest skip is a 6 yard, that new customers need an account because "we're not doing sort of like cash skips at the moment", and that the price would come as a formal quote by email.

## 4 yard skip hire or 6 yard for bricks and concrete

Heavy rubble is the reason firms talk about fill level rather than volume. Every firm that answered said the same thing in different words: **fill it level, not heaped.** M&M said you "could just fill it up as long as it's level loaded". Collard: "level with the sides". Mick's: "can only be level filled". B&E said there's no overweight charge on a 3 or 6 yard, "but obviously if the lorry can't pick it up, it's too heavy".

The tonnage answers for a full level load of rubble were all estimates:

- 4 yard: about 3 tonnes (M&M), about 4 tonnes (Cotswold)
- 6 yard: about 4 tonnes (M&M), about 6 tonnes "rough guess" (B&E)

The two 4 yard answers and the two 6 yard answers don't agree. If your load is close to the limit, ask the firm you book with to write its limit on the booking.

Mick's also listed what can't go in its skips: "plaster board, fridges or deep freezers, mattresses, chairs with upholstery on."

## How the assistant handled the calls

The brief was the same for every firm, and that's what made the answers comparable: one waste type (clean bricks and concrete only), one placement (a private driveway), two sizes, and a fixed list of questions. A few things to know about our side:

- **When a call dropped, it called back** and asked only what was missing. The Cotswold follow-up took 1 minute 26 seconds.
- **It had no email address to give**, so Grundon's formal quote never happened. If you want quotes from firms that only send them by email, give your assistant an address it may share.
- **It opened the Oxford Skip Hire call by saying it was "following up"** (the transcriptions differ on the exact words), although no earlier conversation is in this set.
- **It didn't always push for a number.** Collard's and Mick's weight answers were about fill level, and the assistant accepted them without asking again for tonnes.
