**The short answer:** the way that lowered a Spectrum bill on our recorded call was to **call 833-267-6094 and ask for retention**. Spectrum doesn't publish a retention number, so at the voice menu say **"cancel my account,"** then **"disconnect."** That puts you in line for a "customer solutions specialist," Spectrum's retention team. You don't have to cancel anything. On October 1, 2026, that call took a call4me user's internet bill from **$85 a month to $55 a month for a year**, a $30 a month cut, with no contract.

What got the bill down, in order:

1. **Ask for retention, not billing.** Say "cancel my account," then "disconnect," and answer the menu's questions about which service and why.
2. **Give a reason and a competitor.** The menu asks for both. Ours: "The bill's gotten too high, and the service keeps cutting out," and "Verizon Fios or T-Mobile."
3. **Be the account holder, or an authorized user.** The rep wouldn't make changes for anyone else.
4. **Let the rep see the price history.** He pulled it up himself: it "got started about 62 and went to 65," then $85.
5. **Ask for both offers.** The rep found two: "One, the first one is good for a year, so that would drop your price to 55. And then I also have a two-year deal for 60." Both came with a free year of one unlimited Spectrum Mobile line, then $40 a month if you keep it, and "no contracts included at all."
6. **Confirm the new price on your next bill.** "My next bill... will be 55 instead of 85, right?" "That is correct."

Prices are what Spectrum offered this account on that night. Offers vary by account and area.

## Where to call

Spectrum's [contact page on spectrum.net](https://www.spectrum.net/contact-us) lists **(833) 267-6094** for TV, Internet and Home Phone. That's the number our caller dialed. Spectrum's [contact page at spectrum.com](https://www.spectrum.com/contact-us) also lists **888-369-2408** for Spectrum Internet customer service, "available 24/7," and **833-949-0036** for account and billing questions. We only called 833-267-6094, so we can't say whether saying "cancel" reaches retention the same way on the others. The full phone tree, step by step, is in our [Spectrum retention department guide](/blog/spectrum-retention-department).

## What happened on our call, with timings

- **0:00 to 2:21:** the voice menu. Account number, then "cancel my account," then "disconnect," then which service (internet), why, and which provider we were considering.
- **2:21:** "Please hold while we connect you with a customer solution specialist." The queue offered a callback in "between five and seven minutes."
- **7:49:** a rep answered. About 5½ minutes of hold.
- **8:41 to 10:59:** verification. He asked for the four-digit security code, then a security question, then texted a code to the phone on file.
- **11:07:** "Unfortunately, we wouldn't be able to make any changes on the account." He'd learned he was talking to an assistant, and he needed the account holder.
- **12:36:** our caller rang the user, who joined the call. The user verified again, the rep put them on a hold of about 2 minutes, and came back with the two offers.

## What to have ready

- **Your Spectrum account number**, or the phone number on the account. The menu asks for one first.
- **Your four-digit Spectrum security code**, if you have one. The rep said it used to be printed on bills, "but they did take that off." Without it, expect a one-time text to the phone number on the account.
- **The phone on the account, in your hand,** for that text code.
- **Your old price, your current price, and a competitor's price.**

Spectrum's [account roles page](https://www.spectrum.net/support/account-and-billing/spectrum-account-roles-and-privileges) says authorized users can add, remove, upgrade or downgrade service, and that "Authorized Users can only be added or removed by calling (833) 267-6094." If someone else in your home usually makes these calls, add them first.

## What to say: a script

> Hi, I'm the account holder. The internet bill has gone from [old price] to [new price] a month, and the service keeps cutting out. I'm looking at [competitor and price]. Is there a promotion you can put me on for the same speed? What would the 1-year and 2-year offers be?

**Which offer to take.** If the price goes back to $85 after a year, the 2-year $60 deal costs less over two years: $1,440 against $1,680 for $55 then $85. The user on our call took the 1-year deal.

**One more tip from the rep.** If your service keeps dropping, once the new price is set, wait 24 hours, call back, and ask for "the retention department or customer solutions department, either way, or you can just tell them that you want to cancel." Tell them about the outages, "and we'll send a technician out there to you free of charge." He said a visit normally costs "about $50, $60."
