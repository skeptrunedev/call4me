For a **Mandarin phone call about scheduling**, tell your assistant which language to use, the meeting date and time, and whether it may agree to a change. If the participants are in different cities, include both local times in the brief.

On October 6, 2026, a call4me customer's AI assistant spoke Mandarin to check whether a meeting still worked that day. The proposed time was **1:30 PM in Houston, corresponding to 11:30 AM in Los Angeles** on that date.

The recipient said the original time was inconvenient and suggested **Saturday**. The assistant said it would pass that suggestion along and have the customer confirm the details. **No replacement time was agreed.** The useful result was an availability answer and a proposed day for a later conversation.

## An availability check needs a different finish from a booking

The customer's instruction was to gather information. When the original time did not work, the assistant asked about an alternative, recorded the proposed day and left confirmation to the customer.

That distinction belongs in the result returned to your assistant. “Saturday suggested, exact time unconfirmed” gives you something useful to act on. “Meeting moved to Saturday” would overstate what the conversation established.

The call also demonstrates one concrete Mandarin exchange. It does not establish performance for every dialect, language, accent or type of scheduling conversation.

## How to brief a call across languages and time zones

1. Name the language you want used throughout the call.
2. Give the exact date and the relevant local times.
3. Explain whether the task is to check availability or confirm a booking.
4. State which alternative days and hours are acceptable if changes are authorized.
5. Ask for the person's answer and any unresolved details in the language you prefer for your summary.

If a recipient suggests only a day, keep the exact hour open in your calendar until someone confirms it. The same applies if the conversation leaves a time zone unclear.
