**The short answer:** an out-the-door price is the total you pay to drive the car home: the selling price plus sales tax, title, registration, and every dealer fee and add-on. Ask for it by that name, ask for it itemized and in writing, and ask how long it's good for. That's what two call4me users' AI phone assistants did when they called 10 car dealers in October and November 2026. A **San Francisco Toyota** salesperson said a **$46,530 out-the-door price** on a 2026 RAV4 Plug-in Hybrid SE would still be honored the day after the written offer expired, "As long as it's available," and that the **$500 hold deposit** "is fully refundable if he does not buy it." In Texas, **Car Place Dallas** called its "Elite Protection" package "just as an option" on a BMW M3 Competition xDrive, and came back with **$102,000 out the door** against a $100,000 ask.

If you're chasing a Black Friday or year-end car deal, these are the questions that turn a quote into a number a dealer will stand behind.

What we learned, in short:

1. **A price isn't an out-the-door price.** Toyota Walnut Creek confirmed a $41,556 price that included "the doc and e-filing fee," and our caller still had to ask for the itemized out-the-door total, with registration and no add-ons.
2. **A written offer can outlive its deadline.** San Francisco Toyota's write-up said the offer "expires today." The salesperson still said yes to the next day, as long as the car was still there.
3. **Add-ons come off if you ask. Fees built into the price don't.** Car Place Dallas said its Elite Protection is "not... forced on anybody," but its $999 reconditioning charge is "part of my sales price."
4. **Make sure the car is there and not spoken for.** A listed M3 at Principle BMW of San Antonio was "a customer order," still "on the water." At Sewell BMW in Midland, the M3 "just arrived today."
5. **Dealers want a name and a phone number before they'll talk.** One BMW dealer in Houston wouldn't check a car without one: "I just, um, need to pull a profile."

These 17 calls were made by two call4me users: one in the Bay Area, shopping for a 2026 Toyota RAV4 Plug-in Hybrid SE in late October, and one in Texas, shopping for a BMW M3 Competition xDrive in November. Our caller didn't buy a car, sign anything or give a card on any of them. VINs, stock numbers and salespeople's names are left out, and in the two recordings the user's name, our caller's name and the day of a planned visit are muted and shown in brackets in the transcripts. Prices and availability are what each dealer said that day, and they can change any day.

## What an out-the-door price includes

Everything you pay to leave with the car: the selling price, sales tax, title and registration, the dealer's own fees, and any add-ons. Anything less is a price, not an out-the-door number. Our caller's version at Car Place Dallas was "total out the door including all the Texas tax, title, registration, and any dealer fees, with no protection packages or add-ons."

Two calls show why the words matter:

- **Toyota Walnut Creek** was asked whether $41,556 was the right price, "and does that include the doc and e-filing fee?" The salesperson: "It does." That covers the dealer's paperwork fees, not the total. Our caller then asked for "an itemized out-the-door total" with registration and no add-ons, and the salesperson said, "I am doing that as we speak." That email isn't in our call records, so we don't know the final number.
- **San Francisco Toyota** had already put an out-the-door number in writing: "the out-the-door forty-six five-thirty," in a write-up the salesperson emailed. That's the number to hold a dealer to.

Some fees hide inside the selling price. Car Place Dallas's own listings show it: each car has an "Original price," then "Recon $999" and "Doc Fee $225," then a "Sale price" $1,224 higher ([carplacedallas.com](https://carplacedallas.com/)). When our caller asked about removing "that nine hundred and ninety nine reconditioning charge," the salesperson said, "No, so it's-it's part of my sales price."

## Will a dealer honor an out-the-door price? San Francisco Toyota said yes

At least this one did, for one more day. The salesperson had emailed the user a write-up with a $46,530 out-the-door price on a 2026 RAV4 Plug-in Hybrid SE. Our caller pointed out, "Your write-up says the offer expires today," and asked whether the price would still be honored the next day. "Um, yes," and then: "As long as it's available."

So the price would hold, but nothing held the car: someone else could still buy it in the meantime. The dealer's way to hold it was a $500 deposit, and the salesperson said, "It is fully refundable if he does not buy it." The deposit wasn't placed on this call, and the salesperson hadn't cleared it with a manager yet: "I have not asked my boss because you guys have not told me..." Asked to put the refund terms in writing: "Of course I can."

Asked whether it could hold the car, Toyota Walnut Creek described a different kind of hold: "We could hold it if uhh I had a phone conversation with him and I was able to uh have him upload a credit application, driver's license, and current insurance." No hold was placed at either dealer.

## Add-ons, reconditioning fees and the "best number"

Car Place Dallas is an independent used-car dealer in Addison (its site calls it "a trusted used luxury car dealership in Addison, TX"), and the 2026 M3 Competition xDrive the Texas user was after came with a $999 reconditioning charge. Over two calls one afternoon and a voicemail the next morning, here's how the number moved:

1. **The add-on came off on request.** "The beautiful thing is I don't have any dealer add ons, but I do have an Elite Protection that covers your wheels, key fobs, windshields, paintless dent repair, interior and exterior." Asked to remove it entirely: "Yeah, yeah, absolutely. This is just as an option. It's as an option. It's not... forced on anybody."
2. **The reconditioning charge didn't.** "It's not an additional, it's it-it's part of my sales price."
3. **So our caller asked for the difference somewhere else.** On the next call: "If a nine ninety nine recon fee has to stay listed, that's fine, but the difference should come off the selling price." And: "If not, what's your best firm number?"
4. **The best number was $102,000.** "Uh at the moment the best number I have is at one-o-two. Um so if if I can get anything different then I will send the breakdown to you." Asked whether that number assumed outside financing or the dealer's financing: "Whichever, whichever option you decide to go with." It was already itemized in writing: "I already sent an image with the itemized breakdown."
5. **The manager wanted the buyer on the phone.** "I think at this moment my GSM would like to just speak uh with your buyer directly uh in regards to a couple of things just to make sure everything's good. So if you can make that happen, then of course we are here to accommodate and earn your business." (A GSM is a general sales manager.) Our caller said the buyer was "not available to speak directly right now, but they are ready to review your written email."
6. **The user countered by email.** The next morning, our caller left a voicemail asking for "a quick yes or no on his emailed offer of one o' one two fifty total out the door, inclusive of all Texas taxes and fees, no add-ons." Our call records end there, so we don't know whether the dealer took $101,250.

On taxes, the salesperson's warning: "here in Texas, we have very low tax rate on vehicles. However, taxes you're gonna be looking at a hefty amount on that dollar value." That's why our caller negotiated the out-the-door number, not the selling price.

## Make sure the car is really there, and really for sale

A car can be online and still not be for sale:

- **Principle BMW of San Antonio** looked up the listed 2027 M3 and said, "This may be an order, but let me double check." It was: "It looks like this one's not here. It's on the water. It's expected to arrive in Galveston on the 29th, um, and get here sometime in December. It is a customer order. Looks like someone ordered this car."
- **Sewell BMW of the Permian Basin** checked "to see if anyone has claimed it just yet," then: "I don't see that it has been claimed on my side, but let me just double check with a manager." After about four minutes of hold and checking: "It's available. How soon can you be here." And: "It's here. It just arrived today."
- **Toyota Walnut Creek**, asked "Is it physically on the lot right now?": "Currently, yes."

Two more things worth asking about the specific car:

- **The options.** Our caller asked Sewell to confirm a list of packages, including carbon bucket seats. The salesperson read the build and caught one: "if it has the front ventilated seats, it will not have the carbon bucket seats."
- **Open recalls.** Our caller asked Toyota Walnut Creek whether "that safety recall twenty-six V six five nine, the hybrid ECU software update" had been done on that RAV4. "It is not, because it didn't qualify," the salesperson said, because it covered cars built "between uh July and August," and "This vehicle was produced in the month of September." NHTSA's record for recall 26V659 covers "certain 2026 RAV4 Plug-In Hybrid vehicles," with dealers updating the software "free of charge," but it doesn't list build dates, so we can't confirm that range. You can check any car's VIN at [nhtsa.gov/recalls](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls).

And ask about incentives. Sewell's answer on the M3: "because it's an M3 competition there's no rebates or incentives. It's just the highest end car." Then: "if they either have a BMW currently in their name, um- or are active or prior military. Uh- there might be an opportunity for additional rebates. I just have to verify if it's on the M3 specifically."

## Dealers want a name and a phone number first

- **One BMW dealer in Houston** answered with "I would be happy to look that up for you," then wanted a phone number "Just in case we get disconnected." Our caller said, "I'd rather not provide a phone number. Could you check it by the VIN or stock number?" The answer: "I just, um, need to pull a profile and if you could give me your phone number, that'd be great. I mean, I have, um, most of the digits, what looks like on caller ID, um, like eventually we'll have to share information I would assume." Our caller ended the call without an answer. That dealer's greeting also said the call "is being recorded and processed by a third-party AI service we use."
- **Car Place Dallas** asked "what's your phone number?" too. Our caller said "I don't need a callback, I'm just after the sales team's email address," and got it. From then on, the quotes went to email.
- **Sewell BMW** asked, "are you a broker?" Our caller said, "I'm an assistant gathering info for a shopper," and the salesperson went ahead.

The phone system remembered, too. By that evening, Car Place Dallas's phone system offered to connect our caller straight to the user's "sales representative." The next morning it greeted the call with the user's first name.

## What didn't get through

- **Phone menus.** The second BMW dealer in Houston played the same greeting for about five and a half minutes: "Please say sales, service, parts, finance, directory, or reception. You may also continue to hold for the next available receptionist." No receptionist came. BMW of Arlington's voice menu heard our caller say "Sales" three times and played the greeting again each time.
- **Sales queues.** At SouthFork Chrysler Dodge Jeep Ram, our caller sat in the queue for about 6 minutes without an answer. On the second try, the operator transferred it, and that line went to voicemail.
- **Direct extensions.** City Toyota's salesperson extension said only that the person "is unavailable." A follow-up call to Toyota Walnut Creek got the salesperson's voicemail.
- **The main line.** San Francisco Toyota's sales line couldn't check the car on the spot: "Can I check on this and call you back in a few minutes." Nine minutes later, our caller called the salesperson who had sent the written offer and got answers on the spot.

## Where our caller fell short

- **It asked for things it had already been told.** At Toyota Walnut Creek, after the salesperson had answered the price, lot and recall questions, our caller asked for all of it again in the email. "Uh, we just covered all of that. The vehicle is on the lot. The price is correct." Ask once, then ask for the email.
- **It didn't say "sales" to a voice menu.** At the second Houston BMW dealer, the greeting asked callers to say a department, and our caller just waited through five and a half minutes of it.
- **It lost a call.** The first Car Place Dallas call that day ended 24 seconds in, after our caller asked, "can you hear me okay?" It dialed again right away, and that one worked.

## How to get an out-the-door price before the end of the year

Built only from what these calls showed:

1. **Call the salesperson who sent you the quote, not the main line.** San Francisco Toyota's sales line said it would call back. The salesperson who sent the offer answered right away.
2. **Say "out the door" and say what's in it:** all the tax, title, registration and dealer fees, with no protection packages or add-ons.
3. **Ask for it itemized and in writing.** Toyota Walnut Creek was emailing "an itemized out-the-door total." Car Place Dallas "sent an image with the itemized breakdown."
4. **If the offer has a deadline, ask whether they'll honor it past that.** San Francisco Toyota said yes for the next day, "As long as it's available."
5. **If there's a hold deposit, ask if it's refundable and get that in the email.** "It is fully refundable if he does not buy it," and "Of course I can" to putting it in writing.
6. **Ask which line items are optional.** Elite Protection came off. The $999 reconditioning charge didn't.
7. **If a fee won't come off, ask for the same amount off the selling price,** then ask for their best firm number and whether it depends on financing.
8. **Confirm the car:** on the lot, not claimed, not a customer order. Ask them to read the options off the car you care about, and ask about open recalls.
9. **Ask about rebates and incentives.** On an M3 Competition there were none, but owner loyalty or military status "might" apply.
10. **Decide what contact details you'll give before you call.** One dealer wouldn't look anything up without a phone number. Another was happy to work by email.

## What to say: a script that worked

This is close to what our caller said at Car Place Dallas and San Francisco Toyota:

> Hi, I'm calling about the [year, model and trim] you [emailed me about / have listed]. Is it physically on the lot and unreserved? Could you get to [price] total out the door, including all the [state] tax, title, registration and any dealer fees, with no protection packages or add-ons? If not, what's your best firm number? Please send it itemized by email, and let me know how long that price will be honored.

If they mention a deposit: "Can you confirm it's fully refundable if I don't buy, and put that in the email?"
