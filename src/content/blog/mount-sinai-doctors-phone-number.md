**The short answer:** the Mount Sinai Doctors practice at **104 Delancey Street, New York, NY 10002** is **212-677-2157**. That is the number Mount Sinai's own [104 Delancey Street location page](https://www.mountsinai.org/locations/msd-104-delancey) lists, and the one dialed on all three calls below. The same page lists the central line **1-800-MD-SINAI**, fax **212-982-2792**, and office hours of **8:00am to 8:00pm Monday to Friday** and **9:00am to 5:00pm Saturday**. Nobody dialed the central line on these calls.

The path to a person on these calls:

1. The greeting, a Spanish option (9) and an emergency notice play.
2. At the main menu, **press 1** ("To schedule, reschedule, or cancel an appointment").
3. At the next menu, **press 2** ("to connect with a representative for personalized assistance with your appointments"). Press 1 there is the self-service option.
4. An automated system asks for the **patient's date of birth**, then the **phone number on the medical record**, and sometimes the **five-digit ZIP code**.
5. If it can't find a single record, it sends you to a person: "Please let me connect you with a patient access coordinator", or "We found multiple records with the given identifiers. Let me route you to an agent."

The two longer calls held for about 66 and 47 seconds after an announced "estimated wait time" of "one, two, five minutes" and "one, two, four minutes". The quickest call skipped the hold after the "multiple records" message.

"If he's scheduled for an annual, initially fasting is recommended, but it's not required" (second call). "They do need to be fasting six hours prior to the appointment" (third call, after the representative checked the patient's age).

## Calling Mount Sinai Doctors about someone else's appointment

The assistant introduced itself as calling for someone else on calls 1 and 3, and both representatives confirmed the appointment once the patient's identifiers matched. On call 2 the representative asked for the street address and email, and the assistant only had the email. That was the end of the chart lookup, though the representative still gave a general answer about fasting.

So have the full set ready before calling: the patient's full name, date of birth, the phone number on the medical record, the ZIP code, the street address and the email on file. The automated system asks for the date of birth and phone number first. If you also know the doctor and appointment time, say them up front so the representative can find the right visit.
