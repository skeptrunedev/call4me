## What this call did not settle

- **Per person package pricing.** The manager gave a minimum, not a menu or a price per person.
- **Service charge, tax rate and deposit.** "Before tax and gratuity" was the only qualification. No rate or deposit was given.
- **Anything in writing.** The manager started taking an email address, but the call reached its time limit before she confirmed it or agreed to send anything.
- **A hold.** The brief was quote only. The room was described as available, not reserved.

One thing to know about our side: when the manager asked whether the team would be comfortable with the $1,500 minimum, the assistant said "I think that should be fine." That is a budget signal, not an agreement, and nothing was signed or charged. It also answered "Yeah" when asked if it was familiar with the restaurant. If you brief your own call, tell the caller what to say about budget so it does not improvise.

## What to ask when you call about a private dinner

A room minimum, a room fee and a per person package are three different numbers. The Capital Grille quoted a minimum you spend on food and drink. Patsy's quoted a room fee that buys no food at all. Ask which one you are hearing, then ask for the rest:

1. The room name, its seated capacity at one table, and whether it is fully enclosed.
2. The minimum or room fee, and whether it is before or after tax and gratuity.
3. The gratuity or service charge rate, and whether it is added automatically.
4. Deposit and cancellation terms.
5. The events contact's name and email, and a written proposal.

Our [private dining room cost guide](/blog/private-dining-room-cost) explains each charge, and the [Lai Wah Heen private dining call](/blog/lai-wah-heen-private-dining) shows how a room minimum can differ from a full event quote.
