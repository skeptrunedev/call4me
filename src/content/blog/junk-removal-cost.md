**The short answer:** junk removal costs depend on **how much of the truck your load fills** and **how hard it is to reach**. You get a real number by calling and describing the load, not from an average. On the call below, a San Francisco hauler quoted **"about 140"** for a small load: five office-chair boxes, a couple of TV boxes and a handful of smaller ones, from a third-floor office with a freight elevator. It booked the pickup for **11 the next morning**.

Before naming a price, the hauler asked four things. Have them ready:

1. **The city.** That was the first question.
2. **What the load is,** counted out: "five chair boxes, a couple TV boxes, and a handful of smaller ones."
3. **Whether it's a house or a commercial building.**
4. **How the crew gets to it:** the floor, and stairs or an elevator. The price came right after "there's a freight elevator, it's on the third floor."

Then the hauler wrote down the wrong street number. Our caller said "Exactly" to it, and a second call was needed to fix it. **Get the address read back to you one digit at a time before you hang up.**

These calls were made on September 30, 2026 by a call4me user's AI phone assistant. The user wrote to us afterwards: "Just used it to book junk removal tomorrow morning. What a service. Felt like living in the future!" They're okay with us sharing the calls. Their name, the office address and the phone numbers are muted in the audio.

## What moves a junk removal price

- **Volume.** Most haulers price by the share of the truck a load takes. Describe it in items you can count, not "some junk." A few boxes is a small load. A room of furniture isn't.
- **What's in it.** This load was only empty cardboard with a little styrofoam: light and easy to take. If yours has anything heavy, electronic or hazardous, say so and ask whether it changes the price.
- **Access.** Stairs, floors and long carries take crew time. "Is that at a house, or?" was the hauler's last question before the price.
- **Timing.** Our caller said "anytime, earliest available is best," and got the next morning.

One call is one data point, not a price list. Prices vary by city and by company. Call two haulers with the same description and compare.

## How to get a junk removal quote by phone, step by step

1. **Lead with the job, small and specific:** "a quote for picking up some cardboard boxes from our office." Saying it's a small load up front sets the price conversation.
2. **Count the items instead of guessing a volume.** "Five chair boxes and a couple TV boxes" is something they can picture. "Some junk" isn't.
3. **Say how the crew gets in:** house or commercial, the floor, stairs or elevator. The quote came right after "there's a freight elevator, it's on the third floor."
4. **Give a wide window.** "Anytime, earliest available is best" got the next morning.
5. **Ask for the price before you agree to a time,** then lock both in together.
6. **Spell the name and confirm the callback number.** The hauler asked for both after the address.
7. **Make them read the address back, digit by digit.** Don't accept a quick "Exactly." This is where our call went wrong.

## What to say: a script that worked

> Hi, I'm wondering about a quote for picking up [what it is] from our [home or office], please. It's in [city]. It's just a small load: [count the items]. It's [on the ground floor / on floor N with a freight elevator / up N flights of stairs]. Anytime works, earliest available is best. What would the price be for a load like that?

Once they quote: "That works. Can we lock in [day] at [time]? The address is [street number, said one digit at a time], [street], [unit]. Could you read that back to me?"

## Ask what the quote covers

Our caller took "about 140" without asking anything more, and that's worth doing better than we did. Before you book, ask:

- **Is that the total, or are there fees on top?** (dump fees, a minimum charge, stairs)
- **Can the price change when the crew sees the load?** Phone quotes are estimates; ask how they settle the final price.
- **When on the day will the crew arrive,** and will they call first?
- **For a commercial building:** do they need a certificate of insurance, or does the building need the freight elevator reserved? Some property managers ask for both. The user had flagged this for our caller; it never came up on the call.

## The second call: fixing the address (2 minutes 3 seconds)

The user caught the wrong street number in the call summary and sent the agent straight back. The brief said to give the street number digit by digit, to correct anything that came back wrong, and to give the crew the user's own phone instead of the number the hauler had.

The phone number got a full readback. The address got a "Yes," not a readback, so it's worth checking it with the crew when they call on the day. Two habits would have made this call unnecessary: say the street number one digit at a time the first time, and give the crew a number that reaches the person who'll be there.
