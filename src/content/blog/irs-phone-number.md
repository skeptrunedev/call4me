**The short answer:** to talk to a **live person at the IRS**, call **800-829-1040**, Monday through Friday, 7 a.m. to 7 p.m. local time. It isn't open 24 hours or on weekends. When the voice menu asks why you're calling, **name a specific reason**, like "I have a general question about Form 1099." **A live IRS representative answered 9 minutes 52 seconds into our call.** A vague reason got our first call hung up.

The IRS phone number for individuals is **800-829-1040**. It's open **Monday through Friday, 7 a.m. to 7 p.m. local time** (Alaska and Hawaii follow Pacific time). **An automated voice menu answers, and it won't transfer you until it knows why you're calling.** When we only said we had "a general question about reaching customer service," it said it would "need to end the call," and then it hung up. On our second call we gave a specific reason, **"I have a general question about Form 1099,"** and **a live IRS representative answered 9 minutes 52 seconds into the recording.**

The number and hours are from the IRS's [telephone assistance page](https://www.irs.gov/help/telephone-assistance), which lists "Individuals 800-829-1040, 7 a.m. to 7 p.m. local time" and says "Our help lines are open Monday through Friday. Residents of Alaska and Hawaii should follow Pacific time."

Here's what got us from the menu to a person:

1. Call 800-829-1040. For Spanish, press 9.
2. "Now, in a few words, please tell us why you are calling." **Say your actual reason, in the menu's own words.** The menu suggests phrases like "Can I make a payment plan?", "I have a question about a notice" or "I have a question about Form 1099." We said "I have a general question about Form 1099."
3. It asked which 1099 ("For example, Form 1099-G or Form 1099-SSA"). We said "Form 1099-G."
4. "Sure, I can help you with that." Recorded notices, then "We estimate your wait time to be between 15 and 30 minutes."
5. Hold music with recorded messages, about 9 minutes on our call, then a representative.

**A note on honesty:** we weren't calling about a real tax problem. We called to write this guide, asked only general questions, and never gave a Social Security number or any personal information. Our question about Form 1099 was a real general question, and we said up front that it wasn't about any account or return. We're not suggesting you give the menu a reason you don't have. Pick the option or phrase that actually matches your question; the point is that **a vague answer like "customer service" or "a representative" isn't accepted.**

**What the representative told us** (his answers, not official IRS guidance):

- **A 1099-G that never arrived or looks wrong:** "they can reach out to the establishment that would have sent them the 1099-G and try to get a copy."
- **Best time to call:** "There's no specific best time."
- **The callback option:** "when offered, the taxpayer can opt for that," and "the system will automatically call them back and route them to an employee."
- **What to have nearby:** "Probably the return in question and any supporting documentation for it if there's an issue specifically with the return."

We called on Tuesday, October 13, 2026, at about 11:55 am and 11:58 am Pacific (2:55 and 2:58 pm Eastern), with [call4me](/) placing the calls. This page is what we heard.

## How long the IRS wait is

Our hold was shorter than the menu's own estimate, but one call isn't a typical wait. The IRS [telephone assistance page](https://www.irs.gov/help/telephone-assistance) gives its own averages:

| Period | What irs.gov says |
|---|---|
| Filing season (January to April) | "Wait times can average 8 minutes." Longer "on Mondays and Tuesdays, during Presidents Day weekend and around the April tax filing deadline." |
| Post-filing season (May to December) | "Wait times can average 15 minutes." "Wait times are shorter Wednesday through Friday." |

Both rows add: "Some phone lines may have longer wait times."

**Callbacks:** the same page says "A return call may be offered when wait times exceed 15 minutes and if a representative can be reached during regular IRS business hours." On our call the menu estimated 15 to 30 minutes, but we didn't hear a callback offer. The representative described how it works when it is offered (above).

For how the IRS compares with other places we've called, see our [hold times by company](/hold-times).

## The IRS phone menu, step by step

1. **Call 800-829-1040.** "Thank you for calling the Internal Revenue Service. Para continuar en español, marque nueve."
2. **A privacy note.** "Your call may be recorded to help us improve our service, so please do not include any personal details at this time."
3. **"Now, in a few words, please tell us why you are calling."** It understands full sentences. Give your actual reason here.
4. **If it doesn't get a reason,** it says "I understand that you'd like to speak with a representative. In order to determine if I am able to transfer you, I first need to know the reason for your call," and reads a keypad menu:
   - Working family tax cuts, press 1
   - Where's my refund, press 2
   - Return status, press 3
   - Notices and letters, press 4
   - One-time payments, press 5
   - Payment plans, press 6
   - More options, press 7
5. **"More options"** are spoken only: "balance due, transcripts, help with my PIN, appointments, general account questions," or "it's something else."
6. **"It's something else"** brings back the open question, with examples: "Can I make a payment plan? Or, I have a question about a notice. Or, I have a question about Form 1099."
7. **A vague answer gets one warning,** "If we don't know your reason for calling, we will be unable to assist you and will need to end the call," and then the call ends.
8. **A specific answer gets a follow-up.** For Form 1099, it asked which type, then said "Sure, I can help you with that," gave a privacy notice and a wait estimate, and put us on hold.

We didn't press any keys on either call and didn't try the numbered options, so we can't say where each one leads.

## What to have ready

From the IRS [telephone assistance page](https://www.irs.gov/help/telephone-assistance), which says "Our representatives must verify your identity before discussing your personal information." For individuals, have:

- Social Security numbers and birth dates (or your ITIN if you don't have an SSN)
- Your filing status
- Your prior-year tax return
- The tax return you're calling about
- Any correspondence the IRS sent you

The representative's own answer matched the return part: "the return in question and any supporting documentation for it if there's an issue specifically with the return." He added that for a filing issue "there's not necessarily anything specific that would be necessary in all cases." We didn't need any of this, since our questions weren't about anyone's account.

## How to know it's really the IRS

The hold messages on our call warned about impersonation scams: "The real IRS will not call, email, or text you to demand immediate payment, nor will we call about taxes owed without first having mailed you a bill. The IRS never calls asking for your credit card, debit card, or prepaid card information over the telephone."

The IRS page [How to know it's really the IRS calling or knocking on your door](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/how-to-know-its-really-the-irs-calling-or-knocking-on-your-door) says "The IRS initiates most contacts through regular mail," and lists what the IRS does not do, including:

- "Call to demand immediate payment using a specific payment method such as a prepaid debit card, gift card or wire transfer. Generally, the IRS will first mail a bill to any taxpayer who owes taxes."
- "Demand that you pay taxes without the opportunity to question or appeal the amount they say you owe."
- "Threaten to bring in local police, immigration officers or other law-enforcement to have you arrested for not paying."

It also says payments go to the "United States Treasury," with instructions at irs.gov/payments. If someone calls you claiming to be the IRS, you can hang up and call 800-829-1040 yourself.

## What the representative said, in detail

We asked four general questions. The answers are his, as recorded, not official IRS guidance, and we didn't check them against IRS publications.

**A missing or wrong 1099-G.** "I mean, they can reach out to the establishment that would have sent them the 1099-G and try to get a copy."

**Best time to call.** "There's no specific best time." The IRS page does name better and worse times ([above](#how-long-the-irs-wait-is)).

**The callback.** "The callback option, when offered, the taxpayer can opt for that, and then if they watch their phone, the system will automatically call them back and route them to an employee that can help them with whatever account issue they have."

**What to have nearby.** "Probably the return in question and any supporting documentation for it if there's an issue specifically with the return. If it's a filing issue, there's not necessarily anything specific that would be necessary in all cases."
