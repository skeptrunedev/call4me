**The short answer:** call the store, ask a person to check the shelf for your size, and ask them to set the pair aside under your name. That's what a call4me user's AI phone assistant did on Thursday, October 1, 2026, after the user's dress shoe broke on the morning of a 5 pm gala in Washington, DC. Between 10:18 and 10:55 am Eastern, it made 6 calls to 3 stores. By 10:55 am, the **Men's Wearhouse on Connecticut Avenue had black Stacy Adams loafers in a size 10.5 set aside** for the user to pick up and pay for that day.

The user's ask: plain black men's dress shoes, size 10 or 10.5, no sneakers or white soles, **$120 or less including tax**, and something they could buy that day.

What it took:

1. **Two Nordstrom Racks, no specific pair.** One store put us on hold, and then our caller stopped hearing the line (our bug, more below). The other said it had "quite a variety" but needed an item number from the website to check a specific shoe.
2. **Men's Wearhouse, call 1:** a size 10 at **$124.99 before tax**, over budget. The call ended while the associate was checking for something cheaper.
3. **Men's Wearhouse, call 2:** **Stacy Adams loafers, $119 before tax, in a 10.5**, plus a **$20 off discount** if you sign up in the store. A shoe-only rental was "like, what, $80 to $90" for one day.
4. **Men's Wearhouse, calls 3 and 4:** after the user okayed a hold, one call got "Your call cannot be transferred," and the next one got the pair set aside.

All six calls were made by a call4me user in Washington, DC. Our caller didn't buy anything or give a card. The user's name and phone number and the store staff's names are muted in the audio and shown in brackets in the transcripts. Stretches of dead air longer than about 3 seconds are shortened to about 1 second, and each cut is marked in the transcript. Prices and stock are what each store said on that morning, and they can change any day.

## Can Men's Wearhouse hold items for pickup?

Yes, at least at this store, on this day. The associate checked the shelf, found the pair, and set it aside under the user's name for pickup the same day, with no card and no deposit. The catch is how long: "We're open 'til seven, and then usually folks just pick stuff up at the end of the day and put it back." Treat a phone hold as same day only, and go before closing.

Two things that helped:

- **Name the exact shoe.** "Black Stacy Adams loafers, ten and a half" got a quick yes. The associate still asked, "Does it have like a little silver buckle thing on it?" to be sure.
- **Press 0.** The store's recorded greeting lists its address and hours, then: "To speak to a store associate, press zero." Pressing 0 got a person in under 20 seconds on three of the four Men's Wearhouse calls. The fourth got "Your call cannot be transferred," and calling right back worked.

## Can you rent just shoes at Men's Wearhouse?

The associate said a shoe-only rental for the day would be "like, what, eighty to ninety bucks to rent them," and then added, "Yeah, it's crazy." With $119 to buy and $20 off for signing up in the store, buying cost about the same as renting.

## Nordstrom Rack: what to ask for

Nordstrom Rack's phone menu, at both DC stores: **0** for the operator, **2** for hours and directions, **4** for jobs, **5** for a store representative, **6** for a list of department managers, **8** to repeat. Neither store had someone in men's shoes to check the shelf over the phone. Metro Center said it had "quite a variety" in the size and price range, and asked for an **item number from nordstromrack.com** to look up a specific shoe. If you call a Rack, find a pair on the website first and read them the item number.

## Where our caller fell short

- **At Nordstrom Rack L Street, it stopped hearing the line.** After the store put it on hold, staff came back and said hello several times, and our caller never answered. Our saved transcript ends at "Sure, no problem," so our side stopped receiving the store's audio. That's a bug on our side, not the store's.
- **At Men's Wearhouse, it went one question at a time.** The first call asked about a size, then the price, then before or after tax, then something cheaper, and the call ended on hold before the cheaper pair came up. Asking "Do you have a black dress shoe in a 10 or 10.5 under $113 before tax?" up front would have saved a call.
- **It asked for the total with the discount**, which the associate couldn't give until the user signed up in the store. It should have asked what the signup is.
- **It never asked how long a hold would last** until the last call. "Can you hold it until 5?" is worth asking every time.

## What to say: a script that worked

> Hi, could you hold a pair of [brand and style] in a size [size] for [name] to pick up today? They'll pay in the store.

If you don't know the exact shoe yet: "Could someone check the shelf for a plain black [style] in a [size], under [price] before tax? What's the brand and the price?"
