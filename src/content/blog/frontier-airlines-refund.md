**The short answer:** yes, you can get a Frontier Airlines refund by phone after a delay, and on both of these calls the money went back to the original form of payment, not to a voucher. On the first call, a delay meant the passenger would miss a connection in Las Vegas. The Frontier agent offered "another rebooking, a refund or a uh credit," and when our caller asked for "a cash refund to the original card, not a voucher or credit," the agent confirmed it would go back "as a cash to the account." That was about $235, due "within the next seven business days." On the second call, a return flight from Las Vegas had been pushed overnight, and Frontier had already refunded the airfare, **$57.96**, to the original payment method. The same call asked Frontier to pay for a rental car and meals. A supervisor said no: "here in Frontier, we do not offer a goodwill cash reimbursement," only a $50 flight voucher per passenger.

What it took:

1. **Call 1, a missed connection (5 minutes 40 seconds).** A person picked up about 1 minute 50 seconds in. The agent needed the confirmation code and the passenger's name, saw the delay, and offered three choices. Our caller asked for a cash refund of the whole itinerary, and the agent processed it on the call and emailed a confirmation.
2. **Call 2, an overnight delay (14 minutes).** Frontier's AI assistant read out the refund rules but couldn't confirm the amount, and a person took over about 2 minutes 50 seconds in. The agent confirmed the $57.96 refund, then checked twice with a supervisor about roughly $300 in rental car and meal costs: no cash, $50 per passenger in vouchers, valid 90 days, which our caller didn't accept.

These two calls were made for two different call4me users: one for a passenger whose first flight was running late enough to miss a connection in Las Vegas, and one for a call4me user in the Bay Area whose return flight from Las Vegas was pushed overnight. Our caller didn't accept a voucher, rebook anything or give a card. Names, confirmation codes, card digits, email addresses, phone numbers and flight times are muted in the audio and shown in brackets in the transcripts, and we've left out flight numbers and travel dates. Refund amounts and timelines are what Frontier's agents said on each call, and Frontier's policies can change.

## Call 1: a missed connection, refunded to the card

The passenger hadn't flown yet. Our caller called while the first flight was delayed and told the agent: "with that delay, he'll land in Vegas after his connecting flight to San Francisco has already left. There's no way he'll make that connection." The agent had just read out the new times, a delay of about an hour and 45 minutes on the first flight.

What Frontier needed was short:

1. **The confirmation code.** "Can you enter the confirmation code for the delayed flight."
2. **The passenger's first and last name.**
3. **The email address and phone number on the booking**, "just reconfirm," before processing.

The agent didn't ask how late the passenger would get in, and didn't ask for the passenger on the line. Once the agent saw the delay, the choices came right away: "For this missed connection, I can offer [the passenger] a another rebooking, a refund or a uh credit for the for- for the flight that was delayed."

Our caller picked the refund and said what kind: "He doesn't wanna travel on this itinerary anymore. We'd like a full refund to the original card for the whole itinerary including any bags or seats." The agent read out the total, which our call transcript has as "two thirty four ninety eight cents" (the cents are hard to make out in the recording), going to a card our caller confirmed by its last digits. Then our caller asked once more: "Could you process that as a cash refund to the original card, not a voucher or credit?" The answer: "Yes, it will be refunded as a a refund, as a cash to the account."

The agent processed it on the call ("I'm processing the refund as we speak"), sent a confirmation email "within two to five minutes," and said the refund would be in the account "within the next seven business days." There was no case number; the email was the confirmation. Our caller didn't ask how the total broke down, so we can't say whether it included any bags or seats.

## Call 2: an overnight delay, a refund already on its way

The second user's return flight from Las Vegas was pushed overnight, and they drove home in a rental car instead. Our caller called Frontier to confirm the airfare refund and to ask for about $300 for the rental car and meals.

**Frontier's AI assistant answered first** and read out the rules: "For delays of more than three hours on domestic flights or more than six hours on international flights, refunds can be processed automatically if you did not travel, and refunds issued to the original form of payment are processed within seven business days for credit cards or twenty calendar days for other payment methods." It added: "Keep itemized receipts and your baggage claim reference if you are filing bag delay expenses." But it couldn't confirm this booking's refund: "I can share the refund rules, but I can't confirm an exact refund amount or a specific status from here." About 2 minutes 50 seconds into the call, a person came on in the middle of the AI's next sentence.

**The agent found the refund already done.** After the full name, the confirmation code and one passenger's name, the agent said: "there is already a refund that was been processed yesterday. The amount is fifty-seven dollars point ninety-six cents and just to inform you that refund will take seven business days to be completed or it may take a few more days depending on your bank's processing time." Our caller confirmed at the end that it was going back to the original payment method: "Mm-hmm. Correct."

**The rental car and meals: no cash, twice.** The agent checked with a supervisor ("But I will not guarantee. I will just try my best here") and came back with the answer in the clip above: no goodwill cash reimbursement, a $50 flight voucher per passenger instead, $100 total. Our caller asked again for cash "up to the documented costs. Uh, no vouchers," and after another hold the agent said the supervisor's answer was the same: "Frontier cannot able to process any a cash reimbursement," and the vouchers were "valid until ninety... days."

**Why: air traffic control.** Our caller asked what caused the delay. "The cause of the of this delays on a flight is due to uncontrollable circumstances which is the air traffic delays or a- air traffic control delays. So it is out of the Frontier's control, that's why we cannot able to issue any cash reimbursement."

**Where to send receipts.** The agent said to go to flyfrontier.com, "Under customer service look for the document submission form," attach the receipts and submit, and "the back office team will gonna review your request and that would be subject for approval." The agent was clear it might not work: "I will not guarantee if it will be issued to you by the back office team." For a reference, the agent gave their own agent number, which we've left out. A case number for the receipts "would be emailed to you" after submitting. The call took 14 minutes, and our records count about 6 of them on hold.

## What Frontier's site and the DOT say

What the agents said lines up closely with what Frontier publishes. Here's the written version, checked November 25, 2026.

**Frontier's delay and missed connection FAQ.** [What can I expect if my flight is delayed, canceled, diverted or causes me to miss a connection?](https://faq.flyfrontier.com/help/my-flight-is-delayed-more-than-3-hours-what-should-i-expect) (last modified November 25, 2026) says that if "you're expected to arrive at your final destination three hours or more late for a domestic itinerary or six hours or more late for an international itinerary, you're entitled to a refund for the unused portion of your ticket if you choose not to travel and don't accept a travel credit instead." On missed connections: "You may be able to rebook, receive a travel credit, or request a refund for the affected segment or unused portion of your itinerary if you choose not to continue traveling." On timing: "Any refund you're owed will be issued automatically to your original form of payment, even if you don't respond. Once due, refunds are issued within seven business days for credit card purchases or 20 calendar days for other payment methods."

**Frontier's refund options page.** [Refund Options](https://www.flyfrontier.com/refund-options/) says refunds apply only if the customer "did not travel on the delayed or rescheduled flight, and did not choose to receive a travel credit instead of a refund to original form of payment." It also covers an extra you paid for and didn't get: you may be eligible for a refund "if Frontier was unable to provide an optional ancillary service you purchased for your Frontier itinerary, through no fault of your own." And on expenses, in so many words: "Frontier does not reimburse expenses, provide hotel accommodations, or provide monetary compensation for passengers impacted by flight delays or cancellations." For delays within its control, "Frontier will provide meal vouchers for flight delays of 3 hours or more." For weather and air traffic control: "we do not offer compensation for uncontrollable events beyond the re-accommodation on the next available Frontier flight."

So the $50 vouchers on call 2 were more than that page promises for an air traffic control delay, and they came on top of the airfare refund, not instead of it. One thing the agent said doesn't match the site: Frontier's customer service page describes the Document Submission Form as for "emergency events or name changes. Travel Insurance Verification Request," not expense receipts, and the refund page says, "Please do not complete a refund application for these requests."

**The US Department of Transportation.** The DOT's [Refunds page](https://www.transportation.gov/individuals/aviation-consumer-protection/refunds) (last updated November 7, 2025) counts a domestic flight as significantly delayed when you're "scheduled to arrive at the destination airport 3 hours or more for domestic itineraries or 6 hours or more for international itineraries later than the original scheduled arrival time," and also counts an itinerary "with more connection points than that of the original itinerary." The catch is the same as Frontier's: "If you chose to take a significantly delayed/changed flight or an alternative flight offered by the airline, you are not entitled to a refund under DOT rules." When you turn down a rebooking, "A refund is due within 7 business days after you reject an offer if you paid for the ticket with a credit card."

## How to get a Frontier Airlines refund after a delay

Built from what these two calls showed, before your holiday travel:

1. **Call before you fly the delayed flight.** Call 1 went out before the delayed first flight left, and the refund was done on that call. Both Frontier's page and the DOT say that if you take the delayed flight, the refund is off.
2. **Have the confirmation code and the passenger's full name ready.** That's all Frontier asked for before pulling up the trip, plus the email and phone number on the booking to reconfirm. Neither agent asked for the passenger on the line; both talked to an assistant.
3. **Say "a cash refund to the original card, not a voucher or credit."** The agent's first offer on call 1 was three options, and a credit was one of them. Naming the kind of refund got a plain yes.
4. **Ask for the amount and the timing before you hang up.** Both agents said seven business days. Call 1 also got a confirmation email within minutes.
5. **If Frontier already refunded you, call to see the amount.** On call 2 the refund had gone through on its own, and only a person, not the AI assistant, could read out the amount.
6. **Talk to Frontier's AI assistant in short sentences.** After a long request it said, "I'm sorry, I couldn't quite catch all of that. Could you try again using fewer words."
7. **Ask what caused the delay first if you want expenses back.** On call 2, air traffic control was the whole answer: "it is out of the Frontier's control." Frontier's page only promises meal vouchers for delays within its control.
8. **Don't count on cash for a hotel, rental car or meals.** Two supervisor checks on call 2 ended at $50 per passenger in vouchers, valid 90 days. On a different airline, a goodwill ask after a delay got [a $100 JSX travel voucher](/blog/customer-story-flight-credit), also not cash.

## Refund calls to other airlines

Other call4me users' assistants called other airlines about refunds this fall. Three of those calls added something our other airline posts don't cover.

- **American Airlines, a paid seat after an upgrade.** One user's assistant asked American for a $72 Main Cabin Extra seat fee back after the traveler paid to upgrade to first class on the same flight. The agent's first answer was "I just wanna inform you that uh the seats are non-refundable." Our caller pointed to American's policy, and after about three and a half minutes on hold the agent came back: "you're eligible for the refund," and it was already refunded. Then: "so please allow seven business days for your refund to show on your online statement." There was no separate refund number; the agent said to check its status on aa.com under receipts and refunds with the ticket number and the passenger's last name. American's [seat terms](https://www.aa.com/i18n/fragments/merchandising/seats/selectSeatsTerms.jsp) list "You pay to upgrade to First or Business" under "When we'll issue a refund for seats (no request needed)." For using a credit instead, see [an American Airlines flight credit by phone](/blog/american-airlines-flight-credit).
- **Etihad, a paid business upgrade on a seat that wouldn't lie flat.** The same user's assistant called Etihad about a $1,512 paid upgrade (our [Etihad customer service post](/blog/etihad-customer-service) has the earlier follow-up). The agent said that refund "has never been actioned" because "the guest was offered the business cabin itself," and "the upgrade amount is... non-refundable." A separate $236 refund for a seat bought in economy had gone through: "it would take five to fourteen business days to be reflected in your account."
- **Southwest, canceling one leg of a round trip.** Another user's assistant asked Southwest to cancel only the return of a Wanna Get Away round trip. The agent said the fare "won't let me split it up," canceled the whole ticket and rebooked the outbound one-way at the same fare ("I'm doing a goodwill gesture"), and the rest, $217.41, stayed on the old booking as travel funds, not a refund, expiring "six months from the date it was booked." The agent's advice for next time: "book 'em as two one-way." See also [how to reach a live agent at Southwest](/blog/southwest-airlines-phone-number).

## Where our caller fell short

- **On call 2, it gave Frontier's AI assistant everything at once:** a goodwill reimbursement for a rental car and meals, plus the pending refund, then a longer explanation. The AI answered with general rules, then asked for "fewer words."
- **It asked what caused the delay last.** Two supervisor checks went by before our caller asked, and the answer, air traffic control, explained the no. Asking first would have saved a hold.
- **On call 1, it didn't ask for a breakdown** of the roughly $235 total, so we don't know whether bags or seats were in it, and it didn't ask for a refund reference beyond the confirmation email.

## What to say: a script that worked

> Hi, I'm calling about a refund on confirmation [code] for [passenger's name]. The first flight is delayed, so [they'll] miss the connection and won't travel. Could you refund the whole itinerary as a cash refund to the original card, not a voucher or credit? What's the total, and when will it post?

If you also want expenses back: "What caused the delay, and does Frontier count it as within its control?"
