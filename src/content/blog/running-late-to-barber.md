If you are **running late to the barber**, give the shop your appointment time and an honest estimate of when you will arrive. Ask them to acknowledge the update so you know someone received it.

On October 5, 2026, a call4me customer's AI assistant called a barbershop about an existing **6:20 PM haircut and beard trim**. The message was simple: the customer was on the way and **about five minutes away**, with thanks for waiting.

The barber acknowledged the message and said they would be waiting. **This was an arrival update.** The caller did not create a new appointment, change the service or negotiate a different time.

## Five minutes away is an arrival estimate

The customer gave the assistant a narrow task with enough detail for the shop to recognize the booking. The appointment time and service identified which customer was coming, and the travel estimate explained what to expect next.

“Five minutes away” and “five minutes late” are not interchangeable. The first is an estimate from the moment of the call. The second compares arrival with the booked time. A caller should pass along the estimate the customer actually supplied rather than quietly convert it into a different claim.

The barber's reply establishes that the update was received and accepted in this conversation. It does not establish when the customer eventually arrived or whether the haircut was completed.

## What to give your assistant

1. The barbershop and the name on the existing booking.
2. The appointment time and service, so staff can identify it.
3. Your current arrival estimate in plain language.
4. Instructions to pass along the update and ask whether the plan still works.
5. A clear boundary if you do not want the assistant to cancel, rebook or accept a fee.

If the shop says it can no longer fit you in, have the assistant bring back the available options. A brief arrival message does not need to become a new booking without your instruction.
