**To ask for connecting rooms at Moxy St. Petersburg Downtown, call the front desk; the hotel doesn't offer them, but staff will try to keep your rooms close.** The hotel is at 1234 Central Avenue, St. Petersburg, Florida 33705. Marriott's [official hotel page](https://www.marriott.com/en-us/hotels/tpasx-moxy-st-petersburg-downtown/overview/) lists **+1 727 677 2000**. That is the number the call below dialed, and a person at the front desk picked up right away, with no phone menu.

**The short answer on connecting rooms:** the employee said **"we don't offer connecting rooms, but we do try to get rooms as close to each other as possible."** She then said she would make sure the two rooms were close, and agreed it was a note on the reservation with no change in price.

On Sunday, October 4, 2026, a call4me customer's assistant called the Moxy St. Pete front desk. The customer had two existing rooms on one reservation, each with two queen beds, for a stay from the 8th to the 11th. The brief asked for connecting rooms, and if those weren't possible, for rooms next to each other or on the same floor.

Staff asked once whether the other room had a different confirmation number. The assistant said it had been given one number for both rooms and thought they were under the same one. Staff took a few lookups after that, then came back with the note.

## What this call did not settle

- **No room numbers or floor were assigned.** "Close" is a request on the reservation, not a promise of adjoining or same-floor rooms.
- **No written confirmation.** Nothing was emailed or read back beyond the dates.
- **The customer's Marriott Bonvoy number was in the brief but never used.** The call didn't ask about elite benefits or upgrades.
- **The assistant didn't ask the same floor question by name.** It accepted "close" once staff said connecting rooms weren't offered.
- **The check-in result is unknown.** The recording can't show which rooms the guests actually got.

A note on the assistant's side of the call: while the employee was looking up the reservation, the assistant asked "Hello, are you still there?" three times. She was there each time. Those pauses are kept in the recording as they happened.

## Getting rooms together at the Moxy hotel in St. Petersburg

The useful part of this brief was the fallback. The customer said up front that if connecting rooms weren't possible, rooms next to each other or on the same floor were fine, and that any extra charge needed their approval first. So when staff said the Moxy St. Petersburg Downtown doesn't do connecting rooms, the assistant could move straight to the next best thing instead of ending the call.

If being on the same floor matters to you, say it as its own request, and ask the caller to repeat it by name. Here the assistant took "close" and moved on. Ask whether the note is a guarantee or a request, and whether you can check again closer to arrival. Calling a few days before check-in, when rooms are being assigned, may also help, though this call didn't test that.

Give the caller the confirmation number privately, and say whether both rooms share it. Staff asked about that here.
