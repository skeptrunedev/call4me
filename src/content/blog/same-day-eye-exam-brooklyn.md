**Brooklyn Eye Center's Williamsburg office is at 597 Grand Street, Brooklyn, NY 11211.** The practice's [official website](https://www.brooklyneyecenter.com/) lists **718-388-5200** for that office, open Monday to Friday 8:30 AM to 4:30 PM, and a central appointment line at 718-221-3333. The same site's appointment form names the Williamsburg office as 719 Grand Street, so confirm the address when you book. The phone greeting we heard said 597 Grand Street.

On Friday, October 9, 2026, a little after 9:00 AM Eastern, a call4me customer's assistant called three eye practices in Williamsburg and Greenpoint at the same time. The patient was new to all three, had a painful red eye since the day before, and had Aetna medical insurance. The brief was strict: ask about availability only, and book nothing.

**Brooklyn Eye Center on Grand Street could see the patient that day.** The receptionist offered 10:00 AM and said 11:30 AM "would be the latest", and confirmed "Medical Aetna, yes."

## What the calls did not establish

- **Nothing was booked or held.** Each office asked for a detail (date of birth, or whether the patient had been seen before), and the assistant said the patient would call back with it. No time was reserved at any of the three.
- **Nobody confirmed a specific plan.** Staff answered "Aetna" and "Aetna medical". No member ID was read, and no office checked eligibility or a copay.
- **Brooklyn Eye Center's 11:30 AM.** The receptionist called it "the latest". It is not clear whether 10:00 and 11:30 were the only open times or the ends of a range.
- **Greenpoint Eye Care's "until 11".** Two transcriptions disagree on whether the receptionist said the patient "can" or "can't" reach the office until 11. The meaning that fits the rest of the call is "if the patient can get here by about 11". The second time sounds like 10:30 but is unclear.
- **No clinical advice was given.** Nobody on these calls assessed the eye. A painful red eye can be urgent. If vision changes, pain gets worse, or you can't reach an office, seek urgent or emergency care.

## Calling around for a same day eye exam in Brooklyn

Calling several offices at once turned one slow morning task into about two and a half minutes. Each call asked the same three things: can a doctor see a new patient today, what's the earliest time, and do you take my insurance. That makes the answers easy to line up.

A few things this morning showed:

- **Say "new patient" and the symptom up front.** Both offices with openings answered with a time right after hearing it. The Grand Street receptionist asked about insurance before offering a slot.
- **Have the date of birth ready if you want to book.** Two offices asked for it as soon as a time came up. Here the assistant was told not to share it, so nothing was held. If you want the slot, give your assistant permission to share what the office needs.
- **The menu matters at Brooklyn Eye Center.** Press 1 for appointments. A person answered after about 24 seconds of hold music.
- **A full office may still point you somewhere.** Greenpoint Vision Care named New York Eye and Ear and Brooklyn Eye Center.
