**The short answer:** the Social Security Administration phone number is **1-800-772-1213** (TTY **1-800-325-0778**). People answer **Monday through Friday, 8 a.m. to 7 p.m. local time**; the automated services run 24 hours a day. **A voice menu answers first, and it asks for your nine-digit Social Security number.** We didn't give one. After one more try the menu said "Let me connect you to an agent," quoted a **20 minute hold**, and offered a callback. We declined the callback and waited, and **a live representative answered 19 minutes 33 seconds after the call connected.**

The number, hours and TTY line are from SSA's [Contact Social Security By Phone](https://www.ssa.gov/agency/contact/phone.html) page, which says "You can call us at 1-800-772-1213 between 8:00 a.m. – 7:00 p.m. local time, Monday through Friday" and "Our automated telephone services are available 24 hours a day and do not require you to wait to speak with a representative." When we loaded it on October 13, 2026, it also carried a banner: "Due to technical difficulties, we are experiencing higher than normal wait times."

Here's what got us from the menu to a person, with no Social Security number:

1. Call 1-800-772-1213. For Spanish, press 7.
2. A recorded message about a Medicare premium rebate asks you to say yes or no. We didn't answer it, and the menu moved on.
3. "How can I help you today?" We said we had a general question about reaching customer service.
4. "Please provide the reason for your call." We said it was a general question, not about any account. It said "One moment while I transfer your call to an agent."
5. It asked: "Please say or enter your nine-digit Social Security number, one digit at a time." **We said we couldn't provide one.**
6. "Looks like we are having a problem understanding you. Let me connect you to an agent." Then the hold estimate and the callback offer: **press 1** for a callback to the number you're calling from, **press 2** to enter a different number, **press 3** to wait on hold.
7. About 17 minutes of hold music and recorded messages, then a representative.

**A note on honesty:** we weren't calling about anyone's benefits. We called to write this guide, said so to the menu and to the representative, and never gave a Social Security number or any personal information. If your call is about your own record, entering your number is probably what the menu wants, and it may route you differently. We didn't test that.

**What the representative told us** (the representative's answers, not official SSA guidance):

- **Best time to call:** "We really don't have a set best date or time to call." SSA's own website and hold messages say otherwise ([see below](#best-time-to-call-the-rep-versus-ssa)).
- **A lost Social Security card:** "They can replace their Social Security card online if they have an online account set up." Without one, "they would have to create one through SSA.gov."
- **A call saying your Social Security number is suspended:** "They should definitely not give any information and hang up."
- **Walk-ins or appointments at field offices: not answered.** The call dropped into SSA's post-call survey right after we asked, before the representative answered, and before we heard where to report a scam call.

We called on Tuesday, October 13, 2026, at about 2:58 pm Pacific (5:58 pm Eastern), with [call4me](/) placing the call. It was a general question for this guide, not about any account. Billed time was 22 minutes. This page is what we heard.

## The Social Security phone menu, step by step

It's a voice menu. The only keypad options we heard were 7 for Spanish, 1 or 2 for the Medicare rebate check, and 1, 2 or 3 at the callback offer.

1. **Call 1-800-772-1213.** "Welcome to the Social Security Administration. Para español, marque 7." Then "For training and quality assurance, your call will be monitored or recorded."
2. **The Medicare rebate message.** "If you are calling about the Trump Administration's Medicare Improvement Fund premium rebate, and you are enrolled in Medicare Part B, you may be eligible for a one-time payment of $90 to offset some of the cost of your October 2026 Medicare premium." It ends with "To check whether you are eligible for this payment, say yes, or press 1 to continue, say no, or press 2." We didn't check this message against SSA or Medicare's website; it's just what the menu played on October 13.
3. **"How can I help you today?"** and then **"So that an agent can serve you better, please provide the reason for your call."** It accepted "a general consumer guide about reaching your customer service, not about any specific account" and said it would transfer us.
4. **The Social Security number prompt.** "Let's try again. Please say or enter your nine-digit Social Security number, one digit at a time."
5. **No number, still an agent.** After we said we couldn't provide one: "Looks like we are having a problem understanding you. Let me connect you to an agent." It then asks you to stay on the line after the call for a short survey.
6. **The hold estimate and callback.** "The estimated hold time is 20 minutes. To avoid waiting on the phone, we can call you back when it's your spot in line." Press 1 for a callback to the number you're calling from (it reads that number back), press 2 to enter another number, press 3 to wait on hold. We chose to wait. Our caller's choice isn't audible on the recording.
7. **Hold.** Music, a Privacy Act statement, reminders that much of SSA's business can be done at www.socialsecurity.gov, a scam warning, and a second callback offer at 14:42. A representative answered at 19:33, about 17 minutes after the callback offer, a bit under the 20 minute estimate.

## Best time to call: the rep versus SSA

These don't match, so here are both.

**The representative:** "We really don't have a set best date or time to call."

**SSA's hold message, at 10:13:** "Did you know that our wait times are shorter early in the morning, later in the week, and later in the month? If your business is not urgent, you may experience a shorter wait for service if you call back during those times."

**SSA's [phone page](https://www.ssa.gov/agency/contact/phone.html):** "Wait times to speak to a representative are typically shorter in the morning, later in the week, and later in the month."

We'd go with the written guidance: early morning, late in the week, late in the month. We called on a Tuesday afternoon in the middle of the month, close to the opposite, and waited about 17 minutes.

## Replacing a lost Social Security card

**The representative:** "They can replace their Social Security card online if they have an online account set up." Without an account, "they would have to create one through SSA.gov." The documents part of our question went unanswered of our question.

**SSA's [Replace Social Security card](https://www.ssa.gov/number-card/replace-card) page**, checked October 13, 2026, says:

- "You can request a free replacement card."
- "You may not need to replace your card if you know your Social Security number. In most cases, a physical card isn't necessary."
- "The fastest way to get a replacement card is to start your application online. Your local Social Security office cannot print one for you."
- "Depending on your situation, you may be able to apply for a replacement card online. If not, you'll need to make an appointment at a local office."
- "You'll receive your replacement card by mail in 5 to 10 business days."

The same page says you can also call 1-800-772-1213 and "tell the representative you want to request a replacement Social Security card." The online account the rep meant is **my Social Security**, at [ssa.gov/myaccount](https://www.ssa.gov/myaccount).

## Scam calls about your Social Security number

**The representative's answer** to a call saying your number is suspended: "They should definitely not give any information and hang up." The call ended before we heard where to report it.

**SSA's hold message, at 11:26:** "Criminals continue to impersonate SSA and other government agencies to obtain personal information or money. If you receive a questionable call, email, or message on social media and suspect it is a scam, please report it to the Office of the Inspector General at ssa.gov slash scam."

SSA's [Protect Yourself from Scams](https://www.ssa.gov/scam/) page (ssa.gov/scam), checked October 13, 2026, lists "Threaten to suspend your Social Security number or account" among common scam tactics, along with threats of arrest, demands for gift cards, wire transfers, cryptocurrency or cash, and spoofed government phone numbers. Its advice includes:

- "Hang up or ignore the message. Do not click on links or attachments."
- "Report the scam to the Office of the Inspector General at oig.ssa.gov/report."
- "Beware of fake call transfers. Legitimate companies and other agencies cannot transfer your phone call directly to SSA or OIG. If they claim they can, it's a scam!"

The page's "Report a scam" button leads to SSA OIG's online form. When we loaded the OIG's [report page](https://oig.ssa.gov/report/) the same day, it said: "We are currently experiencing technical issues that may affect some users' ability to submit scam reports," and recommended trying again if it fails.

If someone calls claiming to be Social Security, you can hang up and call 1-800-772-1213 yourself.

## What wasn't answered

- **Walk-ins or appointments at field offices.** We asked; the call went to the survey before the representative answered. SSA's replacement card page says that if you can't apply online for a card, "you'll need to make an appointment at a local office," but we didn't find or check a general walk-in policy. The phone page points to SSA's office locator for local office numbers.
- **Where to report a scam call,** from the representative. SSA's website and hold message say the Office of the Inspector General (above).
- **What documents a card replacement needs.** The representative answered the online part only.

## Things we didn't test

- **Entering a Social Security number.** We had none to give. With one, the menu may route you differently or faster.
- **The callback.** We declined it both times, so we can't say how long a callback takes.
- **The Medicare rebate option** (say yes or press 1).
- **Calling early in the morning or late in the month,** when SSA says waits are shorter.
- **The TTY line** at 1-800-325-0778, or your local office's number.

## How we made this recording

call4me is a phone for your AI agent: your agent (Claude Code, Codex, ChatGPT) tells it what to get done and it makes the call. The brief was to reach a person on SSA's national line and ask general questions for this guide: the best time to call, replacing a lost card, what to do about a "suspended number" scam call, and whether field offices take walk-ins. It wasn't allowed to give a Social Security number or any personal details.

Two things our caller could have done better:

- **It saved the walk-in question for last,** and the call dropped into the survey before the answer. Asking the question we cared most about first would have saved it.
- **It combined two questions** about scam calls (what to do and where to report), and the representative answered only the first. One question at a time gets more complete answers.

We've called another federal line for this series: [the IRS phone number](/blog/irs-phone-number), where the voice menu hung up on us until we named a specific reason. On another automated line, [Experian's](/blog/experian-phone-number), the menu wanted a full Social Security number before anything else and we never reached a person. For how SSA's wait compares, see our [hold times](/hold-times) and the [companies we've called](/companies).
