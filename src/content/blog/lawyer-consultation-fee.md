**The short answer:** the one lawyer consultation we booked cost **$300 for 30 minutes by phone**. The firm first quoted **"like a $400 fee that if you hire us, we'll apply it to the bill,"** and said yes when our caller asked for 30 minutes at $300 or less. That's one fee from one Michigan firm, not a price list. The other three firms we called never got to a price: a voice phone menu misheard our caller, one firm hung up right after our caller's request, and one call stayed silent for two minutes.

What the firm asked before booking. Have these ready:

1. **When you're free.** It opened with "How is Tuesday the 6th?" and settled on 6:30 pm, an evening slot.
2. **Where you are.** "Are you close to [place removed]?" Not close, so it offered "an initial phone call" instead of meeting in person.
3. **An email address,** for a promised calendar invite.
4. **Who's on the other side.** This is the conflict check: "before we can actually give any substantive advice," the firm has to make sure it doesn't already represent the party you're up against. It asked for the name, checked, and said "that's not going to be a conflict problem for us."

It didn't ask for details of the legal problem on this call. The fee, the time and the conflict check were the whole booking.

These calls were made on Thursday, October 1, 2026, between about 9:32 and 9:41 am Eastern, by a call4me user's AI phone assistant. The brief: book a 1-hour or 30-minute consultation with an attorney who handles software and internet law, on a weekday evening or a weekend, for no more than $300. The user is okay with us sharing the calls. We left that firm unnamed here, since naming it would say who they may hire.

## Do lawyers charge for a consultation?

Some do and some don't, and you find out by asking. The one firm that answered charged. Our caller had been told a free intro call was fine too, but none was offered.

If you're in Michigan and want a low-cost first conversation, the State Bar of Michigan's [Lawyer Referral Service](https://lrs.michbar.org/LRS-Info/Lawyer-Referral-Service) says that for a $25 administrative fee it will match you with a participating attorney "who will provide up to a 25-minute initial consultation free of charge." After that, it says, fees are negotiated between you and the attorney. Other states' bar associations run similar services; check your own.

What these calls showed about the fee itself:

- **The first number wasn't the only number.** $400 was quoted for an open-ended consultation. Asking for "a 30-minute consult for $300 or less" got a yes in about two seconds.
- **It may count toward the bill.** The firm said the $400 fee would be applied to the bill if the user hires the firm. When it agreed to $300, nobody said whether that still applies. Ask.
- **The format followed the distance.** Far from the office meant a phone consultation, which also made an evening slot easy.

## How to book a lawyer consultation, step by step

1. **Know your other party's name before you call.** The firm needs it for the conflict check, and it can't give "substantive advice" until that's done.
2. **Say the area of law in plain words.** "An attorney who handles software or internet law" was enough to get a yes.
3. **Give your availability as a range.** "Anytime weekday evenings or weekends is best" got a 6:30 pm slot on the first try.
4. **Ask the fee before you agree to the time,** or right after. Then ask: **does it count toward the bill if I hire you?**
5. **Set your limit ahead of time, and ask for a shorter consult if the price is over it.** "Could you do a 30-minute consult for $300 or less?" turned $400 into $300.
6. **Ask whether it's by phone, video or in person,** and who calls whom.
7. **Get written confirmation.** The firm promised an invite; receipt was unverified. Confirm the date: it corrected "the 5th" to "the 6th."
8. **On a phone menu, wait for the whole recording.** Then pick the option or say "receptionist."

## Where our caller fell short

- **At Silverstein Legal,** a person answered "Silverstein Legal" after the hold message, our caller made its request, and the line was hung up from the firm's end about a second later. We don't know why. Our caller didn't call back to try again.
- **At Oakland Law Group,** it talked over the recorded greeting. The phone system is voice-driven, so it took our caller's words as menu choices, rejected them twice, and dropped the call. That's on us, not the firm.
- **At Falkowski PLLC,** the line connected but we hear nothing from the firm's side. Our caller said "Hi, hello?" once and then stayed silent for almost two minutes, until the line was hung up from the firm's end. We don't know whether someone was there and couldn't hear us. Our caller should have spoken up again, or hung up and called back.
- **At the firm we booked,** it said "Hi, hello?" over the person's first greeting, so they had to say their name twice. And when the price dropped to $300, it didn't ask whether that fee still counts toward the bill, or who calls whom at 6:30.

After the Oakland call, the user added a line to the brief for the next call: if a phone menu or hold message answers, stay quiet until it finishes, then say "receptionist" or press 0, and wait on hold for a person. The next call is the one that booked.

## What to say: a script that worked

> Hi, this is [name]'s assistant. I'm calling to see if we could schedule a consultation with an attorney who handles [area of law]. Is that something your office could help with?

Then: "[Weekday evenings or weekends] is best. What's the consultation fee? Does it go toward the bill if [name] hires you? Could you do a 30-minute consult for [your limit] or less? Is that by phone, video or in person? The other party is [name], if you need it for a conflict check. Can you send a calendar invite to [email]?"
