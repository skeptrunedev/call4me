## How plowers price a long lakeshore or estate driveway

A standard Wayzata or Orono driveway is quoted one way; a 300-foot private drive down to the lake is quoted another. Here's what the companies that publish their terms say.

Past that, "the price climbs by roughly one pass for every additional 1,000 square feet," and "Private approaches longer than 150 feet are quoted on their own." - **Past a size cutoff, a custom quote.** [Chronos Snow Management](https://chronos-snow.com/) (rank 17) prices a normal drive per trip, with an early-bird "Same-Day plowing $70/trip (reg. $80)" at 2 inches or more, and sends "Parking lots and driveways over 2000 sq ft" to a custom quote.

Chronos also sells a Next Day plan that waits for 4 inches and clears "by the end of the next day," which it calls "Perfect for snowbirds."
- **Seasonal unlimited vs. a set number of visits.** West Metro's contract has "no cap on the number of service visits." Freshwater's owner said its price covers "ten pushes for the season." [Peter Doran Lawn & Landscaping](https://www.peterdoranlawn.com/wayzata-mn/snow-removal/) (rank 43) sells only seasonal flat rates, where a "1-inch trigger covers up to 17 qualifying snowfall events" and a "2-inch trigger up to 12 per season." - **Where the snow goes.** Freshwater: "If there's open ground beside the drive that can take a winter's worth of snow, storage costs nothing extra"; hauling it away "is priced separately."

## How we ranked them

One scoring method for every company, from public facts only:

- **Start from the rating, adjusted for how many reviews back it up.** We blend each company's star rating with a neutral 4.3, counted as if it were 10 extra reviews. West Metro's 5.0 from 457 reviews stays at 4.99. Lifecycle's 5.0 from 21 counts as 4.77. A 5.0 from 3 reviews would count as about 4.46, less than a 4.8 from 200. A company with no rating we could find starts at 4.0.
- **+0.30** for publishing an actual dollar price for the plowing itself. A pricing model like "seasonal flat rate" or "competitive rates" doesn't count, and neither does a fee for something else, like an add-on or a cancellation. Freshwater and Chronos get it for their driveway prices. West Metro doesn't: its $60 add-on fees are online, but its seasonal price isn't.
- **+0.15** for being based in Wayzata, Orono or Long Lake.
- **+0.15** for saying it's taking new customers, online or on our call. We read each company's own words to decide; having no "routes full" notice doesn't count.
- **+0.05** for dated activity in 2026 (a review, a post, a new BBB file).
- **-0.60** for saying its routes are full or it isn't taking new customers.
- **-0.40** for a serious red flag: signs of a broker or lead-generation front, a pattern of no-show or non-delivery complaints (or one dated 2024 or later), signs of fake reviews, or a listed number that's disconnected.

The calls only filled in facts that weren't online. Freshwater's "we have availability for driveways" added the taking-customers points to a company that was already first. A missed call took points from no one.

## Snow plowing near me in Wayzata and Orono: what to ask before you sign

Everything here comes from these companies' own pages, the two cities' pages and our calls.

1. **Ask whether the route still has room, now.** On November 27, Three Legacies' snow routes were already full, and Freshwater's page says, "Routes fill and then we're closed." Freshwater had room that day.
2. **Give your drive's real size.** Freshwater's base price covers about 20 by 60 feet, and approaches over 150 feet are quoted separately. Chronos sends anything over 2,000 square feet to a custom quote. Measure before you call.
3. **Ask how many visits the season covers.** West Metro's contract is unlimited. Freshwater's owner said its price covers ten pushes. Peter Doran's covers up to 12 snowfalls at a 2-inch trigger. Ask what the next storm costs after that.
4. **Ask about the trigger and the clock.** Most plans here start at 2 inches. Get the promise in hours: Freshwater says 10 hours after snow stops, West Metro guarantees 16.
5. **Ask who clears the end of the drive after the city plow.** [Orono plows](https://www.oronomn.gov/377/Snow-and-Ice) every street "curb-to-curb" when more than 2 inches falls and says that takes "8 to 10 hours," so its plow can pass after your contractor's. [Wayzata](https://www.wayzata.org/258/Snow-Emergency-Parking-Ban) bans street parking from 2 a.m. after a snowfall of 2 inches or more, and advises waiting "until the street is plowed before shoveling to the end of the driveway." Both cities say not to push driveway snow into the street. West Metro charges $60 per incident to clear what the city plow leaves; The Snow Fighter promises one free return.
6. **Ask what's extra.** Salting: $60 when West Metro decides it's needed; not included at Freshwater, per its owner. Hauling snow away: priced separately at Freshwater. Card payments: 3% at West Metro.
7. **Read the renewal and cancel terms.** West Metro's contract renews every year unless you cancel, with 7 days' notice and a prorated refund mid-season. David's Lawn Service's one BBB review is about auto-renewal notices and a cancel request that got no response.
8. **Use the form if that's the only door.** West Metro's phone line sends you to its website contact form and takes no voicemail.
