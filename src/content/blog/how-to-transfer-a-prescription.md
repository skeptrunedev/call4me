**The short answer:** to transfer a prescription, call the new pharmacy first and ask whether they have it **in stock**. If they do, they register you as a patient (name, date of birth, phone, address, insurance or self-pay) and request the transfer from your old pharmacy, which needs **the old pharmacy's name and phone number**. You can also call the old pharmacy yourself. Amazon Pharmacy's phone menu said the new pharmacy has to ask, but its customer care representative **transferred the prescription out to Walgreens in under five minutes** on the third call below, once we gave the store's name, ZIP code, address and phone number.

These are my own calls, made on Monday, September 28, 2026 by call4me, my AI phone assistant. Amazon Pharmacy had my prescription out of stock, and a Walgreens near me had it on the shelf. It took three calls in about half an hour:

1. **11:50 am PT, Walgreens:** is it in stock? Yes. They started registering me, and the call dropped partway through. (3 minutes 37 seconds)
2. **11:54 am PT, Walgreens again:** finished the registration, asked them to request the transfer, got a self-pay price. (8 minutes 26 seconds)
3. **12:18 pm PT, Amazon Pharmacy:** asked Amazon to send the prescription to that Walgreens and cancel the pending Amazon fill. Both done. (5 minutes 14 seconds, plus an automated survey after the goodbye)

My name, date of birth, phone number, address, the verification code, the medication and its strength, the store's street address and phone number, and the staff names are muted in the audio and shown in brackets in the transcripts. The calls end at the transfer: they don't show the pickup.

## How to transfer a prescription, step by step

1. **Call the new pharmacy and ask for the pharmacy.** Walgreens' phone menu asks "How can I help you today?" Saying "I need to transfer a prescription to your pharmacy" got the caller straight through, with the store's hours read out on the way.
2. **Ask if it's in stock before anything else.** It took the staff under half a minute to check. If the new pharmacy doesn't have it, a transfer just moves the problem.
3. **Register as a new patient.** Walgreens searched by phone number first ("I can't find you in our system"), then asked for insurance, the patient's name spelled out, date of birth, phone number, sex and home address. Self-pay was fine.
4. **Give them the old pharmacy's name and phone number.** "We're gonna have to get a hold of them, 'cause we're not connected with them." Walgreens said its pharmacist would call Amazon.
5. **Ask the price and when it will be ready.** Walgreens quoted "about $109.89 out of pocket" for self-pay. On timing: "I will check back in later on this afternoon, maybe around 4 to 5 o'clock."
6. **Or call the old pharmacy and have them send it.** Amazon Pharmacy verified the account with a code, its phone menu said it doesn't take transfer-out requests on that line, and then it put us through to a person who did the transfer.
7. **Cancel the pending fill at the old pharmacy** so you aren't filled or charged twice. Amazon's representative canceled it on the same call.

## What you need ready

- **For the new pharmacy:** the patient's full name (spelled out), date of birth, phone number, home address, and insurance, or "self-pay."
- **The old pharmacy's phone number.** Read it off their website or your account. Amazon Pharmacy's customer care number, the one our third call dialed, was (855) 745-5725. Amazon's [page for prescribers](https://pharmacy.amazon.com/prescribers) also lists a separate prescriber and pharmacy line, (855) 206-3605, which is worth giving the new pharmacy.
- **For Amazon Pharmacy:** the mobile number on the account, and access to that phone or the account email. Amazon sends a six digit code there before it will talk about the account.
- **The new pharmacy's details:** store name, street address, ZIP code and phone number. Amazon's representative asked for the ZIP, confirmed it was a Walgreens, then asked for the full address. Our caller added the phone number before the transfer went through.

## How long does it take to transfer a prescription?

On the phone, not long. Amazon's representative transferred the prescription out 4 minutes 33 seconds into the call, about 75 seconds after picking up. The whole chain, from the first Walgreens call to Amazon canceling its fill, took about 33 minutes, and most of that was a dropped call, a hold and the time between calls.

Before the new pharmacy can fill it, the paperwork has to arrive. Walgreens put it plainly: "It does take a bit of time for us to call Amazon and get it transferred over here," and pointed to "later on this afternoon, maybe around 4 to 5 o'clock." These recordings end there, so they don't show when the prescription was ready.

## What to say: scripts that worked

To the new pharmacy:

> Hi, I need to transfer a prescription to your pharmacy. Could you check if you have [medication and strength] in stock? ... Could you transfer the prescription in from [old pharmacy]? Their number is [number].

If they need to register you, give your name, date of birth, phone, address and insurance (or "self-pay"), then ask: "Do you happen to know the self-pay price?" and "When do you think it'd be ready?"

To the old pharmacy:

> I was hoping you can note on [patient]'s account that [patient] authorizes transferring the [medication] prescription to the Walgreens at [street address], [city]. ... Their phone is [number].

The second line got the transfer done, not just noted. Then: "Can you cancel the pending fill for it so it doesn't get double-filled?"
