**The short answer:** yes, a hotel will usually hold a package for a guest, and most other order and delivery problems can be fixed with one phone call too, if you call whoever has the package right now and have the tracking or order number and the exact name on the label ready. All four hotels in these calls hold packages for guests, and two of them already had one waiting at the desk. One, in Birmingham, UK, set rules: guests with a reservation only, no earlier than one day before arrival, and tell the front desk the delivery details first. It never said it would hold the parcel the call was about.

Between October 25 and November 23, 2026, call4me users' AI phone assistants made 33 calls about orders and deliveries: to four hotels, UPS, FedEx, a Canada Post counter, a delivery app, two phone carriers and four stores. What got done:

1. **Hotels.** A hotel in Kyoto confirmed that three suitcases sent ahead by Yamato had arrived. A New York hotel still had a guest's boxed FedEx shipment at the desk for pickup. A Hyatt Regency near Chicago said "Yes, we do," and an AC Hotel in Birmingham, UK, explained its rules (recording below).
2. **Stopping a shipment.** UPS sent a T-Mobile device order back to T-Mobile before it was delivered, in one 16-minute call, once the user had the tracking number.
3. **Holding off a delivery.** UPS's phone system refused, citing "restrictions set by the sender." A UPS agent then called the local facility and got the next attempt moved to after the user was home. Shipt moved a delivery back two days.
4. **Checking on a package or order.** A Canada Post counter inside a Rexall checked its unscanned parcels by hand. Xfinity Mobile said an iPhone order was "currently pending" and due in one to two days.
5. **Canceling and returning.** Filson couldn't cancel an order it had already handed to USPS, but said refusing delivery would get a refund and that it would waive its $8 return fee. Apple's phone line announced its holiday return window. Two Austin stores answered return questions about worn-once shoes and a shirt with its tag cut off.

These calls were made for 13 different call4me users, to businesses in the US, Canada, the UK and Japan. Our caller didn't pay for anything on any of them. Guests' names and staff names are muted in the audio and shown in brackets in the transcripts, and tracking, order, case and confirmation numbers are left out. Policies, fees and wait times are what each business said on that day, and they can change any day.

## Can I send a package to a hotel? What 4 front desks said

Yes, all four hotels in these calls accept packages for guests. The difference is in the rules, and only one hotel spelled them out.

- **AC Hotel by Marriott, Birmingham, UK.** The call was about a guest's small John Lewis parcel. Asked whether the guest had to be registered, the desk said: "Yes, you have to be a guest." First the reservation has to be made, then the guest has to "contact and give us the details of the delivery." The label needs "the name and the reservation details." Space is limited ("Because we have limited space for packages"), so the desk asked how big it was, and our caller said shoe box size. Asked how long it holds parcels: "We cannot hold it for longer. It has to be like one or two days before arrival." Asked whether it can arrive before check-in: "It has to be one day before arrival. Otherwise we won't accept it." And the fee: "no, there's no fee."
- **Hyatt Regency near Chicago.** "Yes, we do. We do hold your package when you check in, then you can ask for it." The agent mentioned the hotel has its own FedEx ("we had FedEx here in a hotel") and said the question of how early a package can arrive "has to go to one of our FedEx colleague." The agent's own guesses ran from "a week" to "Like two days" to "Or three days," then they offered to check, and the call ended from our side before they could.
- **Ameritania Hotel, New York.** This one went the other way. A guest had left a FedEx box with the front desk, with a prepaid label on it. The desk still had it ("Yes, we actually do have it"), the label was attached, and the pickup time didn't matter: "there's always somebody at the desk." Separately, FedEx's automated pickup line said it was "unable to schedule a pickup on the account associated with this shipment" and transferred us to a rep, who booked a pickup at the hotel's front desk for that afternoon, "Up until 6 p.m." The fee: "the pickup, ma'am, is going to be covered, ma'am, by the label itself."
- **KOKO HOTEL, Kyoto, Japan (in Japanese).** A guest had sent three suitcases ahead by Yamato Transport. The front desk asked for the reservation name, spelled out in the alphabet, and the check-in date, then checked for about two minutes: "三点スーツケース届いてます" (the three suitcases have arrived). Where to collect them: "フロントのチェックインの際にあの、お渡しします" (we'll hand them over at the front desk when you check in). More calls to Japanese shops are in [our post on checking store stock in Japan](/blog/check-store-stock-in-japan).

**The Birmingham hotel never said it would hold the John Lewis parcel.** It gave the rules above and nothing more. The user's assistant called twice more that evening to ask whether the desk would accept and hold that parcel, and both saved transcripts end mid-question, before anyone at the desk answered. So all we have from this hotel is its general rules, not a yes. If you're shipping to a hotel, call about your specific delivery and get the answer before you hang up.

## How to cancel an order, and what happens once it has shipped

Call the store's order line as early as you can, with the order number and the name on the order. In the call below, once the order was packed and handed to the carrier, the store couldn't cancel it, even though tracking hadn't moved. The way out from there was refusing the delivery, and the refund came as a return.

**What the store said.** On Monday, November 23, a user wanted a Filson online order canceled. The menu: "For order or refund support, say or press one." The rep took the order number and the name on the order and checked: "Let me see if we are able to cancel it." About 30 seconds later: "it does look like this order has already been packaged up and sent with USPS. So we're not gonna be able to cancel it at this time."

**When it's too late.** Tracking still showed only a label created on November 20, so our caller asked whether the order had physically left the warehouse. "Yes, it has. USPS can take three to five days to scan. They don't scan it when they first pick it up. They scan it once it gets to their main hub." So a "label created" status doesn't mean you can still cancel. Ask whether it has physically shipped.

**What happens once it has shipped.** Refuse the delivery: "it would go back to us. Once we receive it back, we could um we'll process it as a return," and "we would refund back to the original form of payment." The shipping charge wasn't refundable, "because that is something that is paid directly to USPS," but the rep offered to waive the return fee: "the eight dollar fee we can waive um just because we-you attempted to cancel the order." There was no case number ("we don't have a case number"). The rep said they'd note the order and send a confirmation email.

**If the store can't stop it, the carrier might.** One user asked T-Mobile to cancel a device order. It took 13 minutes to reach a person, and our saved transcript ends before anything was canceled. UPS, on the other hand, sent the package back to T-Mobile before delivery once the user had the tracking number. Those calls are in the next section.

After almost nine minutes on hold: "finally they are agreed to deliver the package on the third of November," and "after third in November they will do an attempt." The agent gave a case number.

## When the FedEx phone menu loops

One user in New York had a FedEx Home Delivery package marked as a failed delivery on Friday, October 30, though they said they'd buzzed the driver in. FedEx's main line kept reading the same status ("We were not able to leave the shipment because no one was there to accept it," and the shipment "isn't eligible to be redirected to a FedEx location of your choice") and kept asking "Will you or someone be present to accept this shipment" instead of transferring. Three calls, about 17 minutes, no person.

What worked was calling a **FedEx Office store**. Its menu offers **8** "To speak with a team member for questions." The first team member said, "Sure, I could transfer you to a live rep," and that call ended on hold. On the second, the team member transferred us again, and after two hand-offs a FedEx rep picked up about 11 minutes in. The rep sent a follow-up to the delivery facility ("Oh, they just acknowledged"), asked them "to at least knock on the door," and gave the next attempt: "from 8 a.m until 12 noon." The rep also said: "we experience a high volume of packages right now," and for packages that need a signature, "we will exhaust three attempts first before it will be sent back to the merchant." ## Tips before Black Friday and Christmas

- **Call whoever has the package.** A front desk or a post office counter can walk to the back and look. A carrier's automated line mostly reads you the tracking screen.
- **Have the tracking number before you call the carrier.** UPS couldn't search by name and address. If you don't have it, the seller does.
- **Shipping to a hotel: book first, then call the desk.** Ask whether you need a reservation, how to address it (AC Birmingham wanted the guest name and reservation details), how early it can arrive (one day before arrival there), and whether there's a fee (there wasn't). Then get a yes for your specific parcel.
- **"Restrictions set by the sender" isn't the last word.** The UPS automated line said no. An agent who called the facility got the delivery pushed back.
- **Return to sender works before delivery.** UPS did it in one call. Call back later and ask whether the request is active, the way our caller did.
- **If an order has shipped, ask about refusing it.** Filson couldn't cancel, but explained the refund for a refused package and offered to waive its $8 return fee.
- **For returns, ask before you drive over.** Nordstrom looked up purchases by card or phone number with no receipt, and STAG took a shirt back without its tag.
- **Menus that lead to a person:** AC Hotel Birmingham offers 2 for the hotel team and 5 for all other queries. The Hyatt and Ameritania menus offer 0. At the Rexall it's 3, then 2 for the post office counter, and FedEx Office offers 8 for a team member. At UPS, "I have a question" then "Agent" worked, and at Shipt, answer the AI's first question.
- **Odd hours can work.** Shipt rescheduled a delivery at 5:08 am Central on a Sunday, and the Ameritania front desk is open around the clock.

## What to say: scripts that worked

For a hotel:

> Hi, I'm calling about a package for a guest, [name], who checks in on [date]. Can the front desk accept it and hold it until check-in? How should it be addressed, how early can it arrive, and is there a fee?

To cancel an order:

> I'd like to cancel order [number], under [name]. If it has already shipped, has it physically left your warehouse? If I refuse the delivery, will you refund it to my original payment method, and can you waive the return fee?

For a carrier, to stop a package:

> I'm calling for the receiver. The tracking number is [number]. It hasn't been delivered yet. Can you put a hold on delivery and return it to the sender?

To hold off a redelivery:

> It just had its final delivery attempt, and [name] is away until [date]. Can you check with the facility and attempt delivery on [date] or later, so it isn't returned?
