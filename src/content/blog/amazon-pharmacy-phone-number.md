**The short answer:** the Amazon Pharmacy phone number for customers is **855-745-5725** (Customer Care). Doctors and other pharmacies use a separate line, **855-206-3605**, open 8 am to 9 pm ET on weekdays and 10 am to 8 pm ET on weekends. Both are listed on [Amazon Pharmacy's page for prescribers](https://pharmacy.amazon.com/prescribers).

If your prescription is stuck in **"pharmacist review"**, call Customer Care and ask a person to look at it. The automated assistant offered us an "expedited review", then said it had no tool to do one and connected us to clinical customer care. The representative there said she'd **mark the prescriptions as important**, and that "usually once that happens, they're done within like an hour." Our "ready to order" email arrived 48 minutes after we dialed.

## What happens when you call Amazon Pharmacy, step by step

1. **Dial 855-745-5725.** The greeting names Amazon Pharmacy and PillPack, gives the 911 notice, and says all calls are recorded.
2. **"Are you an Amazon Pharmacy customer?"** Say yes.
3. **"Is the mobile number you are calling from associated with your account?"** Answer honestly. If you're calling from another phone, say no and give the mobile number on the account. Saying yes from a phone that isn't on the account gets you "I don't see an account with this phone number."
4. **Enter the verification code.** Amazon sends a six digit code to the email and mobile number on the account and waits on the line while you find it. Have your email or that phone in reach before you call.
5. **Say what you need in one sentence.** The automated assistant listens for the request: "Three prescriptions have been stuck in pharmacist review for over 24 hours. I'd like them moved along today."
6. **Get to a person.** For us, the assistant offered an expedited review, then said it couldn't do one and transferred us. The quoted wait was about a minute; a person answered 20 seconds later.
7. **Ask them to mark the prescriptions as important.** That was the phrase the representative used, and the result came in under an hour.
8. **Don't expect a price yet.** Prices aren't shown until after the first pharmacist check ("the PV1 process"). Amazon emails you when the order is ready to place.

## What "pharmacist review" means, and how long it takes

New prescriptions at Amazon Pharmacy go through a pharmacist check before you can order them, and the status reads "Prescription received and under pharmacist review" until it's done. The representative called the step "the PV1 process"; prices and ordering unlock after it.

How long it takes varies. Ours had been in review for days with an estimated ready date further out still. When the representative looked, she saw no problem on the prescriptions ("I'm not showing any issues. That's weird."), marked them important, and they cleared 42 minutes after she said goodbye. If yours has been in review for more than a day with nothing asked of you, it's worth a call.

If a prescription clears review but Amazon doesn't have it in stock, ask them to transfer it to a pharmacy that does. Amazon's phone menu says it doesn't take transfer-out requests, then routes you to a person who can; we walk through that call in [how to transfer a prescription](/blog/how-to-transfer-a-prescription).

## What to say: a script that worked

To the automated assistant:

> Hi, I'm trying to make sure three prescriptions under [name] move out of pharmacist review so they can be ordered today. They've been stuck for over 24 hours.

To the representative:

> I'm calling because [name] has three prescriptions still under pharmacist review for over a day. Can you see what's blocking them and help move them along today?

If they don't see a problem, ask them to mark the prescriptions as important, and ask whether anything is needed from you or your doctor.
