**The short answer:** to dispute a charge on a Discover card by phone, call **1-800-DISCOVER (1-800-347-2683)**. Discover's [contact page](https://www.discover.com/credit-cards/help-center/contact-us/) lists it for U.S. customer service and says it has "live customer service agents" 24/7. On our call, saying you want to dispute a charge gets you asked for **the last four digits of your Social Security number**, then put through to a **live person in billing disputes**. That took **2 minutes 3 seconds**.

**Calling alone may not protect you.** Discover's own [cardmember agreement](https://www.discover.com/content/dam/discover/en_us/credit-cards/card-portfolio/docs/cma/Prime_Cardmember_Agreement_063025.pdf) says, under "Your Billing Rights": "You must notify us of any potential errors in writing or electronically. You may call us, but if you do we are not necessarily required to investigate any potential errors and you may have to pay the amount in question." So:

1. **Call 1-800-347-2683** if you want to talk it through. Have your card number and the last four of your SSN ready.
2. **Also send written notice within 60 days** after the error appeared on your statement, at **discover.com/billingerrornotice** or by mail to **Discover, P.O. Box 30421, Salt Lake City, UT 84130-0421**.
3. **Include** your name and account number, the dollar amount, and "what you believe is wrong and why you believe it is a mistake."
4. **Discover then has 30 days** to tell you it got your notice, and **90 days** to correct the error or explain why the bill is right.

**What happened on our call:** we said up front that we didn't have a Discover account and only wanted to know how the process works. The billing disputes representative said: "I wouldn't be able to provide any information unless I'm able to pull up an account." She didn't answer how long you have, what to have ready, or how long it takes. So this page takes those answers from Discover's cardmember agreement instead, and says so.

We called on Saturday, October 24, 2026, at 6:22 pm Eastern, with [call4me](/) asking the questions. We gave no card or personal details. This page is what we heard and what Discover's documents say.

## The Discover phone tree, step by step

This is the menu as it played on October 24, 2026, calling 1-800-347-2683 with no card.

1. **Call 1-800-347-2683.** "Thanks for calling Discover. Just so you know, this call may be monitored and recorded, and your voice may be used for verification." Spanish: "oprima el número dos."
2. **"Now tell me, how can I help you?"** Say what you need in a sentence. We said we wanted general information on how the dispute process works.
3. **"Very well, what's your question? If I can't help, I'll transfer you to an account manager who can."**
4. **We asked how a cardmember disputes a charge by phone.** "Okay, to get information on the transaction you're disputing, please say or enter the last four digits of your social security number."
5. **We asked for a representative.** "I'll put you through to an account manager. Please say or enter the last four digits of your social security number."
6. **We said we didn't have it.** "If you don't remember it, just say so." We said "I don't remember it", then "No, I don't have it" when it offered to wait.
7. **"In this case, please hold while I put you through to an account manager."** An ADA accommodations message plays.
8. **A person in billing disputes answers,** 2 minutes 3 seconds in, and asks for your name, then your card number.

Saying you don't have or don't remember the SSN digits did not stop us reaching a person. The person then needed the account.

## What to have ready

From the call and the cardmember agreement:

- **Your Discover card number.** The representative asked for it first.
- **The last four digits of your Social Security number.** The menu asks for it as soon as you mention a dispute.
- **The charge:** merchant, date and dollar amount.
- **What's wrong and why,** in a sentence or two. The written notice asks for exactly that.
- **Anything from the merchant:** receipts, emails, return tracking.

## What Discover's cardmember agreement says

The "Your Billing Rights" section of Discover's agreement (the 2025 version linked above, issued by "Discover, a division of Capital One, N.A.") is the standard Fair Credit Billing Act notice. The parts that matter for a dispute:

- **Where to write:** "Discover, P.O. Box 30421, Salt Lake City, UT 84130-0421," or "on the Web: https://discover.com/billingerrornotice."
- **Deadline:** "Within 60 days after the error appeared on your statement."
- **Phone isn't enough:** "You may call us, but if you do we are not necessarily required to investigate any potential errors and you may have to pay the amount in question."
- **While Discover investigates:** it "cannot try to collect the amount in question, or report you as delinquent on that amount." You still owe the rest of your balance, and Discover "can apply any unpaid amount against your credit limit."
- **If Discover made a mistake:** you don't pay the amount "or any interest or other fees related to that amount."
- **Unhappy with a purchase** (not a billing mistake): you may not have to pay the rest if you tried in good faith with the merchant, the purchase was over $50, and it was in your home state or within 100 miles of your mailing address. That notice goes to **P.O. Box 30945, Salt Lake City, UT 84130-0945** or the same web page.

Discover's contact page also says that if your account has moved to Capital One, you log in at capitalone.com or use the Capital One app. We also recorded [a Capital One dispute call](/blog/capital-one-dispute-charge).
