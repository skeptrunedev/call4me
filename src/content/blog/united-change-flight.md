**The short answer:** United change flight rules depend on your ticket. A United reservations agent told us that on a **Standard Economy** ticket, and on United First, you can change "anytime" and pay **the fare difference only**. **Basic Economy** "is the most restrictive ticket": she said a **flexibility fee plus the fare difference** applies, with the fee depending on the route. united.com says something different about Basic Economy (more [below](#what-united-com-says)), so check your own ticket before you rely on either.

The United customer service number for reservations and changes is **1-800-864-8331**, open "24 hours daily" ([united.com](https://www.united.com/en/us/fly/help/contact-reservations.html)). In Spanish it's 1-800-426-5561. This got us to a person:

1. When the voice assistant asks why you're calling, **say "flight change fee"**. Keep it short: a long sentence got "Sorry, I didn't understand."
2. **Press 1** if you're traveling within the United States and Mexico. Otherwise, press 2.
3. **Press 2** to skip the survey.
4. **Stay on the line.** Press 9 only if you need rebooking help for disrupted travel.

The menu estimated "approximately 10 to 15 minutes". A person picked up **19 minutes 27 seconds** after dialing: 18 minutes 10 seconds after "Please hold while I transfer you to an agent". The agent told us:

- **Standard Economy and United First can be changed any time.** You pay the fare difference only.
- **Basic Economy is "the most restrictive ticket".** "There's actually a flexibility fee and then a fare difference that would apply." The flexibility fee depends on whether the flight is domestic or international.
- **Same-day changes:** Basic Economy pays the flexibility fee plus the fare difference. Standard Economy and up pay the "flexibility fee only". You can make the change online or by phone, up to one hour before departure.
- **No extra fee for changing by phone**, "unless it is a new reservation with a booking service fee."
- **There's no faster way to reach an agent.** "It's actually depending on the queue."

We called on Thursday, October 1, 2026, at about 4:09 pm Eastern, with [call4me](/) asking the questions. This page is what we heard.

2. **"How can I help you? Please say in a few words why you are calling us today."** Our caller said "I just want to ask about changing a flight." The menu answered "I can help you with your question" and asked for a bit more.
3. **Say "flight change fee".** Our caller's longer answer got "Sorry, I didn't understand", with the hint "You can say things like book a flight or get flight status." "Flight change fee" got "Please hold while I transfer you to an agent who can assist you."
4. **A recording notice**, then: "If you are traveling within the United States and Mexico, please press one. Otherwise, press two." We pressed 1.
5. **A survey offer.** "To participate in the survey, please press one. Otherwise, press two." We pressed 2.
6. **"All of our agents are temporarily busy."** "Your wait time is approximately 10 to 15 minutes." Hold music, then an ad for the United Explorer Card, then more music.
7. **"We sincerely apologize for the wait."** About 4 minutes 10 seconds after the wait estimate, it offered a "no wait voice assisted experience" for rebooking after disrupted travel: "Press nine to immediately be connected to this experience. Otherwise, stay on the line." We stayed on the line.
8. **An agent answers.** About 13 more minutes of hold music (the full hold is in the recording), then: "Hi, thank you for calling reservations desk." Her first question was for the confirmation number.

## What the agent told us

**It depends on the ticket.** "With regards to the changes on the reservation, it actually depends on the ticket that you purchase."

**Basic Economy.** "If you purchase a basic economy ticket, it is the most restrictive ticket. So there's actually a flexibility fee and then a fare difference that would apply upon doing the changes." The flexibility fee "is depending on the route, if it is international or if it is like a domestic flight." She didn't give an amount, and we didn't ask for one.

**Standard Economy.** "Changes are permitted, so you can change it anytime," paying "the fare difference only."

**United First.** "It is also the same. It is changeable anytime" with the fare difference.

**Same-day changes.** She asked whether we meant the same day as booking or the same day as departure. For the day of departure: "Flexibility fee and fare difference will apply for basic economy ticket. And then flexibility fee only if it is standard economy up to the higher cabin." The change can be made "one hour prior to the departure", "either online or via phone call."

**By phone or online.** "There is no fee for changes on the phone unless it is a new reservation with a booking service fee. But with regards to the changes, no changes fee, ma'am."

**Getting through faster.** "It's actually depending on the queue, because, you know, we also have a lot of customers also waiting on the line."

## What united.com says

We checked the United Airlines change flight pages on united.com on October 1, 2026, the day of the call.

**Economy and up, the United change fee.** United's [flexible booking page](https://www.united.com/en/us/fly/travel/trip-planning/flexible-booking-options.html) says: "If you're flying on an Economy, Economy Plus® or a premium cabin ticket, your change fees are waived for the following: Domestic flights within the U.S., Puerto Rico and U.S. Virgin Islands", plus flights between the U.S. and Mexico, between the U.S. and the Caribbean, and international flights that start in the U.S. Its FAQ says: "You can change your flight as many times as you want without change fees. But, you will have to pay any fare differences that apply each time you change." That matches what the agent told us.

**Basic Economy. This is where they disagree.** The same page says: "If you're flying on a Basic Economy ticket, you can't change your flight unless you upgrade to Economy or a premium cabin first." The agent told us a flexibility fee plus the fare difference applies. We're showing both. We don't know which one applies to your ticket, so confirm with United before you count on changing a Basic Economy fare.

**Same-day changes.** United's [flight change page](https://www.united.com/en/us/fly/travel/trip-planning/flight-change.html) says Premier members "may be able to get another flight for free", and: "All other travelers may be able to get another flight within 24 hours of the original flight. You may have to pay a price difference even if the same cabin is available." Flying standby on an earlier flight the same day is free to join. United's [list of optional service charges](https://www.united.com/en/us/fly/products/upgrades-and-optional-service-charges.html) prices "Same-day flight changes" at "Up to $75 per traveler, depending on Premier® status". The agent said Standard Economy and up pay the flexibility fee only. The website says you may also pay a price difference.

**Changing by phone.** The flight change page says: "You can also change your flight over the phone, but you might be charged a fee." The agent told us there's no fee for changes by phone. The charges page lists ticketing through a United agent at "$25 by phone, $30 at a City Ticket Office, $50 in person at the airport", and the [reservations directory](https://www.united.com/en/us/fly/help/contact-reservations.html) says: "To book with an agent, there's a $25 fee by phone". That fits what the agent said about a booking service fee on a new reservation. The charges page also lists "Other flight changes and cancellations" at "From $0 to $1,000 per traveler, based on applicable fare rules".

## What to have ready

- **Your confirmation number.** It was the agent's first question. Without one she could only explain the general rules.
- **Your ticket type.** Basic Economy, Standard Economy or a premium cabin changes everything above.
- **Whether the flight is domestic or international.** The agent said the Basic Economy flexibility fee depends on it.
- **The new flight you want,** and, for a same-day change, enough time: the agent said at least one hour before departure.
- **Time.** Our hold was about 18 minutes, against an estimate of 10 to 15.

## Things that don't work

On October 1, 2026, at "Please say in a few words why you are calling us today":

- **A long sentence.** "I want to ask what the fee is to change a flight, like for basic economy versus regular economy" got "Sorry, I didn't understand."
- **A general one.** "I just want to ask about changing a flight" got "In a few words, please tell me a bit more."

Three words, "flight change fee", went straight through to the agent queue.
