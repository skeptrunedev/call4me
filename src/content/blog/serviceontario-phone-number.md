**The short answer:** the ServiceOntario phone number is **416-235-2999**, or toll-free **1-800-387-3445**. For TTY, it's **416-325-3408** or toll-free **1-800-268-7095**. ServiceOntario's [contact page](https://www.ontario.ca/feedback/contact-us?id=8330) lists all four and says: "Our call centres are open Monday to Friday: 8:30 a.m. to 5:00 p.m." That's the ServiceOntario contact number our customer's assistant dialed, and the voice menu on the line says the same: "A representative will be available if needed, between the hours of eight thirty AM and five PM, Monday to Friday." So ServiceOntario hours by phone are weekdays only.

A voice system answers first, not a person. Two ways got through to a representative:

1. **Say the whole problem in one sentence.** On October 13, 2026, "I'm calling about a suspended driver's licence and a licence plate that didn't renew" went straight to the transfer message. **A representative answered 2 minutes 7 seconds into the call.**
2. **Or say "representative".** On October 14, saying only "suspended driver's licence" got a recorded answer about renewing an expired licence. Saying "representative" right after that transferred the call. A representative answered at 2:27.

Both transfers play a survey offer (press 1 if you want it, otherwise wait) and a recorded notice about respectful conduct, about 45 seconds in all, before the person picks up.

What ServiceOntario representatives told a call4me customer's assistant about a licence suspended for unpaid fines, and how to pay the reinstatement fee:

- **They won't open your file unless you're on the call.** Both representatives asked whether the licence holder was there and declined to go further without him. The second one took the licence number and name, then said: "the card holder is currently not with you, and there is no way for me to confirm."
- **You pay the fine plus a reinstatement fee.** The second representative said the reinstatement fee is **$281**. Ontario's [reinstatement page](https://www.ontario.ca/page/reinstate-suspended-drivers-licence) lists the same "$281 reinstatement fee" and says you must pay any outstanding fines first.
- **"It is valid right away"** once the fine and fee are paid, according to the first representative.
- **Any ServiceOntario location can take the payment in person,** according to both representatives.
- **Online payment got two different answers.** The first representative said you can pay online if you have the suspension number from the suspension letter that's mailed to you. The second said usually not, once a reinstatement fee is on the account, and pointed to a branch instead. The reinstatement page says the fee can be paid "online or in person at a ServiceOntario centre".
- **A plate won't renew automatically with unpaid fines on file.** Once they're paid, "the easiest way to do that is actually to renew the license plate," and the renewal asks you to pay what's outstanding.

## Finding the unpaid ticket: Toronto Court Services

ServiceOntario wouldn't say which ticket caused the suspension. So on October 14, nine minutes before the second ServiceOntario call, the assistant called the City of Toronto's court services line, **416-338-7320**. The City's [unpaid fines page](https://www.toronto.ca/services-payments/tickets-fines-penalties/courts/pay-your-provincial-offence/collection-of-unpaid-provincial-offence-fines/) gives that number for the Court Services Collection Unit ("please press four for collections") and warns: "Your driver's licence could be suspended for certain offences (there is a fee for reinstatement)."

Its menu offers press 3 for a call centre representative ("please have your ticket and or driver's license ready") and press 4 for anyone contacted by a collection agency. The recording doesn't show which key our caller pressed. A representative answered at 2:54.

Unlike ServiceOntario, this office looked the record up for the assistant, using the licence number, the name and a date of birth. The representative said:

- **The licence is "currently suspended" because of one ticket with an outstanding payment.** She read out the case number (muted in the recording).
- **The amount owing was $458,** and it was payable online.
- **There was no record of the ticket the customer expected.** The customer thought it was a June 2026 handheld phone ticket. The representative found two Toronto South cases from August 2025 instead, one paid and one with a balance.

That's what the assistant then took to ServiceOntario.

## What these calls did not settle

- **Nothing was paid, and the licence wasn't reinstated.** These were information calls.
- **No suspension number.** ServiceOntario didn't read any record, so the suspension number, an itemized total and any other requirements on the file were never confirmed.
- **The online payment question is open.** The two representatives contradicted each other. Whether the court case number alone works for paying online was never answered.
- **The $281 was suggested by our caller.** The second representative confirmed it, and the website lists it, but the assistant asked "is the reinstatement fee two eighty one" rather than asking what it was.
- **What to bring to a branch wasn't answered.** The second representative's reply moved on to medical suspensions for commercial drivers, and the call ended from the customer's side while she was still speaking.
- **No written authorization route.** The assistant was meant to ask how a licence holder could authorize someone else to call. The call ended before that came up.
- **Whether plate renewal was blocked by this ticket, or by tolls or insurance, wasn't checked.**

## The ServiceOntario phone tree, step by step

This is the menu as it played on October 13 and 14, 2026, calling 416-235-2999.

1. **"Thank you for calling ServiceOntario. Bonjour."** Then: "For service in English, please stay on the line. Pour obtenir des renseignements en français, appuyez sur le deux."
2. **A renewal message.** "To learn more about online renewals, appointment booking, locations, or to set up renewal reminders, visit Ontario dot CA slash ServiceOntario, or stay on the line."
3. **"Welcome to our new system. It responds to your voice. Just ask a question. We won't ask for any personal information, such as your name, date of birth, or card numbers."** Then the hours, a recording notice and "How can I help you?"
4. **Say what you need, in a full sentence, or say "representative".** A short answer like "suspended driver's licence" can get a recorded reply instead (see below).
5. **Survey offer.** "If you would like to participate in a short satisfaction survey immediately after your call, press one now. Otherwise, please stay on the line."
6. **A recording and conduct notice,** about 30 seconds.
7. **A representative.** "Thank you for calling ServiceOntario. My name is [name]. How can I help you?"

## Things that don't work

- **Saying "suspended driver's licence" on its own.** On October 14 it got a recorded answer about expired licences ("If your license has been expired less than one year, you can renew your license online") instead of a transfer. "Representative" worked right after it.
- **Calling for someone else.** Both ServiceOntario representatives declined to look at the record without the licence holder on the line, even though the assistant said he'd authorized it.

## ServiceOntario license plate renewal after unpaid fines

The first representative said: "the province recently introduced automatic renewal of license plates. So as long as your vehicle is insured and you have no outstanding fines or tolls your license plate will automatically renew." Once fines or tolls are paid, "that does renew the license plate right away as well," and the renewal "will ask you to pay any outstanding amounts."

Ontario's [plate renewal page](https://www.ontario.ca/page/renew-your-licence-plate) agrees on the conditions ("you must have valid insurance and no outstanding fines or tolls") and adds that once those are resolved, "you must renew your licence plate manually." So for a ServiceOntario plate renewal blocked by a defaulted fine, plan to pay it and then renew the plate yourself.
