**The short answer:** to check if a store has something in stock, call the store, ask for the department that carries the item, read them the item number (SKU, UPC, part number or style number), and ask a person to look on the shelf and in the back. A website's "in stock" badge and a store's automated phone line don't look at the shelf, and in the calls below they were wrong, out of date, or unable to answer more than once. The in store availability answers you can act on came from a person who walked to the shelf or looked the item up in the store's own system.

That's what call4me users' AI assistants have been doing all fall. From October 17 to November 25, 2026, **293 calls from 45 call4me users asked a store whether it had something in stock**, and 225 calls from 38 users asked a store to hold something for pickup. Their assistants called about capacitors, tequila, sweaters, energy drinks, tires, wiper blades, a dog clipper blade, houseplants, a video game and an RC jet.

Below are five of those hunts, call by call, each one a single call4me user:

1. **A hardware part in Pine Bluff, Arkansas.** Lowe's said capacitors are online only. A local supply house walked to the shelf and found three.
2. **A specific tequila in Las Vegas.** Three stores had the regular bottle, not the one asked for. The fourth had two at $89.99.
3. **A seasonal energy drink in Jacksonville.** 18 calls in 8 minutes, no one had it, and the stores explained when the next truck comes.
4. **Sweaters, a T-shirt and a NAS in the Bay Area and Mississauga.** Product codes got fast answers, and one store said its website price was "not updated."
5. **A tire the night before Thanksgiving in Rochester, New York.** Walmart's AI pointed to the app. A person in the Auto Care Center found one left, and wouldn't hold it.

Then a table of one-call checks, what didn't work, what stores said about restocks, and tips for doing this yourself before Black Friday and Christmas.

Every call here was made by a call4me user's own AI assistant. The users' names and phone numbers and the store staff's names are left out of the text, and the user's name is muted in the one recording where our caller said it. Our caller didn't buy anything or give a card in any of these calls. Prices and stock are what each store said on that day, and they can change any day, especially this week. All times are the store's local time.

## In store availability: why the "in stock" badge isn't enough

The calls above and a few more show the same gap between what a site or system says and what's on the shelf:

## What didn't work

**Automated lines that can't see the shelf.** Walmart's AI agent offers a Walmart.com link first. Five of the stock-check calls we read for this post went to Walmart stores. In all five, Walmart's AI agent tried to transfer our caller to a store associate. Two got a person and three got "We're currently experiencing unusually high call volume." DICK'S and Roots send you to the website or app from the phone menu. Verizon's assistant said "Verizon stores do not publish real-time local inventory."

**Stores that need an item number.** Best Buy's assistant asks for "the item's seven or eight digit SKU number." Lowe's virtual assistant said, "If you can share the item number or model number from the capacitor, I can check that exact part." Roots couldn't check without the style number. Find the number on the store's own site before you call.

**Stores that won't check by phone.** The UK reseller asked for an email. A GEO game store in Tokyo plays a recorded message saying it doesn't take phone questions about game stock (more in [checking store stock in Japan](/blog/check-store-stock-in-japan)). A Lennox parts store in Little Rock: "Only way you gon get it if you have an account sir." A costume rental shop in West Palm Beach could describe what it had but not sizes: "I have no idea. I gotta come in and try it."

**Stores that won't talk to an AI caller.** Several people asked whether our caller was a robot or an AI, and it said yes, it's an AI assistant. Most kept helping: Wholesale Electric in Pine Bluff asked "Is this a robot?" and still pointed us to the store that had the capacitor. At an electrical distributor in Little Rock, the second person on the line said "It's a robocall," and the call ended.

**Lines nobody answered.** GameStop's recording, twice in a row: "At this time, all store associates are currently assisting other customers. Please call back in a few." The third call got a person. More GameStop calls are in [PS5 in stock near me](/blog/ps5-in-stock-near-me). Three Target stores in the Houston area never connected our caller to a person, and 99 Ranch in Houston played "All of our representatives are currently busy" until the call ended.

**Holds on hot items.** Some stores that had a hot item wouldn't set it aside. The Walmart tire above. Kohl's automated line on NeeDoh: "We are unable to honor customer holds or reservations for these items" ([NeeDoh and holiday toys](/blog/needoh-in-stock)). A Shoppers Drug Mart in Waterloo, Ontario, on a Pokémon collection: "we do have one in stock, but we don't put it on hold" ([Pokémon 30th Anniversary cards](/blog/pokemon-30th-anniversary-cards-in-stock)). ## How to check stock in store before Black Friday and Christmas

Built only from what these calls showed:

1. **Get the item number first.** Find the SKU, UPC, part number or style number on the store's own website. PetSmart, America's Tire, Office Depot, Central Computers and Suitsupply answered fast with one. Best Buy, Roots and Lowe's asked for one.
2. **Say exactly which version, and what it isn't.** "Ocho Puntas, not the regular Plata." "Sugar Cookie, not the cranberry." "A single run, not a dual 50/5." Say how a hard name sounds, and spell it if asked.
3. **Ask for the department, not the automated line.** "Electrical or HVAC" got a Lowe's associate. "Garden" got a Home Depot associate. At Walmart, ask for "a live associate" and call back if it's busy.
4. **Ask them to look on the shelf and in the back.** "None in our back room" is a different answer from "I don't see it."
5. **Ask the in-store price, with tax.** Central Computers quoted $599, $100 more than the $499.99 our caller had seen. R&E Supply gave "tax and all."
6. **Ask the closing time and how to pay.** R&E closed at 4:50 and took no cash after 4. Central Computers closed at 5 on a Saturday. Home Depot's phone message on November 22 said "On Thanksgiving, we will be closed."
7. **If they don't have it, ask who does.** Wholesale Electric sent us to the store that had three. For parts, try the specialist supply house before the big box store.
8. **Ask when the next delivery comes**, and call back that morning.
9. **For a hot item this week, don't count on a hold.** If a person says they have one, go now, or buy it online for pickup while you're on the phone, as the Walmart associate suggested.

## Where our caller fell short

- **It hung up on a Five Below associate who was checking.** At one store, the associate said "Yeah, let me go look," and our caller said "Thanks so much for checking, I appreciate it. Bye!" When it called back a few minutes later, the associate said: "Yeah, I came back and you're gone." That's on us.
- **It didn't spell "Puntas" until asked.** Hacienda Liquors spent about two minutes on the name. The later calls said how to pronounce it from the first sentence.
- **It spent 17 minutes at Lowe's.** The virtual assistant kept answering "Sorry, I didn't catch that" until our caller asked plainly for the electrical or HVAC department. Then came about 11 minutes of hold for a one-sentence answer.
- **It didn't have the style number at Roots**, so the online team could only check the exact item, not other grey shirts in the size.
- **It talked over the Walmart associate** before he'd reached his screen: "I'm not even at the screen to look you up yet." When someone says "hold on," wait.

## What to say: a script that worked

> Hi, I'm checking whether you have [item, exact version, size or color] in stock today. The [SKU / item number / part number] is [number]. Could someone check the shelf, and the back if it's not out? If you have it: how many, what's the in-store price with tax, and what time do you close?

If they don't: "Do you know when your next delivery comes in? Is there another store or supplier you'd try?"
