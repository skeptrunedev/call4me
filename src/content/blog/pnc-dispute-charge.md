**The short answer:** to dispute a charge on a **PNC credit card**, call **1-800-558-8472**. PNC's [card help page](https://www.pnc.com/en/customer-service/card-management.html) says: "You can dispute a credit card transaction by calling PNC or going to your local branch," and "For Credit Card transactions, please call 1-800-558-8472." Business credit cards use 1-800-474-2101.

This got us to a person:

1. When it asks for your credit card number, **enter it** (or, as we did with no card, get past it with "I don't have it" or **press 1**, which the menu offers).
2. At "How can I help you?", **say you want to dispute a charge.** It confirms: "You'd like to dispute a transaction, is that right?"
3. At the dispute menu, **say "other transaction"** for an ordinary charge. The other choices are "payments and fees", "balance transfer check" and "lost or stolen dispute".
4. "Please hold while your call is being transferred." **A live representative answered 2 minutes 16 seconds into the call.**

What the representative told us:

- **Bring evidence.** "All types of receipts. Let's say, for example, if a product has been returned, if there are pictures or videos, and even just the receipt of the transaction is needed when filing for a dispute."
- **It takes two to three billing cycles.** "Usually it will take two to three billing cycles before it's complete or done."
- **No provisional credit up front.** Asked if they get one during that time: "Not yet. Just after the investigation."
- **Call, don't click.** Filing in the app or online banking isn't faster: "even if you'll go to the online banking or to the application, it will just give you the number that you have to call."
- **Dispute team hours:** "Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 8 p.m. Eastern Time" for transactions you made, and "Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. Eastern Time" for a fraud dispute. **We reached him at 6:36 pm Eastern on a Saturday**, so the line itself answered outside those hours.

**Debit cards are different.** PNC's card help page says debit disputes can be started in Online Banking: "Select Help," "Select Manage Accounts," then "Select Dispute a Transaction from the Checking and Savings section." Debit disputes fall under different federal rules, including provisional credit deadlines (see [how to dispute a charge](/blog/how-to-dispute-a-charge)).

## The PNC credit card dispute phone tree, step by step

This is the menu as it played on October 24, 2026, calling 1-800-558-8472 with no card.

1. **Call 1-800-558-8472.** "Welcome to PNC Voice Banking Credit Card Services." Spanish: "oprima el ocho." Then a notice that the call "may be recorded for security, fraud prevention, training, and quality assurance purposes."
2. **"Please say or enter your credit card number or say I don't have it."** Cardholders enter the 16 digits here.
3. **If it doesn't hear you:** "One digit at a time, please say or enter your 16-digit credit card number. Or if you do not have it, say I don't have it or press 1." Our caller got past this prompt; the recording doesn't show whether it pressed 1 or spoke.
4. **"How can I help you? For example, you can say your payment mailing address or I lost my card."** We said we wanted to know how someone disputes a charge.
5. **"You'd like to dispute a transaction, is that right?"** Yes.
6. **The dispute menu.** "If you are expecting a merchant credit, be advised that it may take up to 30 days from the issuing date to post to your account." Then: "To report a payment problem or dispute a penalty fee or a finance charge, say payments and fees. To dispute a balance transfer check on your statement, say balance transfer check. If your account was previously reported as lost or stolen, say lost or stolen dispute. For other disputed transactions, say other transaction."
7. **We said "other transaction."** "To expedite service, please have your 16-digit account number ready. Please hold while your call is being transferred."
8. **A representative answers,** 2 minutes 16 seconds in, and asks for your first and last name.

**If you're waiting on a refund the merchant promised,** the menu's own advice is to wait: it "may take up to 30 days from the issuing date to post to your account. Please call back after 30 days if credit does not post."

## What to have ready

- **Your 16-digit PNC card number.** The menu asks for it twice.
- **Your first and last name.** The representative's first question.
- **Receipts and proof:** the transaction receipt, proof of a return, and any pictures or videos.
- **The charge:** merchant, date, amount, and what went wrong.

## Why there's no provisional credit on a PNC credit card

It sounds harsh, but it's how credit card rules work. Under the federal billing error rules for credit cards (Regulation Z), the card company doesn't have to credit you while it investigates. Instead, you **don't have to pay the disputed amount**, and the card company can't try to collect it, until the investigation ends. It has "2 complete billing cycles (but in no event later than 90 days)" after it gets your written notice, which lines up with the representative's "two to three billing cycles."

Debit cards are the opposite: if the bank takes longer than 10 business days, Regulation E generally requires a provisional credit. Our [general guide](/blog/how-to-dispute-a-charge) has the details and sources.
