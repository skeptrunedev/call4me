**The short answer:** the Xfinity customer service phone number is **1-800-934-6489**, which spells **1-800-XFINITY**. **An AI voice assistant answers, not a person.** Choose **internet**, then **"something else,"** then say **"billing."** Skip the offer to chat by text. **A live billing representative answered 3 minutes 27 seconds into our recording**, about 3 minutes 32 seconds after we dialed, and only about 50 seconds after the assistant said it was connecting us.

Xfinity's [rights and contact page](https://www.xfinity.com/support/articles/rights) lists it this way: "For inquiries related to residential services, please contact us at: 1-800-xfinity (1-800-934-6489)." Xfinity Mobile has its own line on the same page, **1-888-936-4968**. We only called the residential number.

Here's the route that reached a person, with no account problem to describe:

1. Call 1-800-934-6489. After the greeting, the "virtual voice assistant" asks how it can help. We asked for a representative about billing or service support.
2. **"Do you need help with service support?"** We said yes.
3. **"Which service are you having trouble with, internet or TV?"** We said **internet**.
4. **"Do you want to troubleshoot your internet connection, replace your modem, reset your Wi-Fi password or something else?"** Say **"something else."**
5. It asks you to describe the problem. We said we had general questions. It answered: **"I can connect you with an agent, but I need more information to get you to the right team. Is this about billing, making a payment or an internet issue?"**
6. Say **"billing."** "I understand you have questions about billing, and I'm connecting you with an agent now who can help."
7. It offers text chat: "Press 1 to connect with an agent via text messaging on your mobile device. Otherwise, press 2." We didn't press 1.
8. "Please wait for the next available representative." About 10 seconds later, a person answered.

The first words from a live person, at **3:27** in the recording:

> "Thanks for choosing Xfinity Services. My name is [name removed]. How can I assist you today?"

**What the representative told us** (the rep's words, not anything we checked against Xfinity's policies):

- **When live agents are available:** "They'll be available anytime, ma'am." We asked about hours and time zone, and that was the whole answer.
- **Callbacks:** "They'll call you back in 30 to 40 minutes."
- **A billing dispute or credit:** asked how long a typical billing dispute takes to resolve or to see a credit, the rep said, "it may take around 5 to 10 minutes, ma'am." The rep didn't say whether that meant the phone call itself or the credit showing up on a bill, and we didn't ask.

We called on Tuesday, October 13, 2026, at about 11:41 am Pacific, with [call4me](/) placing the call. It was a general question for this guide, not about any account. This page is what we heard.

## The Xfinity phone tree, step by step

It's a voice assistant. You answer by speaking, and the only keypad options we heard were "say no or press two" if the account it found is wrong, and the chat offer.

1. **Call 1-800-934-6489.** "Hello, and thank you for calling Xfinity. Ask how you can take advantage of a free Xfinity Mobile line." Then a Spanish option ("Para español, oprima dos") and a recording notice.
2. **The account lookup.** "I have your street address beginning with..." and the first digits of an address. "If this is not the account you are calling about, please say no or press two. Otherwise, please remain on the line." The system matched the phone number we called from. We weren't calling about any account and didn't use one, so we stayed on the line and never confirmed anything. If you call from the phone number on your own account, expect it to find you this way.
3. **The AI disclosure.** "This virtual voice assistant uses artificial intelligence to generate responses."
4. **"Please tell me how I can help you today, and I'll take it from here."** We asked for a representative about "billing or service support."
5. **"Do you need help with service support?"** We said yes.
6. **"Which service are you having trouble with, internet or TV?"** We said internet.
7. **"Do you want to troubleshoot your internet connection, replace your modem, reset your Wi-Fi password or something else?"** We said "something else."
8. **"Please tell me more about what is going on with your internet, and I will guide you to the best next step."** We said we weren't calling about a specific issue.
9. **"I can connect you with an agent, but I need more information to get you to the right team. Is this about billing, making a payment or an internet issue?"** This is the step that leads to a person. Our caller's first answer wasn't picked up, and the assistant asked "Are you still there?" and repeated the question. The second "billing" worked.
10. **The chat offer.** "We have Xfinity chat agents available to assist you right now via messaging. Message and data rates may apply. Press 1 to connect with an agent via text messaging on your mobile device. Otherwise, press 2." We didn't press 1, and after a few seconds it moved on.
11. **"Please wait for the next available representative."** A person answered about 10 seconds later. There was no hold music or wait estimate on our call.

## What the representative said, in detail

The answers are the representative's, as recorded. We didn't find them stated on the Xfinity pages we loaded.

**Reaching a person.** We asked for the best way through the phone menu to a live person. The rep didn't follow the question ("I didn't get you, ma'am"), so we asked again. The answer: "Yes, ma'am. You're speaking with the billing department live agent only." In other words, the rep confirmed this was a live person in billing, but didn't describe a route. The route above is the one that worked for us.

**Hours.** "They'll be available anytime, ma'am." We asked about the time zone too, and didn't get one. Xfinity's contact page doesn't list hours, so we can't confirm "anytime" beyond the rep saying it.

**Callbacks.** "Like, they'll call you back in 30 to 40 minutes." The rep didn't say how to request one. We didn't hear a callback offer anywhere in the phone menu.

**Billing disputes.** "So it may take around 5 to 10 minutes, ma'am." That's the rep's estimate, in reply to how long a typical billing dispute takes "to get it resolved or see a credit." It's not clear whether that meant the length of the call or the time until a credit posts.

## What we didn't find out

- **How long a billing credit takes to appear on a bill.** See above. The rep's answer may have been about the call.
- **What time zone, if any, "anytime" refers to,** or whether that applies to every department or just billing.
- **How to request a callback,** and whether the 30 to 40 minutes the rep gave applies to the callback page.
- **The text chat option** (press 1), and the Xfinity Mobile line.
- **Other routes through the menu,** like saying "representative" or "agent" at the first question, or choosing TV or "internet issue" instead of billing. Billing is the only route we tested.
- **Whether the hold is ever longer.** Ours was about 50 seconds from "connecting you with an agent" to a person, on a Tuesday late morning Pacific time.
