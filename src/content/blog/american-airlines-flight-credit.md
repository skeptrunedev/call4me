**The short answer:** to use an American Airlines flight credit by phone, call reservations at **800-433-7300** (English, 24 hours, per [aa.com](https://www.aa.com/i18n/customer-service/contact-american/reservations-and-ticket-changes.jsp)) and have **the traveler named on the credit on the line, with a credit card**. A Flight Credit can only pay for travel for the person named on it, and the agent applies it and takes payment for any balance in the same call. On our first call the traveler couldn't be reached, so nothing was applied. On the second, the traveler joined, the credit went on the reservation, and the agent quoted **about $285** to pay.

The credit was worth less than the full ticket. The original ticket was a Nashville to New York LaGuardia round trip, out September 21 and back September 25. The agent's records showed **the outbound flight had been flown**, and on the call the traveler confirmed it: asked "did you use the first leg of the flight?", they said "Yes, yes." So only the LaGuardia to Nashville return was left as credit, to put toward a held Nashville to Raleigh trip.

**Nothing was ticketed.** After 30 minutes, a system error, two calls to other departments and about eight minutes on hold, the traveler gave their card details, asked whether they could cancel within 24 hours and get all the money back, and then said: "I'm going to hold on this purchase for now. Maybe I'll call back later."

What American needed, from the two calls:

1. **The 6-character confirmation code** of the held reservation.
2. **The 13-digit flight credit number** (it starts with 001).
3. **The passenger's name and date of birth.**
4. **The passenger,** in person. The first agent wouldn't go further with an assistant: "Can you please confirm your first and last name? It is necessary to verify your name."
5. **A credit card for the balance,** given in the same call. The agent wouldn't keep the credit applied to a held reservation and let the traveler pay later.

These calls were made on Thursday, October 1, 2026, from 7:06 to 7:15 am and from 7:28 to 7:58 am Central time, by a call4me user's AI phone assistant. The user is okay with us sharing the calls. Muted in the audio: every name (the traveler's, their companion's and every American agent's), the date of birth, the record locators, the flight credit number, the flight number, the billing address, ZIP code, email address and phone number, all card details, and one personal detail about why the original trip was cancelled. Balances are what each agent said on that date. In the recordings, dead air longer than about 3 seconds (mostly the line going quiet while an agent worked or while our caller rang the traveler) is shortened to about a second. No conversation, phone menu prompt, hold message or hold music is cut.

## Why the flight credit was worth less than the full ticket

Our caller asked to apply the credit against the original ticket value, $576.80. The agent looked at the ticket: "I can see the ticket image and it says the flight was on 21st of September, from Nashville to LaGuardia... the status says used." On the call, the traveler confirmed the first flight had been flown, so only the return was left as credit. They wanted to use that credit from the second leg.

So the credit only covered the unused return. That lines up with what American publishes: in the [travel credit table on aa.com](https://www.aa.com/i18n/customer-service/payment-options/travel-credit.jsp), a Flight Credit is issued for "unused tickets." If your credit looks smaller than your original ticket, ask the agent first whether part of the ticket shows as flown.

## How to use an American Airlines flight credit by phone, step by step

1. **Look up the credit first.** On aa.com, choose "Manage trips / Check-in", then "View travel credits", and enter the confirmation code or the 13-digit ticket number of the cancelled trip ([aa.com](https://www.aa.com/i18n/customer-service/payment-options/travel-credit.jsp)). That's the American Airlines flight credit lookup.
2. **Try aa.com once.** American's steps: book the new flight, then on "Review and pay" select "Add travel credit" and pay any remaining balance. Our user tried this first, and the credit didn't go through, which is why they called.
3. **Call 800-433-7300 with the traveler next to you,** or on a line you can bring them onto. American's terms say the person named on a Flight Credit can use it "for themselves only," and our first call stopped at exactly that point.
4. **Use the phone menu words that work.** "Apply a flight credit to a held reservation and ticket it" wasn't understood. "Book a held reservation" was. Expect questions about miles, the number of passengers and an AAdvantage account before a person picks up, about two minutes in.
5. **Give the confirmation code, then the credit number,** slowly. The second agent asked our caller to repeat it: "it seems to speak too fast."
6. **Ask whether any part of the original ticket shows as used** before you argue about the amount. That's what decided the value here.
7. **Ask for the balance, and ask how they got it.** Two agents gave two different numbers for the same held fare, and the second couldn't work out the credit's value on its own.
8. **Have a credit card ready, in the traveler's hands.** The agent took the billing ZIP code, an email for the confirmation, the billing address, a phone number, then the card number, expiration date, name on the card and security code. American's terms say a balance above the credit can only be paid "with a credit card accepted by American."
9. **Ask about cancelling before you say yes.** The traveler asked at the last step and didn't like the answer.
10. **If you're also paying for a companion's held trip, say so at the start.** The companion can't use your credit, and in 30 minutes the second reservation never came up.

## American Airlines trip credit vs flight credit

From [American's travel credit page](https://www.aa.com/i18n/customer-service/payment-options/travel-credit.jsp), checked on October 1, 2026:

| | Trip Credit | Flight Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Issued for | Cancelled tickets, significant delays, compensation, refunds, leftover value from an exchange, some bag and upgrade charges | Unused tickets |
| Who can use it | The holder, to book travel for anyone | Only the passenger named on it |
| How many per booking | Up to 8 for up to 9 passengers on aa.com | 1 on aa.com. Up to 2 in one transaction through Reservations |
| How to redeem | On aa.com | Find your trip on aa.com, or call Reservations, or at the airport |
| Expiration | The date listed on the credit, until 11:59 pm Central. AAdvantage members get 12 months, non-members 6 months, for Trip Credit issued on or after April 2, 2024 when cancelled on aa.com or the app | Travel must begin within 1 year of the original ticket's issue date |

A few more lines from American's Flight Credit terms worth knowing:

- **American Airlines flight credit expiration:** "Flight Credit is valid for 1 year from the date the ticket was issued," or 5 years from a qualifying delay or cancellation under DOT rules. "We will not extend or reissue once it expires."
- **No-shows lose it:** "If you don't show for your flight, your ticket will no longer be valid or eligible for any credit."
- **On aa.com, domestic only:** booked online, a Flight Credit "can only be applied to flights within the U.S., Puerto Rico, and the U.S. Virgin Islands."
- **Leftover value:** if the new ticket costs less than the credit, the rest "will be issued as travel credit for future reservations."
- **By phone:** "When contacting Reservations, tell the agent you have Flight Credit and you want to use it to book your travel."

## Where our caller fell short

- **It disputed the partially used ticket twice,** at 3:55 and again at 18:41 in the recording, before checking with the traveler. Once the traveler was on, they confirmed the first flight had been flown. Asking the traveler first would have saved time.
- **It couldn't verify the traveler.** On the first call the agent wanted the passenger's own name, and our caller rang the traveler, got no answer, and the call ended with nothing applied. Plan the call for when the traveler can pick up.
- **It answered the AAdvantage question inconsistently.** It said "Yes" on the first call, and "No" and then "Yeah" on the second. It didn't change where either call went, but it shouldn't guess.
- **It never got a breakdown of the credit.** It asked for "the line-by-line value" and got the same answer, that the ticket was partially used.
- **The companion's reservation never came up.** The brief asked for both reservations to be ticketed. Neither was.

## What to say: a script that worked

To the phone menu:

> Book a held reservation.

To the agent:

> Hi, I have a held reservation I'd like to ticket as it is, and a flight credit to apply to it. No changes to the flights. The confirmation code is [code], and the flight credit number is [13 digits starting with 001].

Then: "Does any part of the original ticket show as used? What's the credit worth, and what's the balance after it? If I pay now and need to cancel within 24 hours, do I get my money back, or a credit?"
