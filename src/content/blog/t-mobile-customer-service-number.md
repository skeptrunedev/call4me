**The short answer:** T-Mobile customer service by phone is **not 24 hours**. Its contact page says Customer Care Representatives are available **daily from 4 a.m. to 9 p.m. Pacific Time**; only the T-Life app offers "24/7 support." To reach a **live person** at **1-800-937-8997**, say **"I don't have one"** twice at the mobile number prompt, then **"something else"** when it lists payment, tech support and billing. **A live representative answered 1 minute 49 seconds into our recording**, with no hold music.

The T-Mobile customer service number is **1-800-937-8997**. From a T-Mobile phone, you can **dial 611** instead. **An automated voice menu answers, not a person.** When it asks for your mobile number, say **"I don't have one"** (it asks twice). When it offers payment, tech support, billing or something else, say **"something else."** **A live representative answered 1 minute 49 seconds into our recording**, about 1 minute 52 seconds after we dialed, and about 20 seconds after the menu said it was connecting us. There was no hold music. **For T-Mobile Home Internet, the support line is 1-844-275-9310**, and on a [recorded customer call](#t-mobile-home-internet-customer-service-fixing-a-login-without-the-pin) a representative verified the account with a code sent to the gateway instead of the PIN, then fixed the T-Mobile ID login.

T-Mobile's [contact us page](https://www.t-mobile.com/contact-us) lists it this way: "Customer Service: Dial 611 from your T-Mobile phone or call: 1-800-937-8997." The same page says "Customer Care Representatives are available daily from 4 a.m. to 9 p.m. Pacific Time," and "Live agent chat is available in T-Life from 4 a.m. until 12 a.m. Pacific Time."

Here's the route that reached a person, with no account and no T-Mobile number:

1. Call 1-800-937-8997. After "Welcome to T-Mobile," a Spanish option and a privacy notice, the menu says: "To get started, please enter or say your mobile number, area code first, or say, I don't have one."
2. **Say "I don't have one."** It asks again: "To look up your account, I'll need the 10-digit mobile number you're calling about." **Say "I don't have one"** again.
3. **"Tell me, how can I help you today?"** Our caller started explaining, and the menu asked again: "In a few words, describe what you need."
4. We said **"Representative."** The menu didn't transfer us. It answered with a list instead: "Are you calling about a payment? Do you need tech support? Have a billing question? Or something else?"
5. **Say "something else."** "I'll get you right over to someone who can help. We care about your experience, so we may record this call."
6. About 12 seconds of quiet, then a person.

The first words from a live person, at **1:49** in the recording:

> "Hi, thank you for calling T-Mobile. This is [name removed]. How can I help?"

**What the representative told us** (the rep's words, not anything we checked against T-Mobile's policies):

- **Hours:** we asked whether care reps are available every day from 4 a.m. to 9 p.m. Pacific time. The rep said "Um, yes." That matches the contact page.
- **Billing credits and disputes:** asked how long they typically take to resolve, the rep said "around 7 to 10 business days."
- **Calling when you're not a customer yet:** "the automated system will ask them. There's a prompt, and they just have to follow it." That's the whole answer. The rep didn't say where the prompt sends you.

We called on Tuesday, October 13, 2026, at about 11:50 am Pacific, with [call4me](/) placing the call. [GitHub Copilot CLI](/blog/github-copilot-phone-calls) placed it through the call4me MCP server. It was a general question for this guide, not about any account, and we didn't say we were a T-Mobile customer. This page is what we heard.

## The T-Mobile phone tree, step by step

It's a speech menu. You can answer out loud, and the first prompt also says "enter," so the keypad works for the mobile number. We only used our voice.

1. **Call 1-800-937-8997.** "Welcome to T-Mobile." Then "Para español, marque asterisco," and a privacy notice: "As part of this call and subsequent interactions from this call, T-Mobile may collect information about you to provide the best service possible."
2. **The mobile number prompt.** "To get started, please enter or say your mobile number, area code first, or say, I don't have one." We said "I don't have one."
3. **The same prompt again.** "To look up your account, I'll need the 10-digit mobile number you're calling about. You can enter or say it now, area code first, or say, I don't have one." We said it again. After the second time, it moved on.
4. **"Tell me, how can I help you today?"** Our caller answered with a full sentence about a consumer guide. The menu replied "Let's try again. In a few words, describe what you need. You can say things like, I want to make a payment, I need to check my account balance, or whatever the reason is for your call."
5. **"Representative" didn't skip ahead.** The menu said "Let's try by choosing one of those options. Are you calling about a payment? Do you need tech support? Have a billing question? Or something else?"
6. **"Something else" got us through.** "I'll get you right over to someone who can help. We care about your experience, so we may record this call."
7. **A person answered** about 12 seconds after that message ended. The line was quiet the whole time. There was no music, no wait estimate and no callback offer.

If you call with your T-Mobile number, the menu will look up your account first, and the route may be different. We didn't test that.

## What the representative said, in detail

These are the rep's answers, as recorded.

## T-Mobile Home Internet customer service: fixing a login without the PIN

T-Mobile Home Internet has its own support line. The [Home Internet contact page](https://www.t-mobile.com/support/home-internet/contact-us) lists "T-Mobile Home Internet Tech Support: 1-844-275-9310" and "T-Mobile Home Internet Sales Support: 1-844-839-5057." On October 2, 2026, a call4me customer's assistant called 1-844-275-9310 three times about one problem: the customer's T-Mobile ID wouldn't link to his Home Internet gateway, so the T-Mobile Home Internet login kept saying the email didn't match. He didn't know his account PIN, and the bill was past due. The account owner agreed to publish all three calls.

**What worked, on the third call:** with no PIN, the assistant asked whether T-Mobile could verify the account with a temporary code sent to the router instead. The representative said yes ("va a recibir entonces un código temporal en la cajita", a temporary code will arrive on the little box). The code showed up in the gateway's messages, the assistant read it back, and the representative opened the account. She then sent a second code to the customer's email to link it to the T-Mobile ID. A few minutes after the assistant read that code back, it told her the customer could now get into the account.

Each call reached a person in under 5 minutes. This is the route through the T-Mobile Home Internet phone number's menu on the third call:

1. Call 1-844-275-9310. "Welcome to T-Mobile Home Internet," a Spanish option and the privacy notice.
2. **The line number prompt.** "Please say or enter your 10-digit internet line number that's on the home page of your T-Mobile Home Internet app. Or it may be found on your internet gateway screen by scrolling to the number." The assistant gave the gateway's line number.
3. **"Tell me, how can I help you today?"** On the third call, the menu then asked for "your six to fifteen digit account PIN, or say, I don't have it." The assistant said "I don't have it." The menu sent it to the T-Life app to change the PIN and said "since I can't verify your account, I won't be able to give you your account balance."
4. **Say "Representative," then "my account."** The menu offered "my account" for billing or equipment status and "technical support" for device or network problems. On the first call, the assistant said "my account" at this prompt without being asked for a PIN. On the second, it described the login problem first, and the menu ran an automated diagnostic check; "main menu" and a second request for a representative got it through.
5. "I'll get you right over to someone who can help." Hold music and T-Life ads, then a person. **A representative answered 3 minutes 45 seconds into the third call**, 3 minutes 46 seconds into the second, and 4 minutes 37 seconds into the first.

All three representatives answered in Spanish, so the assistant switched to Spanish. On the first call, the first representative said "I don't talk very English" and transferred the call to a second one, who said she was with "el equipo de expertos de T-Mobile Puerto Rico." The quotes below are our translations.

### What the representative said about payments and AutoPay

These are the third call's representative's answers, as recorded:

- **Payment arrangement:** if you can't pay by the due date, "a partir del veintidós puede solicitar a través de la app de T-Life una extensión de pago, que le permite pagarlo hasta catorce días más tarde, sin que tenga interrupción" (from the 22nd you can request a payment extension in the T-Life app that lets you pay up to 14 days later, without an interruption). Later she called it "un payment arrangement" where "usted selecciona la fecha" (you pick the date) "dentro de los catorce días hábiles que le da el sistema" (within the 14 business days the system gives you). She said 14 days the first time and 14 business days the second.
- **No exact cutoff:** suspension "puede ser en cualquier momento, no hay una hora en particular o día" (can happen at any time, there's no particular hour or day).
- **Before the payment:** "no tengo el débito automático activado. No hay un pago programado" (I don't have AutoPay on. There's no scheduled payment), and no saved card.
- **AutoPay steps in T-Life:** go to account, the balance, "payment options," then "make a payment" to pay and "manage autopay" to set up AutoPay. She said AutoPay with "una tarjeta de débito con logo o cuenta bancaria" (a branded debit card or bank account) takes **$5 a month** off the bill.
- **After the payment:** AutoPay on, drafting "los días veinte de cada mes" (the 20th of each month) from a card whose last four digits we've muted, the $72.10 payment processed, "y el balance es cero" (and the balance is zero). "Su próxima factura con el autopago activado serían entonces sesenta y siete con diez" (your next bill with AutoPay on will be $67.10).

### What these calls didn't settle

- **No reference number, account number or confirmation number.** The assistant asked for the full account number and a case or reference number. The representative didn't give either before the call ended, and gave no payment confirmation number.
- **No fee answer.** Asked whether there was any extra fee to pay now, the representative didn't mention one. That isn't the same as saying there's no fee.
- **The login fix rests on the assistant's word.** The recording has the assistant telling the representative "ya pudo entrar a la cuenta" (he was able to get into the account). Nobody on the call checked it.
- **One email code looked wrong.** The assistant said the email "dice que es para resetear el PIN" (says it's for resetting the PIN). The representative said the latest code "se supone que sea para hacer la vinculación del email" (is supposed to be for linking the email). We don't know which message was which.
- **We don't know why the third representative accepted the router code.** The first and second representatives each asked for the PIN or for the customer himself. On the third call, the assistant offered the router code before the representative asked for anything else. Asking for it first might be what made the difference. We can't tell from three calls.
- **We don't know why every call was answered in Spanish.** We didn't test other times or other lines.
- **The customer paid online himself.** The assistant didn't take any card details. It relayed the customer's approval and asked for safe steps to pay online.

## How we made this recording

call4me is a phone for your AI agent: your agent (Claude Code, Codex, ChatGPT) tells it what to get done and it makes the call. For this one, the brief was to reach a live person at T-Mobile customer service, say it was a general inquiry for a consumer guide, never claim to be a customer, and ask four questions: whether phone or T-Life chat is faster, whether reps are available 4 a.m. to 9 p.m. Pacific every day, how the menu routes people who aren't customers yet, and how long a billing credit or dispute takes.

Two things our caller could have done better:

- **It skipped the phone vs. chat question.** It asked three of the four questions and said goodbye.
- **It answered the first open question with a full sentence.** The menu wanted a few words and asked again. Saying "something else" sooner might have saved a step. We didn't test it.
