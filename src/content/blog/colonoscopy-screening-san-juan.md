**To schedule a screening colonoscopy with Dra. Marcia Cruz-Correa, call her gastroenterology office at (787) 925-4003.** The office is on the first floor of the Hospital Oncológico Dr. Isaac González Martínez in San Juan, Puerto Rico. That is the location and phone her practice lists in the federal [NPI Registry](https://npiregistry.cms.hhs.gov/api/?version=2.1&number=1396205506) ("Hospital Oncologico Isaac Gonzalez Martinez, First Floor Gastroenterology"). Her profile at the [UPR Comprehensive Cancer Center](https://www.cccupr.org/programas/marcia-cruz-correa/), which names her Director of the Gastrointestinal Oncology Division at that hospital, does not list this office number. It gives **(787) 936-1477** and citas@cccupr.org for general hospital appointments instead.

On the call, a person answered right away, with no phone menu: "Oficina Doctora Marcia Cruz Correa, buen día." The appointment coordinator confirmed that **(787) 925-4003 is the number for scheduling with the doctor**, and that she coordinates the appointments herself.

On October 15, 2026, a call4me customer's assistant called in Spanish to ask how to schedule a screening colonoscopy with Dra. Cruz-Correa in November 2026. The brief said not to book or hold anything. Here is what the coordinator said:

- **The doctor sets the colonoscopy dates.** "La cita se coordinan conmigo, pero la doctora Cruz es la que nos dice la fecha de la cita." (Appointments are coordinated through me, but Dr. Cruz is the one who gives us the date.)
- **She was on vacation until October 28, 2026**, so no November dates were available on the day of the call.
- **A first consultation costs $150.** That was her answer when asked for an out of pocket estimate.
- **The first consultation can be done by telemedicine.**
- **The procedure cost depends on where it's done.** Once the doctor picks a date, the office decides which of her offices will do the procedure, and only then can it quote a price.
- **Call back after October 28.** The coordinator agreed to a follow-up call once the doctor is back.

## What this call did not settle

- **Nothing was booked or held.** The brief said not to, and the call only gathered information.
- **Whether a consultation is required before the colonoscopy was not stated outright.** The coordinator quoted the first consultation price and said it could be by telemedicine, but she did not say a consult is mandatory.
- **No total price for the procedure.** Anesthesia, facility and pathology costs were not quoted.
- **Insurance, referrals and bowel prep were not discussed.**
- **Which office does the procedure** is decided later, so this post doesn't give a procedure address.
- **One unclear answer.** When the assistant asked whether there was a separate direct line, the coordinator's reply reads as either "No es directamente conmigo" or "No, es directamente conmigo" in the transcriptions. She then gave her own name and the office number, so the second reading (it's handled directly by her) fits the rest of the call.

The availability and price are statements from the dated call. Check them again when you call.

## What to ask when you call about a screening colonoscopy

This office doesn't hand out procedure dates until the doctor does, so a first call is mostly about learning the process. These questions got useful answers, and the rest are the gaps this call left open:

1. **Who schedules the doctor's procedures, and on which number.** Here it was the coordinator at (787) 925-4003.
2. **When the doctor releases dates** for the month you want. If the doctor is away, ask when to call back.
3. **Whether a consultation comes first**, what it costs and whether it can be telemedicine. Here: $150, and telemedicine was possible.
4. **Where the procedure would be done**, since it can change the price.
5. **The total out of pocket price**, including anesthesia, facility and pathology, or whether your insurance is accepted and a referral is needed.
6. **Prep instructions**, once a date is set.

Keep your age, screening history and family history ready, but share them only when the office needs them. One small slip on this call: the assistant first said "noviembre del año que viene" (November of next year), then correctly said November 2026 later on.
