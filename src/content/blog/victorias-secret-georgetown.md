On October 1, 2026, at about 12:55 PM Eastern, a call4me customer's assistant called the Victoria's Secret in Washington DC's Georgetown neighborhood for someone who wanted to pick up two things that day: breast lift tape and a sparkly silver thong in a particular style and color. ## How to get an item held at the Georgetown Victoria's Secret

The useful part of this call was asking for a **shelf check, not a website check**. The site showed no pickup option for these items, but an employee walked the floor and found what was actually there, including a last pack that was not the shade asked for.

A few things made it work:

1. **Name the exact product, color and size up front**, then say which substitutes are acceptable. Here the assistant described the requested style, heard it was missing, and asked about the closest match instead of hanging up.
2. **Ask for prices on the substitute**, since the in-store item may not be the one you priced online.
3. **Ask for a free hold under the pickup person's name and phone number**, and say when they will come in.
4. **Ask how long the hold lasts.** At this store the answer was same day.
5. **Ask for the staff member's name or a hold note** if you want something to point to at the counter. This call did not get one.

One thing to do better than this call: the brief asked the assistant to introduce itself as an AI at the start, and it only said so partway through. Put that instruction first in your own brief.
