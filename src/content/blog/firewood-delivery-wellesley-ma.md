## What "a cord" means in Massachusetts, and on these sites

Massachusetts is strict on paper. [Chapter 94, Section 298](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXV/Chapter94/Section298) of the state's general laws says "Cordwood and firewood shall be advertised, offered for sale and sold only in terms of cubic feet or cubic meters," counted as "the closely stacked cubic foot or cubic meter content to be delivered to the purchaser." It goes on: "The terms ''cord'', ''face cord'', ''pile'', ''truckload'' or terms of similar import shall not be used."

[Section 299](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXV/Chapter94/Section299) requires a delivery ticket or invoice at delivery, showing "the quantity delivered to the purchaser in terms of cubic feet or cubic meters," the names and addresses of seller and buyer, the date and the price. Hopkinton's [sealer of weights and measures](https://www.hopkintonma.gov/departments/land_use/sealer_of_weights___measures.php) gives the number to check against: "A cord contains 128 cubic feet," which "tightly stacked should measure 4 feet high, 4 feet wide, and 8 feet long."

In practice, nearly every seller on this list still names its sizes as cords. What separates them is whether they also tell you the cubic feet, and which kind:

- **128 cubic feet (or 4 x 4 x 8 feet), stated:** BostonFirewood.com, Weston Nurseries, J B Sawmill and Tavares Brothers, plus Minuteman's 4 x 8 x 4 ft and Lynch's 4 x 4 x 8 ft. Mahoney's half cord is 64 cubic feet.
- **Loose cubic feet, stated:** MetroWest's cord is "185cf loosely loaded (not stacked)" and West End's is "Loose Thrown Cord-Wood (180 cu ft)." Wood thrown loose in a truck takes up more room than the same wood stacked, so these numbers aren't comparable to 128. MetroWest calls its 185 "Approximately a full cord"; neither page gives a stacked number.
- **No cubic feet at all:** Beech Hill, Wagon Wheel, Gary's, Pellets R Us, James English and Ashland. Wilson Farm sells by the bin, 1/6 of a cord.

So when you compare two Wellesley quotes, ask each seller one thing: how many cubic feet will this be when it's stacked? Then check the delivery ticket. If a load comes up short, the state's Division of Standards has a [firewood sales page](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/firewood-sales) on what to do. For the full breakdown of cords, face cords and "pallets," see [face cord vs full cord](/blog/face-cord-vs-full-cord), and for prices across all three towns in this series, [how much a cord of firewood costs](/blog/how-much-is-a-cord-of-firewood).

## Ordering firewood delivered to Wellesley: what to ask

Everything here comes from these sellers' pages and the two calls.

1. **Ask for stacked cubic feet, and get it in writing.** The law counts closely stacked cubic feet, and only some sellers here state them. Two price by loose volume.
2. **Compare delivered prices, not wood prices.** Delivery to Wellesley ran from free (Beech Hill) to $30 (MetroWest), $55 (Wagon Wheel), $75 (Weston Nurseries), $75 to $95 (Gary's), $99 (Minuteman) and an estimated $135 to $155 (West End). BostonFirewood.com's quote includes delivery.
3. **Ask how soon.** On November 27: roughly 7 days at Pellets R Us, about 3 weeks at BostonFirewood.com, and "Up to 10 weeks" at West End.
4. **Ask about stacking before you count on it.** BostonFirewood.com isn't stacking right now, MetroWest isn't taking new 2026 stacking orders, and Mahoney's dumps the load. Beech Hill starts at $250 a cord and Wagon Wheel charges $100.
5. **Ask whether seasoned wood is really in stock.** Both sellers we reached had only kiln-dried, and BostonFirewood.com's owner said seasoned wood in any quantity was gone in July.
6. **Check the ticket and stack it soon.** Massachusetts requires a delivery ticket with the cubic feet. Stack the load and measure it (128 cubic feet is 4 x 4 x 8 feet) before you burn any.
