**The short answer:** the AT&T customer service number for wireless bill and account help is **1-800-331-0500** (AT&T writes it 800.331.0500). **An automated voice system answers, not a person**, and it won't put you through until it has a reason for your call. On our call that reached someone, we said we had **a billing question**, chose **"explanation of charges,"** said we had no account number, picked **"personal,"** turned down a texted link, and asked for a representative. The system quoted a 24 to 36 minute hold, and **a live AT&T representative answered 44 minutes 30 seconds into the recording.**

AT&T's [Wireless support page](https://www.att.com/support/wireless/) lists the number in its "Contact us" box: "Bill & account, 800.331.0500," open "M - F, 8 a.m. - 8 p.m. CT" and "Sa - Su, 9 a.m. - 7 p.m. CT." The representative we reached gave longer hours for live agents (see [below](#when-att-customer-service-is-open)).

Here's what got us from the voice system to a person:

1. Call 1-800-331-0500. "Welcome to AT&T," then "Para español oprima el ocho."
2. It asks what you need ("Hi, what can I do for you?" or "What's the issue you're calling about?"). **Give a specific reason in the menu's own terms.** We said we had a general billing question.
3. "How can I help with your bill? You can say something like make a payment, explanation of charges, or my bill is too high." **We said "explanation of charges."** Don't say "pay" or "pay my bill" (see [below](#the-call-that-said-pay-my-bill)).
4. It asks for "the account number or phone number on the account." We had none, and it said "That's fine."
5. "A personal or business account?" We said personal.
6. It offered to text a link that explains charges. **We said we'd rather speak to a representative.** "Let me find someone to help you."
7. It offered a callback (press 1), to end the call (press 2), or to keep holding (press 3). Our caller didn't press a key, and we stayed in the queue.
8. "Current hold time is between 24 minutes and 36 minutes from now." Hold music and ads, then a person.

**What the representative told us** (her answers, not AT&T policy we checked):

- **Fastest way to a person:** "You're speaking with a live agent right now, so calling this phone number would be the fastest way to do it."
- **Live-agent hours:** "Monday through Friday from 7 a.m. to 11 p.m. Central Time, and Saturday and Sundays from 8 a.m. to 11 p.m. Central." That's not what att.com lists ([both are below](#when-att-customer-service-is-open)).
- **What to have ready:** "their account number available, as well as their four to six digit PIN number, which is the passcode to get into their account."
- **Credits:** "Normally we say with credits, you wanna expect to see them on your bill within one to three billing cycles," though "that can differ just depending on the severity of the case."

**Three lessons from four calls:**

- **Give a specific reason.** When we only said "representative" or "a general question," the system said "I do need a reason for your call to keep going. Without it, I may have to end this call."
- **Don't say "pay."** Saying "pay my bill" got us a payment web address and a goodbye, not a person.
- **Plan for a long hold.** Ours was about 43 minutes from "Let me find someone to help you" to a live voice. On an earlier call the same day, the system quoted 26 to 39 minutes.

We called on Tuesday, October 13, 2026, with [call4me](/) placing the calls. The call that reached a person started at about 4:00 pm Pacific (6:00 pm Central). These were general questions for this guide, not about any AT&T account, and we gave no account number or PIN. This page is what we heard.

## The AT&T phone menu, step by step

It's a conversational voice system. You can answer in full sentences. The only keypad options we heard were 8 for Spanish and 1, 2 or 3 at the callback offer.

1. **Call 1-800-331-0500.** "Welcome to AT&T. Calls are recorded for quality and the purposes stated in our privacy notice." Then "Para español oprima el ocho."
2. **The open question.** On different calls it said "Hi, what can I do for you?", "What's the issue you're calling about?" and "Please say what you are calling about."
3. **The account number.** "What's the account number or phone number on the account?" We had none each time and it carried on.
4. **The product, if it doesn't know yet.** "Which product are you calling about?" or "Your cell phone, landline, phone advanced, or digital phone?" On our billing call it didn't ask.
5. **"A personal or business account?"**
6. **If it still doesn't have a reason:** "I'm here to help, but I do need a reason for your call to keep going. Without it, I may have to end this call." Then "Tell me how I can help with your mobile phone service. You can say things like new service, can't make or receive calls, activate a device or pay my bill."
7. **On a billing reason,** it offers to text a link instead. Saying you'd rather speak to a representative got "Let me find someone to help you."
8. **The callback offer,** then the hold estimate, then hold music with ads for the AT&T Guarantee, the AT&T app, ActiveArmor, HomeTech and Unlimited Your Way, on a loop.

We didn't try "explanation of charges" with a real account, "my bill is too high," "can't make or receive calls," or any of the other options, so we can't say where each one leads.

## How we made these recordings

call4me is a phone for your AI agent: your agent (Claude Code, Codex, ChatGPT) tells it what to get done and it makes the call. The brief for all four calls was to reach a person on AT&T's wireless line, ask general questions for this guide, and give no account or personal details.

What went wrong on our side:

- **Call 1 was capped by us.** Our caller had a 30 minute time limit, which ended the call on hold. AT&T's own estimate that time was 26 to 39 minutes.
- **Call 2 picked "pay my bill"** from the menu's list because it was trying to give any reason. That's a self-service route.
- **Call 3 dropped on our side** right after the system asked for a reason.
- **Call 4 worked** because our caller led with a billing question, picked a billing option from the menu's own examples, and asked for a person when offered a text link.
