**The short answer:** the Air Serbia phone number for customer service in the USA and Canada is **+1 (718) 725-8642**. Air Serbia's [contact page](https://www.airserbia.com/en/contact-us) lists it for calls "from USA and Canada", and its [customer service plan for US flights](https://www.airserbia.com/en/info-and-help/support/customer-service-plan) points US callers to the same number. The contact center runs on Belgrade time: the contact page gives its hours as 06:00 to 23:00 local time (UTC+2). Air Serbia also lists a separate US toll-free line, **+1 (888) 390-9847**, for passengers who need special assistance. We didn't call that one.

The phone tree, as it played on all four calls:

1. **Press 9 for English.** The greeting starts in Serbian ("Hvala što ste pozvali kontakt centar Air Srbije"), then switches to English.
2. A privacy notice, a recording notice and a reminder that you can change a ticket under "My reservation" on airserbia.com.
3. **Press 1** for booking and general inquiry, **or press 3** for ancillaries (seats, bags, meals, pets) and then **1** to talk to an operator.
4. "All operators are busy at the moment. Please hold the line." That repeats about once a minute until someone picks up.

**A live Air Serbia agent answered on every call, after 13 minutes 32 seconds, 24 minutes 47 seconds, 18 minutes 23 seconds and 17 minutes 36 seconds.** The longest one went to the Serbian queue after a wrong key press, so plan on 15 to 20 minutes on hold. Agents greet you in Serbian, then switch to English when asked.

What the agents said:

- **They can add a seat over the phone.** "You can call us on this number and we can open reservation and add the seat that you want."
- **Exit row 33D on this Airbus A330 flight is "exit row and extra leg room"**, and in economy it was "free but with payment": open, but paid.
- **Exit row seats need a passenger "in good health"** who can speak a foreign language, in case of an emergency.
- **Once you're checked in, they can't assign a seat.** "If the passenger is checked in, we cannot assign the seat." The agent sent a request to "offload" the check-in and asked us to call back in a few hours to see if it went through.
- **She couldn't transfer to a supervisor or the airport team,** and couldn't stay on the line while we checked something: "We have so many passengers waiting on the line."
- **Aisle seats were $43 each** on the morning of the flight (32D, 31D, 31G and 32G), quoted in US dollars.
- **You have to pass verification first.** Agents asked for the booking reference, then some of: where the ticket was bought, the flight number, one way or round trip, checked bags, the phone and email on the booking, the passenger's date of birth, and how many passengers are on the booking.

The calls were placed between October 7 and October 15, 2026. This page sticks to what's in the recordings.

## The Air Serbia customer service phone tree, step by step

This is the menu as it played on +1 (718) 725-8642, the Air Serbia call centar ("kontakt centar" in the Serbian greeting), between October 7 and 15, 2026.

1. **"Hvala što ste pozvali kontakt centar Air Srbije. Thank you for calling Air Serbia Contact Center. For services in English, press nine."** Press 9. On one call, a different key got "Odabrali ste pogrešnu opciju" ("you chose the wrong option") and the Serbian queue, which took 24 minutes 47 seconds.
2. **A privacy notice, a recording notice, and a reminder** that "My reservation" on airserbia.com can change a ticket "in accordance with the selected tariff rules".
3. **The main menu:** 1 booking and general inquiry, 2 redeem a voucher, 3 ancillaries, 4 existing booking exchange or refund, 5 flight information (two calls heard "flights from Nis" instead), 6 baggage services, 7 claims, hash for the previous menu.
4. **For seats, bags, meals or pets, press 3, then 1** at "To talk to an operator, please press one." For everything else, press 1 at the main menu.
5. **"All operators are busy at the moment. Please hold the line."** No position in the queue, no estimated wait and no callback offer on any of the four calls.
6. **A person,** in Serbian: "Air Serbia, [name] na vezi, izvolite." Ask "Do you speak English?" if you need to.

## What to have ready

- **Your booking reference** (the six character PNR). It's the first thing every agent asked for.
- **The phone number and email address on the booking.** Two agents asked for both.
- **The passenger's date of birth.**
- **Whether the trip is one way or round trip, and how many passengers are on the booking.** These were two of the "three questions" in the last call, along with the email.
- **Where the ticket was bought and the flight number,** if it came from a travel agency or another airline. This booking was a codeshare ticket issued by JetBlue, and Air Serbia still handled the seat.
- **Your seat choice and a backup,** with a price limit. Seats sold out between calls.

## Changing a seat after Air Serbia online check-in

The second agent was clear: "If the passenger is checked in, we cannot assign the seat." The check-in has to be offloaded first, and that's a request the agent sends, not something that happens while you wait. She asked us to call back "in a few hours" to see whether it worked. If you want a different seat, call before you check in online.

For an exit row, expect a health question. The agent said exit row passengers need to be in good health and speak a foreign language "in case of emergency".

## What these calls did not settle

- **No seat was bought or confirmed for the flight.** The second call ended with "I cannot hold the seat", the third dropped when the agent stopped hearing the caller, and the fourth failed verification.
- **We don't know if the offload went through.** No recorded call confirmed it.
- **Whether the seat would have been free is unresolved.** The first agent said "business class, so that means free of charge", then checked and said "this is not business class here" and the seat was paid. The second agent said there was "no charge" because the passenger was in business class. The third quoted $43 for aisle seats. We can't tell from the recordings which fare the ticket was.
- **The 33.60 euro price for 35D** comes from the assistant's own brief, not from an agent on these recordings.
- **Hours and the special-assistance line** come from Air Serbia's website. None of the agents were asked about them.
- **A fifth call is not included.** It reached an agent on October 14 after a long hold, but the assistant disconnected before it got any answers.
