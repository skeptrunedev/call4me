**The short answer:** yes, you can check store stock in Japan without speaking Japanese. Have someone call the store in Japanese, name the exact product, and ask whether it's in the store right now. Staff will usually go and look. Between November 14 and 23, 2026, four call4me users had their AI phone assistant make 78 calls in Japanese to stores in Tokyo, Kyoto, Uji and the Osaka area. When a person at a big electronics chain came on the line, they almost always gave a straight answer, and **Yodobashi Camera in Kyoto found a new pair of XREAL smart glasses and held them for four days under a name and a foreign mobile number.** Kyoto's tea shops were a different story. Of the 11 that answered about a handmade bamboo tea whisk, 10 had none. And a few stores don't take stock questions by phone at all.

This is the kind of errand an [app that calls businesses for you](/app-that-calls-businesses-for-you) is built for: the same question, asked politely in another language, store after store, with the answers in one place.

What happened, errand by errand:

1. **Smart glasses, Tokyo then Kyoto (7 calls).** Three Bic Camera stores in Tokyo had the glasses or knew who did, but none had a display unit to try both sizes. **Yodobashi Camera in Kyoto had a new one, held it until closing on November 21, quoted ¥84,980, and said tax-free works by the new refund method.**
2. **Matcha in Uji (1 call).** Marukyu Koyamaen said the Tenju grade had just sold out. Kiwami Choan was in stock in 20 g and 40 g tins, but the shop takes no holds and limits matcha to two per person.
3. **A handmade tea whisk (chasen) in Kyoto (22 calls).** Sold out or not carried at 10 shops. One said "あります" (we have it), then the call ended before it confirmed what it had.
4. **Dishwasher-safe matcha bowls around Osaka (35 calls).** A 212 Kitchen Store in Itami and Toshogama in Hirakata each had one in the store. Hankyu and Takashimaya in Osaka had matcha bowls, but none sold as dishwasher-safe.
5. **Drone batteries, Osaka and Kyoto (3 calls).** Yodobashi Camera doesn't carry FPV drone batteries at either store.
6. **PS5 Pro, Tokyo (9 calls).** None anywhere, new or used.
7. **Stores that won't do it by phone.** GEO's game line, an Apple Store in Tokyo, and two Osaka shops that only answered with a recording or an AI receptionist.

These 78 calls came from four call4me users, and each user's calls are kept separate below. One user, who was sightseeing in Japan, made 65 of them: the smart glasses, the matcha, the tea whisk and the matcha bowls. One looked for drone batteries in Osaka and Kyoto, one hunted for a PS5 Pro in Tokyo, and one called an Apple Store in Tokyo. Our caller spoke Japanese on every call, and it didn't buy anything or give a card. The users' names and phone numbers and the store staff's names are muted in the audio and left out of the quotes. Japanese quotes are verbatim from our machine transcripts, with our own English translation in parentheses, and "…" marks words we skipped. Prices and stock are what each store said that day, and they can change any day.

## Check stock at Yodobashi Camera by phone: what the Kyoto store said

The Kyoto call shows how a phone stock check at a big Japanese electronics store goes when it works.

- **Getting through took about 6 minutes.** The operator rang the TV corner, couldn't reach anyone, and offered a callback. When our caller started giving a foreign mobile number, the operator said "外国にこちらからおかけができません" (we can't call abroad from here). Our caller asked to wait on the line instead, and the operator tried again.
- **Stock, checked twice.** "店頭在庫ございますね" (we have it in the store). Our caller asked if it was new and unopened, and the staffer went to look: "目視で確認してきますので" (I'll go check it with my own eyes). Back on the line: "ございますね、新品のもの" (yes, we have a new one).
- **Support goes to the maker.** "メーカー直接になりますよ" (that would be directly with the manufacturer).
- **A phone hold needs only a name and a number.** "お名前とお電話番号をいただけましたらできますよ" (if you give me a name and phone number, we can do it). The store's system couldn't take a foreign number as written: "ちょっとプラスっていう表記がうちの方ではできないので" (we can't enter a plus sign on our end), so the staffer typed the digits without the plus and read them back.
- **Four days, then it's canceled.** "二十一の、えーと、夜まで取り置き、閉店までとなりますので、それ以降はキャンセルになります" (we'll hold it until the night of the 21st, until closing, and after that it will be canceled). There was no hold number: "お名前と電話番号から検索をかける形になります" (we look it up by name and phone number).
- **The price:** "八万四千九百八十円" (¥84,980).
- **Tax-free still works with a phone hold.** "問題なくいけますよ、リファンド方式になります" (that's no problem, it's the refund method). You pay this price at the register, "免税カウンターに行っていただいて手続きして手数料が引かれた分戻ってきますよっていう形" (then you go to the tax-free counter, do the paperwork, and get back the amount minus a fee). Bring "パスポートの原本" (your original passport), and pay with "ご本人様のカード" (your own card).

## Bic Camera: stock yes, display no

All three Bic Camera stores in Tokyo knew where the glasses were, but our caller's real question was whether the user could try both sizes, and nobody could offer that.

- **The first store didn't know the product name.** "エックスリールというのは、ごめんなさいどういった商品" (this "XREAL," sorry, what kind of product is it). Once the staffer worked out it was "ARグラスっていうやつ" (the thing called AR glasses), it checked and found the store sells them, but "ちょっと展示はないんで" (we don't have a display).
- **The second store had both sizes in stock.** "MサイズとLサイズは在庫ありますけどが、展示の方はございません" (we have M and L in stock, but no display). It said "本店には在庫と展示の方もございます" (our main store has stock and a display too). Asked about swapping sizes later when nothing is wrong with the product, the staffer said "ちょっと無理だと思いますので" (I think that would be difficult), and suggested deciding the fit at the main store first.
- **The main store's display was broken.** After about 4 minutes on hold: "以前Mサイズのものが出てはいたんですが、今故障してしまってメンテナンス中で展示そのものがございませんでした" (we used to have the M size out, but it broke and is in maintenance, so there's no display at all).

Bic Camera's hold recording, which played on all three calls, says its app does stock checks and holds: "ビックカメラ公式アプリ、公式アプリがあれば、ポイントカードとして使えるのはもちろん、お店の在庫確認や、商品の取り置きもできて便利です" (with the official Bic Camera app, besides using it as a point card, you can check store stock and hold items). Yodobashi's hold message says the same about its website: "ヨドバシドットコムは、商品の在庫状況を、お店ごとに、リアルタイムでお知らせしており" (Yodobashi.com shows each store's stock in real time).

Please check in the store or in the official GEO app).
- **An Apple Store in Tokyo:** "この窓口でご注文というのは一切受けたまってないものでして" (this line doesn't take orders at all). In the store, "来ていただいて、在庫があれば購入いただけるというような状況になっているんですね" (you come in, and if it's in stock, you can buy it). For an order, "オンラインストアのコールセンターで相談いただくのがよろしいかなとは思いますね" (I think it's best to ask the online store's call center).

- **Tamaichi Doi Toki, Osaka:** "ただいまAIが一時受付をしております" (an AI is handling first contact right now). The menu splits business and personal customers, then says "ご用件をお預かりし、確認の上、折り返しご連絡いたします" (we'll take your message, check, and call you back) and asks for your full name. Four calls never reached a person.
- **Ippodo's own shop in Kyoto:** the phone menu played twice and never reached a person.

## What the official sites say: tax-free refunds and the Japanese-only PS5

Everything above is what we heard on the calls. These two points come from official websites, not from the stores.

**Tax-free shopping changed on November 1, 2026.** [Japan's tourism agency](https://www.mlit.go.jp/kankocho/tax-free/page01_000001_00021.html) says purchases from that date moved to the refund method: "購入者は、免税店にて税込価格（課税）で免税対象物品を購入することとなりました" (buyers now pay the tax-included price at the tax-free store). You get the tax back after customs confirms you're taking the goods out of Japan. You have to show your passport at a tax-free kiosk at the airport or seaport within 90 days of buying, with all of the goods still with you, so do it before you check your bags. The agency says "返金の詳細は免税店等にお問い合わせください" (ask the tax-free store for details on the refund). That matches what Yodobashi Camera in Kyoto told us: pay full price, then get the tax back minus a fee.

**Japan has a Japanese-only PS5.** [PlayStation's Japan site](https://www.playstation.com/ja-jp/ps5/buy-now/ps5-digital-edition-Japanese-only/) says the PS5 Digital Edition Japanese-only model is ¥55,000 with tax, "利用できる本体言語は日本語のみです" (the only console language is Japanese), and "「国/地域」が「日本」のPlayStationのアカウントのみご利用できます" (only PlayStation accounts with Japan as the country or region work). That's why our caller kept saying "日本語専用モデルではなく" (not the Japanese-only model). The same page says a Midnight Black version of it went on sale on November 19, 2026, the day of these calls, which may be why one Bic Camera staffer double-checked "あの黒とかではなくて" (not the black one or anything).

## Shopping in Japan without speaking Japanese: tips from these calls

Before Black Friday and the Christmas rush, here's what worked on these calls:

- **Name the product, the size and what it is.** The brand name XREAL alone stumped one Bic Camera, and the staffer only got there once it worked out these were AR glasses. Yodobashi in Osaka asked for a model number, and Takashimaya and Hankyu's operators asked for the maker or brand before transferring.
- **Ask if it's in the store right now.** "店頭にありますか" (is it in the store?) got a staffer to go and look, and asking if it was new and unopened got a second, physical check at Yodobashi.
- **At department stores, ask the operator for the counter by name.** Takashimaya's menu says 9 for the operator, Hankyu's says 5, and Daimaru's says 2 for a shop or floor. Then say "the Ippodo counter" or "the Japanese tableware floor."
- **Plan for hold music.** The electronics chains often took 2 to 6 minutes to reach the right counter. One department store operator kept ringing a tea counter for most of a 6-minute call with no luck.
- **If you have a foreign phone number, stay on the line.** Yodobashi in Kyoto can't call foreign numbers back. For the hold, it typed the digits without the plus sign.
- **Ask for a hold, and ask until when.** Yodobashi in Kyoto held the glasses for four days with no card. Marukyu Koyamaen takes no matcha holds right now, and Bic Camera said a PS5 Pro restock would be "先着順" (first come, first served).
- **Ask about tax-free on the same call,** and bring your original passport and a card in your own name.
- **Expect purchase limits and sellouts on matcha and tea tools.** Marukyu Koyamaen limits matcha to two per person, and Kyoto's tea shops had sold out of bamboo whisks with no restock date.
- **Ask about online stock too.** One Kyoto shop sold its whisks one at a time through Rakuten, with 5x points for Black Friday week. Bic Camera's app and Yodobashi.com both show store stock.

## What to say when you call a store in Japan

Phrases our caller used that got real answers, verbatim from the calls:

- **To open:** "買い物の問い合わせなんですが" (I have a shopping question).
- **To ask about stock:** "食洗機対応の抹茶碗を探しているんですけど、今、店頭にありますか?" (I'm looking for a dishwasher-safe matcha bowl. Do you have one in the store right now?) Swap in your own item.
- **To check it's new:** "未開封の新品になりますでしょうか?" (Is it new and unopened?)
- **To ask for a hold:** "お取り置きを電話でお願いすることはできますでしょうか?" (Could I ask you to hold it over the phone?)
- **To ask about tax-free:** "店頭で免税手続きってできますでしょうか?" (Can I do the tax-free paperwork in the store?)
- **At a department store:** "和食器売り場をお願いしたいんですけど" (Could you put me through to the Japanese tableware department?)
- **For a PS5 Pro:** "日本語専用モデルではなく、プロです" (Not the Japanese-only model, the Pro.)
