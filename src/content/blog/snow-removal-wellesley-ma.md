## How we ranked Wellesley's snow plowing companies

One formula scored every company, the same one we used for our other snow and firewood rankings:

- **Rating, adjusted for review count.** We blend each rating with a 4.3, as if the company had 10 extra reviews at 4.3, so a few perfect reviews count for less than a long record. A 4.9 from 386 reviews stays about 4.88. A 5.0 from 2 reviews counts as about 4.42. A company with no rating anywhere starts at 4.0.
- **Plus 0.30** for publishing an actual dollar price. A pricing model like Pure Solutions' "by the inch," with no dollar figure, doesn't count.
- **Plus 0.15** for being based in Wellesley.
- **Plus 0.15** for saying it's taking new customers, online or on our call.
- **Minus 0.60** for saying it's full or not taking new customers. We read each company's own words to decide.
- **Plus 0.05** for showing activity dated 2026.
- **Minus 0.40** for a serious red flag: signs of a broker, lead-gen site or front, a pattern of no-show complaints or a recent one, signs of fake reviews, or a listed number that's disconnected.

The calls only filled in facts we couldn't find online. H&H's "We are not" counted as not taking new customers. Hayden's disconnected number counted as a red flag, and its own "full for 2026 snow" note counted as full. Whether a company picked up wasn't scored.

## Hiring a snow plow in Wellesley: what to ask, and the town's rules

1. **Ask if they're taking new driveways this winter, before anything else.** None of the top 10 says so online, Hayden's profile says it's full, and H&H said no.
2. **Ask how they charge.** We saw three models: per inch (Pure Solutions), per visit (JC Landscape's $45 to $85), and a season price (JC Landscape's from $450). The rest don't publish a price.
3. **Ask when they start plowing.** Pure Solutions starts past 2 inches, and JC Landscape's page says 2 to 3 inches. For comparison, the town's [Winter Operations page](https://www.wellesleyma.gov/1972/Winter-Operations) says Wellesley's DPW plows roads at 3 inches and sidewalks at 2 to 3 inches.
4. **Tell them where the snow can't go.** The same town page asks residents to "instruct the contractor not to push snow into the public way (into the street or onto the sidewalk)," or across the street from the end of your driveway, and says "the property owner could be fined up to $50 per offense." That's your fine, not the plow company's. It also asks you to "remind your contractor not to plow snow onto fire hydrants in your neighborhood."
5. **Keep the end of the driveway clear.** The town asks you not to park "in the public way portion of the driveway," the last eight feet nearest the street, and says "Town bylaws prohibit overnight street parking."
6. **Ask about walks and salt separately.** Pure Solutions shovels only for plowing customers. Highlands, NatureWorks and Lighthouse list walkways and salting on their snow pages, and Greenleaf lists walkways.
7. **Ask who will actually plow.** A town-named website with no company behind it, or an app, may send someone else.
