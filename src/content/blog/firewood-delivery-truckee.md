## Buy it where you burn it: why the wood's origin matters around Tahoe

The California Firewood Task Force's site, on the state agriculture department's domain at [firewood.cdfa.ca.gov](https://firewood.cdfa.ca.gov/), sums it up as "Buy It Where You Burn It": "Moving firewood can spread invasive species," and "Keeping firewood local keeps the pests and diseases in the wood local."

The specific risk for oak is sudden oak death, a disease caused by *Phytophthora ramorum*. California's quarantine for it, in the [state plant quarantine manual](https://pi.cdfa.ca.gov/pqm/manual/pdf/455.pdf), covers all of Alameda, Contra Costa, Del Norte, Humboldt, Lake, Marin, Mendocino, Monterey, Napa, San Francisco, San Mateo, Santa Clara, Santa Cruz, Solano, Sonoma and Trinity counties. Truckee's Nevada County and neighboring Placer County aren't on the list. Firewood with bark from host trees, including coast live oak, California black oak, canyon live oak and tanoak, may leave those counties only with a certificate from an agricultural official or under a permit, and a retail buyer is supposed to have proof of purchase from a seller who complies.

Summit's page says its wood comes from "Sonoma County, and Sierra Nevada regions." Kosich buys through "supplier contacts up and down the state." Neither page says which county a given load came from. If you're buying oak, ask where it was cut, and if it's from one of those counties, ask about the certificate. Local pine and fir, cut in the Tahoe region, avoids the question.

## Ordering firewood delivery in Truckee: practical tips

1. **Pick pine or oak before you call.** Pine and fir ran $265 to $450 a cord on these sellers' pages; oak and almond $550 to $640. Oak burns longer per load.
2. **Order before the storms.** Cross Check won't deliver during storm cycles, and from December to April its deliveries depend on weather and snow. Kosich won't stack with snow in the path.
3. **Ask for the delivered total with tax.** Kosich adds a delivery fee and tax; Summit adds tax but delivers free; Cross Check charges up to $50 to deliver.
4. **Ask what's in stock.** On November 27, Kosich's pine/fir and its hard/soft mix were sold out, and Cross Check had no oak at all this season.
5. **Check how the cord is measured.** A full cord is a stacked 4 by 4 by 8 feet, 128 cubic feet. Firewood Guy states that on its site. For face cords and "pallets," see [face cord vs full cord](/blog/face-cord-vs-full-cord).
6. **Clear a path if you want it stacked.** Kosich's stacking needs wheelbarrow access, with no stairs, steep hills or snow. Cross Check doesn't stack.
7. **Have cash or a check for Cross Check.** It takes no cards, Venmo or Apple Pay.
8. **Ask how long the wood has seasoned.** Tahoe Quarterly passed along the EPA's advice to split wood and keep it off the ground for at least six months before burning.
