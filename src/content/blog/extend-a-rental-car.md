**The short answer:** call the rental company's extension line before the car is due back, say it's an active rental, and ask for the new total with all taxes and fees before you say yes. In November 2026, call4me users had their AI phone assistant do exactly that. **Avis's Rental Extension team added a day and moved the return to a different airport for $132.08**, which "includes all charges, taxes, and fees," due when the car came back. **Budget quoted $309.53 to keep a car 7 more days and $494.24 for 12 more**, said "No, unfortunately we cannot match or lower the rate," and then made the longer extension, a $12 change fee included, about half an hour before the car was due.

If your Thanksgiving or Christmas trip runs long, these calls show what the rental companies ask for, where the phone menus trip you up, and what happens when someone else calls for you.

## How to extend an Avis rental: what the extension team said

Avis lists its **Rental Extension team at 1-800-230-4898** in its [Fees & Taxes FAQ](https://www.avis.com/en/customer-service/faqs/usa/ai-faqs), and its [reservations FAQ](https://www.avis.com/en/customer-service/faqs/usa/reservations) says that once you've picked up the car, "in the U.S. you must contact an Avis reservation agent at 1-800-230-4898." That's the number that worked.

Our caller asked in one breath: "I'm hoping to extend one day and also change the dropoff location for [name]'s current rental. It's due back in [city] tonight, but they need to return it tomorrow afternoon at [airport] instead." The voice system answered, "I didn't get that. Please explain your reason for calling in a few words," then offered "If you are calling about a reservation, please press one. If this is about customer service, press two," and put our caller through. A rental advisor picked up at 1:35.

What the advisor asked for, in order:

1. "Do you have the rental agreement number or the MVA number"
2. "what's the pickup location and the pickup date"
3. The new return airport and time
4. After the user said yes to the price: "what would be the credit card type used to pay and the last four numbers, please"
5. The caller's name ("You can put me down as [name]'s assistant" was accepted)

Then the advisor said, "I'm gonna go ahead and have this extended. And [name] will receive a confirmation, an email confirmation," and added that "both Avis dot com and the Avis app offers the ability to confirm, modify, extend, or cancel." The extension and the new return airport were done in one call, eight minutes long. We didn't hear a separate one-way fee, and our caller didn't ask for a breakdown of the $132.08.

### What Avis's own pages say about extensions

From Avis's [Fees & Taxes FAQ](https://www.avis.com/en/customer-service/faqs/usa/ai-faqs), as of November 25, 2026:

- **Price:** "A rental extension is calculated using the daily rate listed on your rental agreement."
- **Extension fee:** "There is a $12 rental extension fee to extend the rental for more than 7 hours beyond the original return time." There's no fee "for customers keeping the car for less than 7 additional hours." (The older reservations FAQ says "a service fee of up to $15 will apply" at corporate locations.)
- **Late without extending:** "There is a $15 late fee if you return the vehicle 7 or more hours later than agreed without extending the rental." It's billed daily along with the extra days, so call before you're late.
- **Limits:** "Your rental contract can be extended up to three times per agreement, with a maximum of 30 days total."
- **The app:** "If the extension is not confirmed in the app, your original return time still applies."
- **Outside the U.S.:** "we recommend contacting the rental location directly."

## Budget rental extension: what Budget said

### The quote call: customer service

Budget's voice system asked "Are you calling to book a new reservation" three times, then "Have you already picked up your vehicle," "Is this about a rental car or a moving truck" and "Are you the primary driver on this rental," the last one three times. After the third miss it said, "Sorry, I'm having trouble understanding. Please hold and one of the team will be with you shortly," and a rental advisor picked up about 2.5 minutes in.

That advisor couldn't find the rental. Our caller had read the agreement number with its letter prefix, and the advisor said, "Oh, don't need the U. It's just the digits." It still didn't come up, even by phone number, pickup place and date ("Alright, so it's not coming up, so it seems like I'm having some system issues here"), so the advisor transferred us "instead of letting you call back."

The second advisor answered with "Well, I'll be glad to extend one for you" and quoted two lengths:

- **7 more days: $309.53**, "which includes all charges, taxes and fees." About $44 a day.
- **12 more days: $494.24.** About $41 a day. (The advisor counted 11 added days. From the return dates on the call, it's 12.)

The rental was prepaid: "right now this is a prepaid reservation so it's already paid for," and the quotes were the added cost of the extension. Our caller asked, "is there any lower available extension rate, or can Budget match a lower current new booking rate for either date?" The answer: **"No, unfortunately we cannot match or lower the rate."** Our caller didn't extend on this call. The whole thing took almost 14 minutes, and the quote came almost 10 minutes in.

### The extension call: Budget's extension line

Thirty-five minutes later, about half an hour before the car was due back, our caller called **Budget's extension line, 800-824-6287**, the number in [Budget's rental terms](https://www.budget.com/en/help/rentalTnC) ("For rental extension requests: 1-800-824-6287"). The same voice system asked "Have you already picked up your vehicle" three times, gave up, and a rental advisor answered about 2 minutes in.

The advisor confirmed the current return, took the new one, and quoted **the same $494.24**, plus one detail the first call didn't mention: "There is also a twelve dollar change fee included in the total amount as the extension is for more than seven hours."

Then the advisor asked for the last four digits of the card. Our caller didn't have them and asked for a payment link instead. The answer: "in order for me to do an extension, you need to verify the last four digits of the credit card used and the card type." Otherwise, "you could always reach out to [the renter] to get the information, then give us a call back, or you can utilize our self-service option, that is our website or the app to make the extension." Our caller got the card details from the user about 2 minutes after the first ask, and the advisor said: **"The extension has been completed and a confirmation email was sent to the email address."**

## When the renter has to be on the line

On an active rental, both Avis and Budget let our caller extend for the user once it had the card type and last four digits. A reservation that hadn't started yet went differently.

For a car to be picked up in Spain, our caller called Avis's U.S. reservations line to move the pickup an hour later. Avis's voice system asked for the reservation number and then, "For verification purposes, please say the first four letters of the last name on the reservation or rental." A rental advisor picked up, asked "can you confirm your relationship with the rental" and "Is this not a company reservation," and then said: **"we would need to actually speak to the renter... as it is a personal reservation."** Our caller asked whether the change could be noted for later. The advisor said the renter would need to call, "Or you can actually check on the uh, website or the app."

If someone is calling for you, be reachable. call4me can ring you mid-call and patch you in when a business needs the account holder.

## Changing a rental car in Japan, in Japanese

Japanese quotes are verbatim from our machine transcripts, with our own English translation in parentheses. "…" marks words we skipped, and brackets mark dates and branch names we removed.

### ORIX Rent-A-Car: a missed pickup, moved to another branch at no charge

The driver didn't have their physical license at the pickup time, and the booking was made through a third-party site. Our caller's first try at 9:30 am got only a call-screening prompt. At 11:41 am, just after the pickup time, the ORIX branch at the Naha airport answered. Our caller asked first whether the booking could be kept rather than treated as a no-show.

The staffer took the third-party confirmation number, put our caller on hold for about 80 seconds, and then asked: "予約名のローマ字スペルを教えてもらっていいですか" (Could you spell the name on the booking in romaji?). After about two minutes of looking: "レンタカーズドットコムさんの方からご予約で、予約確認取れてまして" (It's a booking through Rentalcars.com, and we've found it). Then: "これ、この分また別、明日とかに来られるとかで、すかね?それともキャンセルですかね" (Will you come another day, say tomorrow? Or is this a cancellation?)

Our caller asked to pick up the next morning at a different ORIX branch near where the driver was staying. After a wait of about 2.5 minutes:

- "キャンセル等まだ行ってないので、で、明日から、あの、[店舗]の方でご出発が可能なので" (We haven't cancelled it or anything, so you can start from [the other branch] tomorrow)
- "…じゃあ明日、[日付]、あの、朝八時から営業はしてます" (So tomorrow, [date], it's open from 8 in the morning)
- "この料金変わらないでご案内っていう形にはなるんですが、大丈夫ですか" (We'd do it at the same price. Is that OK?)

Our caller: "あの追加料金なしであれば大丈夫です" (That's fine, as long as there's no extra charge). The staffer: "はい、大丈夫です" (Yes, that's fine). The call took 13 minutes, about 6 of them waiting while the staffer checked. Our caller didn't ask about the return or the third-party site's records, so those weren't confirmed.

### Duke's Rent-A-Car on Miyakojima: no earlier return

For a future rental, our caller asked: "[日付]の朝八時に返却できるかどうかを確認したくて" (I'd like to check whether we can return it at 8 in the morning on [date]). Before it had even finished reading the booking number, the answer came: "は、できないです" (No, we can't). The latest return the day before? "十八時ですね" and then "午後六時ですね" (18:00, so 6 pm). The call took 53 seconds.

### Mitsubishi Rent-a-Car in Chitose: documents for a foreign driver's license

For a future rental in Hokkaido, our caller asked whether a foreign driver's license, its official Japanese translation and a passport were enough, and "ほかに必要な書類などありますか?" (Is there anything else we need?). The answer: "[国名]の場合、そ、そちらで結構ですよ、大丈夫ですよ" (For a license from [that country], those are fine). "じゃあその3つで問題ないんですね" (So those three are enough?) "はい" (Yes). Our caller didn't check whether a car was free or book anything.

That answer was about one license from one place. Japan's rule depends on where your license is from. Per [JAF](https://english.jaf.or.jp/driving-in-japan/drive-in-japan/switch-to-japanese-license), holders of licenses from a short list of places "may drive a vehicle in Japan" by "carrying a Japanese translation of the license together with their driver's license." Everyone else needs an International Driving Permit issued under the 1949 Geneva Convention, and "IDPs issued in accordance with other treaties (such as the 1968 Vienna Convention) are not valid in Japan." Check JAF's list for your license before you call.

A user had a question about a broker's rate for a Budget car at the Philadelphia airport. - **Avis and Budget national lines:** the same voice system. It opens with "Are you calling to book a new reservation," then asks "Have you already picked up your vehicle" and "Are you the primary driver on this rental." Short answers worked best: "Change your reservation. Is that correct" came after our caller said "I need to change an existing reservation." When it couldn't understand, it either offered "press one" for a reservation or said "Please hold, and one of the team will be with you shortly." Both got a person.
- **Budget and Avis at the Philadelphia airport:** "For emergency roadside assistance, press 1. For lost and found, press 2. For rental extensions, press 3. For rates and reservations, press 4. ## Tips for extending a rental car before holiday travel

1. **Call the extension line, not general customer service.** Avis: 1-800-230-4898. Budget: 800-824-6287. Both are on the companies' own pages. Budget's customer service took almost 10 minutes and a transfer to get to a quote; its extension line was done in about 6 minutes.
2. **Call before the return time.** Budget's terms say "prior to the Designated Return Time." Both extensions here were done on the day the car was due, one of them about half an hour before.
3. **Ask for the all-in added cost.** Both companies quoted a number that "includes all charges, taxes, and fees" and is "in addition to" what was paid in advance. Ask the same way and you get a number you can compare.
4. **Quote more than one length.** Budget's 12-day extension worked out cheaper per day than its 7-day one.
5. **Don't count on a rate match.** Budget: "we cannot match or lower the rate."
6. **Have these ready:** the rental agreement number (Budget wanted "just the digits," without the letter prefix), where and when you picked up, and the card type and last four digits. Budget wouldn't extend without the card details.
7. **Change the return place on the same call.** Avis did the extra day and a different return airport together.
8. **If someone else calls for you, stay reachable.** Both active rentals were extended by an assistant. A personal Avis reservation that hadn't started yet needed the renter.
9. **Avoid the middle of the night.** Budget's extension line said "We are closed" at 5:12 am Eastern on a Sunday. Both companies pointed callers to their app and website.
10. **Take numbers from the company's own site.** One number our caller had for Avis was a "special promotion" recording and then disconnected.
11. **Get the confirmation in writing.** Both sent an email. Budget didn't give a confirmation number, and our caller didn't ask for one. Check the app before you drive past the old return time.
12. **In Japan, if you miss a pickup, call the branch right away.** ORIX hadn't cancelled the booking and moved it to another branch at the same price.

## Where our caller fell short

- **At Avis customer service, it hung up too early.** The voice system asked "Are you the primary driver on this rental" three times, and our side ended the call. At Budget, the same system put our caller through to a person after the third miss.
- **At Budget's first call, it asked several questions at once**, including "are those totals the additional cost to extend, or the new full rental total?" and the advisor had to ask "The last question again." One question at a time would have been faster.
- **It didn't have the card details in hand.** Avis and Budget both asked for the card type and last four, and both times our caller had to wait on the user. At Budget the advisor asked, "Hello, caller, are you there."
- **It didn't ask for a confirmation number** at Budget, or for a breakdown of Avis's $132.08.
- **At Philadelphia, it asked a long, detailed question** about a broker's terms in one go, and the contact center couldn't answer it.

## What to say: a script that worked

> Hi, I'm calling to extend an active rental. The rental agreement number is [digits]. It's due back [day and time] at [location]. Could you quote the new total to keep it until [new day and time], including all charges, taxes and fees, and tell me whether that's in addition to what I already paid?

If you also need a different drop-off: "Could I return it at [other location] instead? What's the total with that change?"
