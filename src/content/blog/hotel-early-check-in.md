**The short answer:** to get early check-in at a hotel, call the front desk on the day you arrive, ask for a small window, and ask them to note it on the reservation. When a call4me user's AI phone assistant called The Lloyd in Stamford, Connecticut, on the day of arrival and asked to check in "around two instead of the usual three," the front desk said "Yeah. 2 p.m. is fine" and added it to the reservation. The whole call took 48 seconds. The same user also got a free 1 pm checkout, an hour past noon, from Virgin Hotels New York City on a morning the hotel was "nearly sold out."

Bigger asks, and asks made days ahead, got a polite "not guaranteed." Here's what 9 hotels said on 15 calls in October and November 2026, made for 8 call4me users:

1. **Small asks on the day got a yes.** One hour early at The Lloyd. One hour late at Virgin Hotels New York City.
2. **If it's not a yes, the only guarantee is the standard time.** A club hotel in St. Louis: "The guarantee is four o'clock," even when our caller asked about paying a fee. Palazzo Artemide in Siracusa, Italy, guarantees the room "dalle tre in poi" (from three on), because its rooms check out at noon.
3. **Hotels can't promise ahead because they don't know yet.** MGM Grand Las Vegas: "you would have to call a night before because we don't know our availability for, uh, those, uh, dates yet." Virgin Hotels New York City said a late checkout gets decided on the day of departure.
4. **A note on the reservation is a request, not a promise.** Three hotels noted or forwarded a request. MGM Grand's guest services explained why calling beats a note: "if we just put a note, they're just gonna see it, and they're gonna be like, oh, well, we don't have any."
5. **The fallback is almost always yes.** Leave your bags, use the bar and the restaurant, and get a call or a WhatsApp when the room is ready.
6. **Already past checkout? Call anyway, and expect a short leash.** A resort in Austin allowed "eleven thirty at the very latest," with no late fee, "but that room is needed today."

These 15 calls were made for 8 call4me users, to hotels in New York, Washington, DC, Stamford, St. Louis, Nashville, Las Vegas, Austin and Siracusa. Our caller didn't pay for anything, give a card or agree to a fee on any of them. Guests' names are muted in the audio and shown in brackets in the transcripts, and names, room numbers, confirmation numbers and stay dates are left out of the post: the table says when each call was made relative to the stay, not the date. Hotel staff aren't named. Check-in times and answers are what each front desk said that day, and they change with how full the hotel is.

## How to get early check-in at a hotel: what 5 hotels said

### The Lloyd, Stamford: "2 p.m. is fine"

Our caller rang the front desk at 12:45 pm on the day of arrival with one small ask: could the guest "check in a bit earlier today, like maybe around two instead of the usual three?" The desk asked for the last name, looked up the reservation for about 8 seconds, and said yes: "Yeah. 2 p.m. is fine. I can, I can make the adjustment." Asked to put it on the booking ("Could you note the two p.m. arrival on his reservation?"), the desk said, "Yeah, it's added." No fee came up.

On its own site: Hilton's page for The Lloyd Stamford lists "Check-in 3 pm" and "Check-out 11 am" ([hilton.com](https://www.hilton.com/en/hotels/hpnstup-the-lloyd-stamford/hotel-info/)).

### A club hotel in St. Louis: "The guarantee is four o'clock"

The night before arrival, our caller said the guest would arrive "around 1 pm after an overnight flight," and asked "if there's any way to guarantee or at least prioritize getting a room ready for him by then." The desk: "Okay, so guaranteed check in is at 4 o'clock. Um, if we have anything available at the time, we can accommodate him."

Our caller pushed once: "Is there any way to guarantee it by 1? Even if there's an early check-in fee?" The answer: "There's no guarantee. Uh- The guarantee uh- is four o'clock."

The rest was yes, in a softer form:

- **The note.** "He does have a note already on his reservation for early check in. Again, if rooms are available, we can accommodate him before then."
- **The bags.** "Once he check in, we do have a coat check area."
- **A heads-up when the room is ready.** "Once he arrive, yes, he'll be able to, we'll be able to assist him," and "he'll just leave his number and everything, for front desk."

### Palazzo Artemide, Siracusa: the room from 3, and a WhatsApp if it's ready sooner

At 9:04 am on the day of arrival, our caller asked, in Italian, whether the guests could check in early, around 1 pm. The front desk said it couldn't promise that: "noi non lo possiamo garantire perché abbiamo la partenza, il check-out delle camere per mezzogiorno" (we can't guarantee it, because the rooms check out at noon).

What it offered instead:

- **Everything but the room.** They can leave their bags, and "se hanno la macchina noi gli parcheggiamo la macchina col valet, tutti i servizi dell'hotel sono a loro disposizione, ma noi possiamo garantire la camera dalle tre in poi" (if they have a car, we'll valet it, all the hotel's services are theirs to use, but we can guarantee the room from three on). Later: "bar, ristorante, vogliono sedersi qua al area comuni, bagni, tutto a loro disposizione" (the bar, the restaurant, the lounge areas if they want to sit, the restrooms, all of it is theirs).
- **A message when it's ready.** If the guests in the room leave early, "noi di sotto ci facciamo lasciare il contatto degli ospiti e poi gli mandiamo un WhatsApp se la camera è pronta prima o li chiamiamo" (downstairs we take the guests' contact and send them a WhatsApp if the room is ready sooner, or we call them).
- **No time estimate, and why.** "Non lo so perché non so quando partiranno gli ospiti che al momento stanno occupando la camera" (I don't know, because I don't know when the guests in the room now will leave). "Magari mi partono a mezzogiorno proprio o a volte ritardano perché capita" (maybe they leave right at noon, or sometimes they run late, because it happens).

Our caller asked for the request on the reservation, and the desk wrote it while still on the line: "Sto scrivendo proprio l'appunto in questo momento" (I'm writing the note right now).

### The Hermitage Hotel, Nashville: a note at 6 am, voicemail at 9

Several days before the stay, our caller called at 6:17 am Nashville time to request a noon check-in. The front desk said that was a job for reservations: "reservations they'll be in at 9 a.m. local time and they'll be able to upgrade or you know, adjust your- your request." It took a note anyway, with a name and a contact number, and said: "we'll make sure to have that um uh forwarded to the reservations department."

At 9:10 am, our caller called back and asked for reservations. The transfer went to voicemail: "Unfortunately, we are unable to answer your call at this time." The saved transcript ends there, so these calls never show reservations confirming noon.

### The Jane Hotel, New York: leave your bags before 3 pm

On an information call, the desk said it was "completely sold out" on the nights asked, and answered the rest in a few words each: check-in is "3 PM," a guest who arrives early can leave a bag before check-in ("That is correct"), and there's no resort or facility fee ("No").

## How to get a late checkout: what 3 hotels said

### Virgin Hotels New York City: ask on the day, before noon

The day before checkout, the front desk confirmed the current time ("So as of right now, it's for noon") and said a late checkout couldn't be promised ahead: "he would need to inquire on the day of departure because it's subject to availability depending on the occupancy." How to ask: "he would just either call or come down to the front desk to inquire. The earlier, the better. He would need to inquire before twelve p.m."

So our caller called at 9 am on checkout day and asked for 4 pm. The answer: "Okay, so for today due to our occupancy, um we're not able to extend it up until four, um but I could give one p.m. complimentary." Plus: "after one p.m. we could store the luggages."

Paying for more time wasn't an option that day. Asked whether there'd be a fee to keep the room until 4: "So for today, just due to our occupancy, we wouldn't do the fee. Just because we are nearly sold out today." Asked to note the 1 pm checkout: "It's already on there."

On its own site: the hotel's FAQ says, "Our standard check-in time is 3pm and check-out is 12pm (hello, leisurely lie-in!). However, we can offer flexible check-in and check-out times. Just call our Junction team and we can help cater to your trip." ([virginhotels.com](https://virginhotels.com/new-york/the-hotel/faqs/)) For the hotel's phone menu and valet rates, see our [Virgin Hotels New York City parking calls](/blog/virgin-hotels-new-york-parking).

### MGM Grand Las Vegas: call the night before

More than a week before checkout, at 8:45 pm Las Vegas time, our caller first dialed another MGM Grand number and got a recording: "We are temporarily changed our hours of operation to seven AM to eight PM, Pacific Time, seven days a week." A minute later it called the hotel's main line, where the menu ends with "To reach a hotel guest, or speak with the operator, please press zero." Guest Services answered after about half a minute of hold.

Asked for a free 4 pm late checkout, Guest Services checked and said it was too early to know: "Okay, unfortunately, you would have to call a night before because we don't know our availability for, uh, those, uh, dates yet."

Our caller asked to note it on the reservation in the meantime. The agent said a note wouldn't do much: "Calling is better, 'cause they're just gonna let you know. Because if we just put a note, they're just gonna see it, and they're gonna be like, oh, well, we don't have any, you know what I mean?" The other option: "Or even when you check in, you could ask. I recommend you ask, see what they could do for you." And a manager couldn't promise it ahead either: "Uh, no, our managers wouldn't be able to."

### A resort in Austin: already past checkout

At 11:11 am on checkout day, our caller called to apologize that the guest was "past the ten a.m. checkout" and to say he'd be out by noon. The desk wasn't having noon: "Um, why is that exactly? Because we can only allow like eleven thirty at the very latest," "because housekeeping will go up there. By like eleven forty five-ish."

No fee, but no slack either: "We don't have any type of late checkout fee, but that room is needed today, so we're kind of very strict on our checkouts."

## Where our caller fell short

- **At the resort in Austin, it pushed past the answer.** The desk said 11:30 at the latest, twice. Our caller then asked, "you have noted though that he's running late and he'll be out by 12 today?" and got "Yes, ma'am." That's a note that the guest is late, not a noon checkout. It should have passed on 11:30 as the deadline.
- **At the Hermitage, it stopped at voicemail.** The front desk said reservations would be in at 9. At 9:10 the reservations line didn't answer, and nothing in these calls shows anyone confirming noon. Calling back later, or asking the front desk how reservations would confirm, would have closed the loop.
- **In St. Louis, it took a soft answer as a yes.** It asked the desk to text the guest when the room was ready, and the desk said the guest could leave a number on arrival. It also asked for a quiet room on a high floor and got a description of which floors have rooms, not a yes.
- **At a Westin in Washington, DC, it never reached a person.** At 3:27 am on the day of arrival, the hotel's voice system said, "In a few words, tell me what you're calling about." Our caller said it was "calling about an early check-in request for a reservation." The system asked, "Is your upcoming stay starting within the next forty-eight hours," heard "it's for today, actually," and said, "Please hold while I connect you with operator." The saved transcript ends about three minutes later, still on hold.
- **At Virgin Hotels New York City, the first try ended in the phone menu.** The next call, two minutes later, got the front desk.

## How to get early check-in or late checkout before holiday travel

Built only from what these calls showed:

1. **Ask on the day, and ask for a little.** The Lloyd said yes to one hour early on arrival day. Virgin Hotels New York City said yes to one hour late on checkout morning, and no to four.
2. **Don't ask for a guarantee.** The hotels that couldn't say yes promised only their standard time: 3 pm in Siracusa, 4 pm in St. Louis. Offering to pay didn't change that in St. Louis, and Virgin Hotels New York City wouldn't sell extra hours on a nearly full morning.
3. **Call the night before, then again on the day.** MGM Grand doesn't know its availability until then. Virgin Hotels New York City said to ask on checkout day, "The earlier, the better," and before noon.
4. **Get a note ahead of time, but don't count on it.** The Hermitage and the St. Louis club hotel noted early check-in requests. MGM Grand's guest services said a note alone tends to get "oh, well, we don't have any."
5. **Ask for the fallback in the same call.** Bags held (St. Louis, Siracusa, The Jane, and after checkout at Virgin Hotels New York City), the bar, restaurant and lounge areas (Siracusa), and a call or WhatsApp when the room is ready (Siracusa).
6. **Give the name on the reservation first, and spell it.** The Lloyd asked for the last name before anything else. Palazzo Artemide had our caller spell the surname, letter by letter, before it could find the booking.
7. **Running late on checkout? Call before the checkout time, not after.** The Austin resort, called after its checkout had passed, allowed 30 more minutes and no more.
8. **Expect less on a full night.** The two firmest no's came with the same reason: "we are nearly sold out today" at Virgin Hotels New York City, and "that room is needed today" in Austin. Over Thanksgiving, Christmas and New Year, plan around the standard time and treat anything earlier as a bonus.

## What to say: a script that worked

This is close to what our caller said at The Lloyd:

> Hi, I'm calling for [name], who's checking in today. Could they check in a bit earlier, around [an hour before check-in] instead of the usual [check-in time]? Could you note that arrival time on the reservation?

If they can't promise it: "Could you note the request, hold the bags if the room isn't ready, and call or text [number] when it is?"

For a late checkout, on the morning you leave: "Could [name] have a late checkout today, ideally [time]? If not, what's the latest you can do at no charge, and can you store the bags after?"
