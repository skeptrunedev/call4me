**The short answer:** to lower a Verizon bill, the account owner calls **800-922-0204** with the **phone number on the account and the four-digit Account PIN** ready, and asks about **a cheaper plan, the Auto Pay discount, and add-ons they don't use**. Verizon's AI assistant answers first. It told us on October 24, 2026:

- **Three ways to save:** "Existing customers can lower their monthly bill by enrolling in Auto Pay, changing to a better fitting plan, or removing unused add-ons."
- **By phone, with the owner.** "Those changes can be handled by phone. The account owner or an authorized account manager must be on the line to make account changes."
- **What to have ready:** "their phone number or account number, along with their four-digit account PIN to verify their identity."
- **Who to ask for:** "They can ask for customer service or the retention and loyalty department."

**What a plan switch did on a real account.** On October 1, 2026, we called about my family's account, 3 lines on 5G Do More. A live agent said switching all 3 lines to **Unlimited Welcome** would take the bill from **$219.08** to **$170.58** a month, "surcharges, government taxes, and everything" included. That's **about $48 a month less**. The agent's view of what we'd give up: "To tell you honestly, there is no much difference between the two." The full recording is in our [Verizon customer service guide](/blog/verizon-customer-service). Those were that account's numbers on that day.

**The Auto Pay discount, from Verizon's own [Auto Pay FAQ](https://www.verizon.com/support/auto-pay-faqs/):** "Get up to $10 off per line with Auto Pay and paper-free billing." It's up to $10 a month per line on plans like myPlan Unlimited Ultimate, and "up to $5/month" on some others. "You must use a bank account or Verizon Visa Card as your Auto Pay payment method."

**Changing plans costs nothing.** Verizon's [plan change FAQ](https://www.verizon.com/support/understand-and-change-your-plan-faqs/) says "There's no cost to change your plan or your allowances," and "Changing plans doesn't affect your upgrade eligibility or contract end date."

We made the no-account call on Saturday, October 24, 2026, at about 6:25 pm Eastern, with [call4me](/) asking general questions. **We did not reach a person on it.** Everything quoted from that call is Verizon's AI assistant.

## The Verizon number to call

Verizon's [pound and star codes page](https://www.verizon.com/support/pound-star-codes/) lists **800-922-0204** to "speak to an agent," open "8AM-7PM (Mon-Sat)" and "8AM-5PM (Sun)." From a Verizon phone you can dial ***611**. The AI assistant gave different hours: "Live customer service is generally available from 8 a.m. to 9 p.m. local time." Go by the website's hours.

## How to reach a person (and what didn't work)

On our account call, the route was: give the phone number on the account, verify with a text code or the **Account PIN**, then say **"An agent, please."** A person answered 6 minutes 34 seconds into that call. "Could I just speak with a representative, please?" didn't work; "An agent, please" did.

**Without an account, Verizon won't put you through.** On October 24 we called twice with no account:

- **The first call** asked its questions to the AI assistant, which answered them (above), and our caller ended it there without asking for a person.
- **On the second call, our caller said only "An agent, please,"** four times, with no reason. The assistant said "There's quite a wait for a representative right now. Are you calling to make a payment, ask about a bill, or troubleshoot a device?" Then: "In order to get you to the right person, I do need a quick idea of what you are calling about today. If you're not able to share that, I won't be able to continue the call." It hung up. That was our caller's mistake. **Give a reason first**, such as "a question about my bill," and ask for an agent after you've verified.

On October 1, a no-account call also stopped at the account prompts: "they'll still need your phone or account number to look up your details."

## What to say: a script

> Hi, I'm the account owner. I have a question about my bill: it's [amount] a month for [number] lines on [plan]. I mostly use [calls, texts, a little data / a lot of data]. Is there a plan that would cost less, am I getting the Auto Pay discount, and are there add-ons I'm paying for that I don't use? If there's nothing, I'd like to speak with the loyalty department.

On our account call, the agent asked how we use data before pricing a plan, and whether anyone in the family wasn't on the account yet. Have that answer ready.
