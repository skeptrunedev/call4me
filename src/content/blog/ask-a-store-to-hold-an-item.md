**The short answer:** yes. Most stores will hold an item until closing time if you call and give a name. Here's what 13 stores said when 11 call4me users had their AI phone assistants ask, between October 18 and November 25, 2026. **Seven said yes.** A Macy's in Washington, DC, held a $60 beauty set "until the end of the day." A Suitsupply in San Jose held two sweaters until its 8 pm close. A hobby shop in Pearland, Texas, still had a $199.99 jet held "back here" the next afternoon. Not one of them asked for a card or a deposit. The most any of them asked for was a name.

**Three said no**, and all three were about scarce stock: a Walmart Auto Care Center in Rochester, NY, had one $79 tire left, "and it's a Black Friday item too." A Shoppers Drug Mart in Waterloo, Ontario, had one Pokémon box, "but we don't put it on hold." An EB Games said holds for a launch-day game without a preorder are "all over now."

What we learned, in short:

1. **Ask for a hold until closing and you'll usually get it.** Same-day holds were the norm. One lasted overnight. One store would only pull an item for about an hour.
2. **They only need a name.** Macy's asked for one "just in case," so a coworker would know who it's for. Central Computer: "we just need a name."
3. **Hot items don't get held.** The last unit, a Black Friday price, a Pokémon release, a midnight game launch: those were the no's. A store with plenty on the shelf still said yes.
4. **If they won't hold it, they'll tell you to order it for pickup.** That's exactly what Walmart said.

These 14 calls were made by 11 call4me users, to stores in Washington, DC, Chicago, New York City, Pearland, San Jose, Pine Bluff, Owen Sound, Waterloo, San Francisco, Palm Beach, Santa Clara and Rochester. Our caller didn't buy anything or give a card number on any of them. The users' names are muted in the audio and shown as [name removed] in the transcripts, and store staff aren't named. Prices, stock and hold rules are what each store said on that day, and they can change any day, especially this week.

## How long will a store hold an item?

Usually until closing time that day. Here's how long each yes lasted:

- **Until close, same day.** Macy's: "We close at 9. 9 p.m. So yeah, until the end of the day." Suitsupply: "we close at 8 p.m. so I can hold it until end of day." In our earlier [dress shoe calls](/blog/need-dress-shoes-today), Men's Wearhouse in DC said the same thing: "usually folks just pick stuff up at the end of the day and put it back."
- **Overnight.** Performance RC Hobby put a jet behind the counter on Saturday afternoon. When the same user's assistant called back Sunday, the associate checked the name and said, "Yes, we have it back here for you."
- **About an hour.** Thursday Boot's associate said, "We don't offer holds necessarily like day holds cause it's first come first serve. Um, but if you come like in an hour, I can pull it for your visit."
- **24 hours a day.** The Baskin-Robbins was open 24 hours, so "later today" had no deadline.

Check the closing time, twice. Macy's recorded greeting said "Today's hours are 11 AM to 8 PM," and the associate said 9. Central Computer's associate first said "We close at 6," then corrected it: "We close at 5." A same-day hold ends when the store closes, so plan for the earlier time.

## What stores asked for to hold an item

A name, and that's it. No card, no deposit, no phone number.

- **Macy's** asked for the name "just in case," and explained why: "if I'm not here, I'll tell one of my co-workers who it's for." Then the associate had it spelled out.
- **Performance RC Hobby** asked for the name after our caller had already said goodbye: "Uh, what name do you want me to put this with."
- **Central Computer** checked before saying yes, then: "Yeah, we could, um, we just need a name," and "just a name and then, yeah, that's it."
- **R&E Supply** took the name. In an earlier call that day, it had warned: "we don't take cash after four," so "it'd have to be card." Ask how you'll pay at pickup.

The one store that wanted money was the Jaeger-LeCoultre boutique, for a new watch it didn't have in stock: "We are more than happy to uh, put in a deposit for it with thirty percent down to be able to get one queued up." Asked if a plain hold was possible: "it would just be the deposit," because "that is a very high demand um, watch."

## Does Walmart hold items? The Auto Care Center said no

The Walmart call started with an AI: "I'm the Walmart AI agent. I'm not perfect." It pointed our caller to the website or app first. When our caller asked for someone in the store, it said, "I can't check store inventory directly for you, but if you'd like, I can see if I can connect you to a live associate at the store who can check on that tire for you." Asking for a person worked.

The associate found the tire, $79, and one left. Asked to set it aside, the answer was no: "order it online for a same-day pickup or just come in and buy it, because it literally only says I got one left and it's a Black Friday item too."

That's one Walmart department, on the day before Thanksgiving, not a chainwide rule. The associate's advice was a pickup order, and Walmart's own help page for scheduled pickup orders says: "If you don't pick up the order by the end of the day, the store cancels your order the next day." ([walmart.com](https://www.walmart.com/help/article/pickup-and-delivery-changes-and-exceptions/97461ebd27b04ab78cfa1ca3de480a83))

## Does Macy's hold items? A Macy's in Washington, DC held one until close

Yes, at a Macy's in Washington, DC, on a Sunday in October. Our caller asked about JLo Beauty, and the beauty counter had only a set: "The set contains a refine and gloss eye mask, a glow then, and a glow serum. It's set for sixty dollars." Asked "are you able to put one aside if I come in today?", the associate said "Yes," held it until the end of the day, and asked for a name.

The catch is the phone menu. From the greeting to a person took about two and a half minutes, through the store, the department list, cosmetics and fragrances, and a list of beauty brands.

If you can't get there the same day, an online order may be the better hold. Macy's own pickup page answers "How long will the store hold my order?" with "Orders are held for 7 days." ([macys.com](https://www.macys.com/customer-service/articles/store-pick-up-information))

## Stores that wouldn't hold an item, and why

The three no's were all about scarce stock, and two more stores set limits rather than saying no:

- **The last one.** Walmart had "one left." Shoppers Drug Mart had one Pokémon 30th Knockout Collection: "we do have one in stock, but we don't put it on hold." (More on hunting those in our [Pokémon 30th Anniversary stock calls](/blog/pokemon-30th-anniversary-cards-in-stock).)
- **A launch.** EB Games in Owen Sound had "about twenty copies left" of the new Zelda for Switch 2 for a midnight launch, and "I can't guarantee you one, unless you're the first few people in line." Holds without a preorder: "I definitely cannot. That's all over now. I'm sorry."
- **A first come, first served policy.** Thursday Boot doesn't do day holds, but would pull a pair for a visit within the hour.
- **High demand.** Jaeger-LeCoultre took deposits, not holds.

Compare that with Performance RC Hobby, which had "about three or four of them on s-on the shelf, so you should be good," and still offered to put one behind the counter. When there's plenty, stores are happy to hold one. When there's one left, they want it to go to whoever walks in.

## If the store won't hold it, order it for pickup

That was Walmart's suggestion. Here's how long a pickup order waits:

- **Walmart:** scheduled pickup orders are canceled the next day if you don't collect them by the end of the pickup day, per the help page above.
- **Macy's:** pickup orders are held for 7 days, per its pickup page.
- **Grainger:** one user had a pickup order waiting at a Grainger branch in Little Rock, to be paid at pickup. Asked how many days the branch would hold it, Grainger's customer service said: "Five business days."

## Where our caller fell short

- **It hung up before giving the name.** At Performance RC Hobby, the associate had to ask, "Uh, what name do you want me to put this with," after our caller said bye. Give the name in the same sentence as the hold.
- **It asked to hold the wrong boot.** Thursday Boot said it didn't carry the Duke Chelsea in Chocolate Suede, but did have the Legend Chelsea in both sizes. Our caller then asked them to hold the Duke.
- **It asked for an evening hold before asking when the store closes.** Central Computer said yes, then said it closes at 5.
- **It didn't place the cake hold.** Baskin-Robbins said it could hold a cake, and our caller hung up without asking it to. A "yes, we can" is not a hold.
- **It lost one answer.** At a Legends Comics & Games store on November 23, the store said it had Magic: The Gathering Star Trek Collector Boosters at $45 a pack, and our saved transcript ends right after the hold request. We don't know what they said.

One thing that wasn't our caller's fault: at Buck Mason, the associate went to look and came back more than five minutes later. "I know it says that we have two in stock, but... I went through it, I couldn't find any." The system count isn't the shelf. Ask someone to put eyes on the item before you drive over.

## How to ask a store to hold an item before Black Friday

Built only from what these calls showed:

1. **Name the exact item and size.** Suitsupply answered three sweaters by product code in about 50 seconds. Central Computer found five units from an item number.
2. **Ask them to look at the shelf, not just the system.** Buck Mason's system said two. The shelf said zero.
3. **Confirm the price on the phone.** Our caller had $499.99 for the item at Central Computer. The store's system said $599: "if it's on the website, it's not updated."
4. **Ask for the hold and the deadline in one breath.** At Suitsupply, our caller asked them to hold the sweaters "and let me know until what time you'd be able to hold them," and got "until end of day" in one answer.
5. **Ask when they close, and plan for the earlier time if you hear two.** Macy's greeting and associate were an hour apart.
6. **Don't count on a hold for the last unit, a Black Friday price, a launch-day game or trading cards.** Ask instead whether to order online for pickup, or when to line up. EB Games said you could line up whenever, with the doors closing at 6 and reopening at midnight.
7. **Ask how to pay at pickup.** R&E Supply took only cards after 4.
8. **Call back to check.** A short call the next day confirmed Performance RC still had it.
9. **Get a person.** Walmart's AI agent couldn't check the store, but transferred our caller to someone who could.

## What to say: a script that worked

This is close to what our caller said at Suitsupply:

> Hi, do you have [item or product code] in [size] in stock today? Could you hold it under the name [name] so they can pick it up later today, and let me know until what time you'd be able to hold it?

If they say no: "Should I order it online for pickup instead, or just come in? What time do you close?"
