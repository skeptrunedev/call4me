**The short answer:** if customs never stamped your Swedish tax free forms, email Global Blue the traveler's name, the form number and a scan of the form so the refund manager can review them; that is what a person at Global Blue told us on the call below. Global Blue's own Sweden help article lists **+46 8 797 6242** and **sweden@globalblue.com** under "Need Assistance in Sweden?" ([Global Blue, How to validate your Tax Free Form in Sweden](https://cs.globalblue.com/s/article/How-to-validate-your-Tax-Free-Form-in-Sweden?language=en_US)). Its [customer service hours page](https://cs.globalblue.com/s/article/Customer-Service-Operating-Hour1?language=en_US) gives 9:00 to 18:00 Central European Time, Monday to Friday, excluding public holidays.

The call on this page went to a different Stockholm number, **+46 8 545 284 40**. We could not find that number on globalblue.com, so treat **+46 8 797 6242** as the official one. A person did answer it as Global Blue, 24 seconds in, with no phone menu.

What the person told a call4me customer's assistant about tax free forms that were never stamped by customs:

- **Email the details** to the address she spelled out as "info dot se at globalblue dot com". That address is not the one on the official page (sweden@globalblue.com).
- **Include** "the traveler's name, the form number, and everything", and a scan of the tax free form if possible.
- **The refund manager decides.** Asked whether after the fact proof such as a consulate stamp or a Thai import receipt would be accepted, she said "I cannot take any of those decisions at the moment."

The call took place on Friday, October 16, 2026, at 2:40 pm Stockholm time. It lasted 2 minutes 10 seconds.

## What the call did not settle

Global Blue did not say the refund would be paid, did not accept or reject any proof of export, and did not give a deadline or a case number. The assistant did not ask about a deadline or a postal address before the call ended, so those parts of its brief stayed open.

Global Blue's own [Sweden tax free page](https://www.globalblue.com/tax-free-shopping/sweden/stockholm-downtown) is blunt on the general rule: "No Custom validation = No refund." It gives 3 months plus the month of purchase to validate a form, says a stamped form is valid for 1 year from the purchase date, and lists a postal address in Bratislava, Slovakia for validated forms. None of that was said on the call, and the page does not describe an exception for forms that missed validation. Whether the refund manager makes one is exactly what this call left unanswered.

## How to write the Global Blue refund email

The representative's request was short: a detailed explanation, the traveler's name, the tax free form number, and a scan of the form. For forms that missed customs validation, the useful facts are the ones this call put on the table:

- **The tax free form number** and a scan of each form.
- **How and when you left the EU,** for example the train or flight, the station or airport, and the date.
- **Why the form was not validated,** for example a closed customs counter or a kiosk that would not scan the form.
- **Any proof of export you have,** such as a ticket, an import receipt in your home country, or a consulate stamp. The representative would not say which of these counts.
- **The original receipts,** and whether you still have the goods unused.

Send it to the official **sweden@globalblue.com** unless Global Blue tells you otherwise in writing. If you also write to the address spelled on the call, say so in both messages so the two do not turn into separate cases. Ask for a case reference in reply, and ask directly whether a deadline applies.
