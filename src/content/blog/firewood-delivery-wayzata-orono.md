Minnesota law defines the cord, not the face cord. Under [Minnesota Statutes 239.33](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/239.33), a cord is 128 cubic feet of wood "if cut in four-foot lengths," and for "sawed and split wood," the kind you burn, "a cord shall mean 120 cubic feet, when ranked," meaning stacked. The Minnesota Department of Commerce's [firewood page](https://mn.gov/commerce/business/retailers/firewood/) says words like "truckload, face cord, rick, fireplace cord, or pile, have no legal definition," and that its Weights and Measures Division "cannot help you in a dispute if the wood is not sold by the cubic foot, the cubic meter, or the cord." By that math, a 4 x 8 ft row of 16-inch split wood (42.7 cubic feet) is a little more than a third of a Minnesota cord.

So compare by cubic feet. At the listed prices, OneLogFire's face cord works out to about $7 a cubic foot before delivery, Espeseth's oak third to $7.95 delivered if it's a standard 4 x 8 ft row of 16-inch wood (the site doesn't say), and the Spikes & Houles pallet to about $11.43. ## Minnesota firewood rules before you order

- **Your delivery should come with a ticket.** Under [Minnesota Statutes 239.092](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/239.092), when the buyer and seller aren't both there to see the wood measured, a bulk delivery needs a delivery ticket that shows, among other things, the amount delivered. Firewood sold in Minnesota must also come with ticket information naming the county or counties and the state where it was harvested.
- **Moving wood is restricted.** The [Minnesota DNR's firewood page](https://www.dnr.state.mn.us/firewood/index.html) says, "It's illegal to move uncertified hardwood firewood out of quarantined counties unless they're next to each other," and that hardwood firewood "may not be brought into Minnesota without written approval" from the Department of Agriculture. The Department of Agriculture keeps the list of [quarantined counties](https://www.mda.state.mn.us/minnesota-plant-pest-quarantines). The DNR's advice: "Moving firewood can move invasive species, so we don't recommend it."
- **Taking wood to a state park or other DNR land:** "Firewood you bring from home" isn't allowed. Firewood bought at the park, firewood "certified by the Minnesota Department of Agriculture" (good "on any DNR lands in the state"), and uncertified bundles "as long as it is not ash, and it was harvested in the same county where you will burn it" are. Keep "your receipt and the bundle label."
- **The DNR doesn't approve or certify firewood sellers.** Its page says, "Minnesota DNR does not certify firewood," and "The Minnesota DNR no longer has a firewood vendor approval program." Two sellers here still use the DNR's name: OneLogFire's face cord listing says "MN DNR approved and certified," and Holtz Firewood's delivery page says it "is a DNR Approved Firewood Vendor" (Holtz also says it's an MDA certified producer). Certification comes from the Minnesota Department of Agriculture (MDA). Jay's Tree Care and ProCut also say they're MDA certified. We couldn't load the MDA's certified producer list on November 27 (it blocked our automated check), so we didn't confirm any seller against it. If certification matters to you, ask the seller for the MDA certified label.

## Tips for firewood delivery near Wayzata and Orono

Everything here comes from the sellers' own pages and our calls.

1. **Ask for the size in feet and inches, not the name.** A "face cord" is 42.7 cubic feet at OneLogFire and 35 at Spikes & Houles. Espeseth, Don's, Doc's and Trinity don't publish their sizes.
2. **Ask for the delivered total.** Espeseth adds $40 to every order. OneLogFire charges a delivery fee it doesn't list. Ron's and Holtz say delivery is free. Jay's says "A $25 delivery fee may applies" outside its standard range (its typo). Trinity says "Delivery charges are based on location and needs."
3. **Ask where they'll put it.** Ron's stacks for free. Jay's stacks "to a convenient location only": you "must be able to get a wheelbarrow quickly and directly to the location." Doc's stacks face cords free and charges for full cords. Spikes & Houles drops it in the driveway. Select Premium charges for stacking.
4. **Ask seasoned or kiln-dried, and how dry.** Ron's says "below 20%" moisture after about two years. Jay's seasoned wood is "stored under cover for two years." ProCut kiln-dries every load "at 265 degrees Fahrenheit for 36 hours."
5. **Have your street ready.** Some sellers price delivery by location, and the seller who called us back asked "where are you located in Wayzata" before quoting anything.
6. **Keep the delivery ticket**, and check that it shows the amount and where the wood was harvested.
7. **Order before the cold.** Jay's says "Ordering early is recommended to ensure availability," and Espeseth's birch was already "Limited Supply" on November 27.
